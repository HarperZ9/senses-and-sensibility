# Part III — The Security Architecture

### The Bridge Made Concrete: An Operator-Protective Authentication Channel

**Abstract.** Parts I–II argued that the *for* — purpose, authorization, will — is authored and cannot be read off the thing conferred, and that this same gap reappears in security as the distinction between *authentication* (is this property real?) and *authorization* (is this real property the operator's uncoerced will toward this referent, now?). This Part makes the gap concrete by instantiating it in the hardest available case: a channel in which the thing to be authenticated is a human will, the protected party is the operator herself, and the adversary attacks the operator's own command path. A brain–computer-interface (BCI) command channel is chosen not because it is near-term but because it removes every comfortable abstraction — there is no password to fall back on, no second device to confirm with, nothing between intention and action but the channel. We design the channel from *intrinsic* primitives, each replacing an extrinsic check with a property whose existence entails what the check verified; we then run an adversarial teardown to its end; and we report the honest specification the teardown forces — which is that the channel's strong, by-construction part is authentication, and its authorization-of-meaning claim is bounded mitigation, paid continuously in availability.

---

## §1. From the *for* to a channel

The bridge from Part II is a single sentence: *authorization is not authentication, because will-toward-a-referent is intentionality (Brentano, 1874), and intentionality is not a property of a signal.* A channel can verify, to any strength we are willing to pay for, that a command is a genuine instance of some property P — that it is fresh, that it carries a live signature, that it descends from an enrolled binding. It cannot verify, from any property of the signal, that the command is what the operator *meant*. The first is a question about the world; the second is a question about a mind's directedness, and the directedness is not in the wire.

The BCI command channel is the sharpest test of this claim because it strips away the usual hiding places. The data flow is bare: *electrodes → signal acquisition → decoder → command → actuator*. The protected party is the human operator; the assets are the integrity, authenticity, and intentionality of her command channel. The adversary is any party or process that would spoof, replay, inject, tamper with, or hijack that channel — together with the safety-critical, non-adversarial seam of distinguishing a deliberate command from a stray thought or muscle artifact. Nothing here is a metaphor for security; it *is* security, with the human inside the trust boundary and the boundary drawn at the skin.

Two design laws govern the construction.

**Intrinsic substitution** (Catalog L6): prefer replacing an extrinsic *check* with an intrinsic *property* whose existence entails what the check verified — collapse the gap between the thing and its verification, so that a forgery is not detected-and-rejected but *unconstructable*.

**Construction over detection** (Catalog L7): prefer making an attack unrepresentable (soundness by construction) to catching it (soundness by vigilance) — with the standing corollary that *every impossibility is impossibility modulo a construction assumption*. The engineering does not eliminate the trusted assumption; a hash's collision-resistance is an assumption. It shrinks it: fewer, smaller, more physical, more auditable, more one-time. "Detection → impossibility" is, stated exactly, "detection → one residual physical assumption."

## §2. The intrinsic primitives

Each primitive below was chosen by running L6 against a place the channel would otherwise do an extrinsic check, and asking: *what property would make the check unnecessary because the property's existence entails what the check verified?*

- **Object-capability / least authority** (Miller, 2006; Hardy, 1988). Authority *is* the unforgeable, attenuable reference; there is no ambient authority to be confused into misuse. The forged command is not caught — it is unconstructable, a reference to nothing. This is the structural antidote to an "edgeless" authorization that cannot tell a command from a stray thought.
- **Zero-knowledge proof** (Goldwasser–Micali–Rackoff, 1989). Verify "a live, intended operator produced this" without holding the raw neural stream — nothing is shown, so nothing in transit can be intercepted. *Caveat:* a proof protects the wire, not the prover endpoint, and carries soundness-of-content, not soundness-of-*now*; it must be bound to a fresh challenge or a recording replays it.
- **Content-addressing / hash-chained log** (Merkle, 1979/1987). The name is the hash; a wrong byte is a different name, not a corrupted one. *Caveat:* tamper-*evidence* is detection, not prevention, unless the root is anchored where the adversary cannot reach.
- **Continuous probabilistic identity.** Replace the binary login token — stealable once, valid thereafter — with a maintained posterior `P(authorized | recent signal)` that decays without fresh live evidence. There is no static credential to capture. The posterior runs on a *fast* clock and gates policy; it never touches the signing key.
- **Corticomuscular-coherence (CMC) witness** (Conway et al., 1995). A live coupling between motor cortex (EEG) and the contracting muscle (EMG), in the beta band, under an unpredictable runtime motor challenge. Its *existence* carries **liveness** and **freshness** intrinsically — a recording has no live coupling, and a coupling that tracks a challenge chosen after the recording was made cannot be replayed. *Caveat, stated honestly:* CMC carries liveness and freshness, but its **identity**-discriminability is the weak leg (the individual-signature claim is unsettled — low confidence); it binds two-and-a-half of *who/alive/now*, and the half is where the fuzzy extractor must live.
- **Error-related-potential (ErrP) veto** (Gehring et al., 1993; Falkenstein et al., 1991; Chavarriaga & Millán in BCI). The operator's involuntary error-monitoring as a disauthorization channel: an action proceeds only in the *absence* of the operator's own error signal. Consent is reframed from an affirmative act to a continuous involuntary veto — the reflective self vetoing the motor self. *Caveat:* the veto's coverage is exactly the operator's perception and not one bit more; an action that does not look wrong to her generates no error signal, and single-trial detection is noisy.
- **Fuzzy extractor** (Dodis–Reyzin–Smith, 2004; cancelable transforms, Ratha et al., 2001). Reproduce a stable signing key from a noisy biometric via rotatable helper data; this converts "compare to a stored template" into "the key reconstructs, or it is garbage." The wrong person yields not a failed match but a reference to nothing — *who*, dragged across the detection→construction line. The discipline it imposes is exact: **intrinsic at the centre, revocable at the surface** — the helper data rotates the *stored form*, never the body.

## §3. The merged architecture

The primitives do not stack as independent checks; they collapse, because most of them were one existence-condition described several ways. A neural command is not data plus a credential. It exists only as a capability **minted iff three clauses hold at once**:

> **the mint condition (the *kun*):** `who (fuzzy-extracted key) ∧ alive-and-now (CMC under runtime challenge) ∧ un-vetoed (no ErrP in the commit window)`.

Fail any clause and there is no capability — not a rejected command, a **non-command**. Authentication, liveness, freshness, integrity, and intentionality stop being five gates and become five conditions on the existence of one object. This is the *kun* at the binary level: a command spoken into being only when the truths hold together, and never uttered when one fails.

Three structural facts discipline the design.

**Two-clock identity.** "Who" runs on two clocks that must not be confused. The *slow*, enrollment-stable substrate mints the key (fuzzy-extractor-bindable precisely because it does not drift). The *fast*, adaptive posterior gates policy and never touches the key. The slow clock covers identity-of-record; the fast clock covers authentication-in-the-moment and covers the replay blind spot the CMC challenge defends.

**Intrinsic core, extrinsic shell, and a third thing.** The mint condition is the intrinsic core. Around it sits a deliberately *extrinsic* shell — rotatable device keys and cancelable helper data, leases that expire by construction, risk-stratified friction — extrinsic on purpose, because revocation needs an outside the physiology cannot provide. And around both sits a third thing that is neither: **the operating point on the authenticity ↔ availability curve**, a value judgment about which failure harms more (the false-deny that freezes the limb versus the false-accept that moves it wrong), authored not once but continuously, re-spoken for the life of the channel.

**Authenticity is not intent-fidelity.** Even a perfect mint guarantees only that *a live, enrolled operator's unaltered signal produced this command.* It does not guarantee that this is the command she *meant*: a faithful transport of a mis-decode still mints. A MAC and nonce close injection on the decode→actuator path, but the mis-decode is not an injection — it is the pipeline working correctly on a wrong reading. This residual is closed only by *forcing the action into the operator's perceptual field within the veto window*, which makes the graduated-authorization shell's real job precise: it exists to *maximise the coverage of the ErrP veto.*

## §4. Teardown I — the two irreducible roots

A rational adversary ignores the cryptography and the biometrics and attacks the two things that cannot be made intrinsic: **enrollment** and the **trusted sensing path** (the TCB). Much of the channel collapses to the second once it is owned.

- **CMC liveness** assumes a tamper-evident path from body to verifier. Control the input to the verifier and you do not reproduce biology — you hand it two correlated streams you manufacture; "live coupling" becomes two numbers.
- **The ErrP veto** assumes the same EEG path is honest. Own it and you suppress the "no" or inject "no-error."
- **Zero-knowledge** protects the wire, not the endpoint: the raw neural data exists momentarily on the device to generate the proof, so the honeypot is the prover, not the channel.
- **The hash-chained log** gives tamper-evidence — detection, not prevention; a compromised verifier writes a perfectly consistent false history unless the root is anchored where the adversary does not reach (Kocher's side channels, and SGX-class enclave breaks, make "trusted" a scheduled assumption, not a fact).

The headline is one physical assumption beneath many elegant intrinsic primitives — and a convergence worth naming carefully: the defender's decomposition and the attacker's economics land on the same two roots. That agreement is *structural certainty about the model*, not independent confirmation (Catalog L10): the architect's `min(authored trust)` and the adversary's `max(attack yield)` co-locate by construction. Its lesson is operational, not evidential — *spend on harder roots, not more primitives* — with the corollary that concentrating trust into a smaller root lowers the probability of root failure while raising its stakes to total, so a hard root must come with a degradation story that survives its fall.

**Enrollment poisoning is strictly worse than TCB compromise.** A poisoned TCB may leave anomalies; a poisoned enrollment leaves a perfectly healthy system in which every intrinsic primitive then faithfully protects the adversary's binding, with no runtime alarm, because the runtime is working exactly as designed around a counterfeit root. The right response is the one move available for a root that cannot be made intrinsic: distribute its authorship. **Threshold (m-of-n) enrollment** — quorum the *decision*, not the data: multiple independent witnesses attest a commitment or proof of valid enrollment while only one enclave ever touches raw biometric. This is the two-person rule, ported. *Caveat (the same bill in different currency):* the two-person rule comes from a domain where denial-of-availability is the goal, but for an assistive device denial-of-enrollment is denial-of-function — quorum converts forgeable-by-one into deniable-by-one, moving the irreducible cost into false-deny, the currency the device can least afford. Bind it to the rare.

## §5. Teardown II — the human seam, which survives a perfect TCB

The deeper tier is the set of attacks that survive even a flawless sensing path, because better hardware cannot fix them — they attack the seam between the human and the system. Five named seams collapse, on inspection, into **four roots**, and the collapse is where the real risk concentrates (because both a per-joint analysis and a single analyst go blind to composites):

1. **Intent-semantics** (relay + perception-blind deception, *one* attack). The false belief that makes the operator authorize B-believing-A is the very fact that guarantees the disguised action generates no error signal; they share a root — her model of what she is authorizing — and fall to one defense.
2. **Duress** (rubber-hose): live, perceiving, unwilling.
3. **Legitimate-but-wrong** (slow drift + holder-compromise): correct exercise by a principal the system cannot distinguish from the rightful one; no sharp event to alarm on.
4. **Biological permanence**: the one purely physical, purely irreversible residue.

Only root 4 is irreducible-by-construction; roots 1–3 reduce to *expensive, observable, reversible, multi-party* — bounded, not closed. Two honesties sharpen the picture. First, the **mitigations conflict**: m-of-n enrollment (the fix for poisoning) widens the raw-biometric capture surface at the one moment it is unavoidable (the fix for root 4 is to minimise exactly that surface); perception-blindness defeats duress-signalling; a deceived baseline defeats trajectory-anomaly detection. Second, **biological permanence has three feeders, not two** — enrollment capture, proving-time TCB compromise, and proving-time *side-channel* leakage of the raw witness through power, EM, or timing (Kocher, 1996/1999) — and the third is worst exactly where the device is most intimate, because an implant or wearable is maximally probe-able. "Minimise raw residency" sharpens to "minimise *physical observability during computation*."

And beneath all of these is the seam neither decomposition imagined because both inherited the same frame: **will-shaping.** A closed BCI loop conditions what the operator comes to want; an adversary who owns the *feedback* path can, over sessions, shape her future intent. She then forms a genuine, perceived, willing, un-coerced command — and *every check passes, because every check is true.* No authentication catches a genuinely-formed shaped will, because (by the Part I no-aseity result) there is no pristine reference will on file to compare against. The defense leaves the authentication layer entirely: cap the **loop-gain below the operator's reflection-bandwidth**; externalise legibility of the shaping to a party outside the loop, because the one inside it is the shaped one; and protect the *meta-capacity* to revise — itself only defensible by an external, pre-committed, edit-locked prior (a Ulysses pact, which is enrollment re-applied to the self). These bound; they do not close.

## §6. The honest specification

The teardown forces a specification the channel may state without overselling:

> The channel makes **forgery, replay, and channel-injection** infeasible relative to a hardware root and a single authored enrollment. It does **not** establish that an authenticated command expresses the operator's uncoerced will toward the specific effect it produces. It **reduces, never eliminates** coercion, perception-blind deception, slow drift, and holder-compromise, and each reduction is **paid in availability**. It contains exactly **one irreversible failure** — biometric capture, at enrollment, proving-time TCB, or side-channel — and a concentration of trust into two roots whose breach is total, requiring degradation that survives root failure. The strong claim is **authentication, to a thresholded root with surviving degradation**. The authorization-of-meaning claim is **bounded mitigation**.

Four constructions carry the bounded half:

- **Reversibility-by-construction.** Replace detect-then-abort with an action model in which nothing is irreversible within the veto window; the unreliable ErrP veto becomes a safe commit. This is the answer to single-trial false-negatives.
- **Risk-stratified friction.** Bind confirmation and quorum to per-action *irreversibility × consequence*, never globally; high-consequence acts are rare, so the tax lands rarely — and *confirmation is depleting capital* (Catalog L12): spend the flow of attention on the trivial and the stock is bankrupt for the critical, so spend almost none.
- **Lease / decay revocation.** Replace "still valid?" lookups with capabilities that expire by construction; revocation becomes expiry, decay tied to the identity posterior.
- **Physical safety envelope** (*safety ≠ security*). For high-frequency, high-consequence, low-latency actuation — where no veto window exists — do not route through the authorization path at all; bound the *effect* with local physical limits that never ask about intent. This is the explicit admission that one class of action cannot be driven by the involuntary-veto model and must fall to physical envelope or denial.

## §7. What the architecture demonstrates

The channel is the *for* of Parts I–II made into hardware. Its strong part — everything driven across the line into impossibility-by-construction — is precisely the part that never touches the human. Its residual threat model is almost entirely the human and the authored root: coercion, deception, legitimate-but-wrong, will-shaping, enrollment, and the two clocks (biometric capture and root-assumption decay) that run monotone toward certainty over a deployment lifetime. The machine side goes nearly impregnable-by-construction *for now, modulo decaying assumptions*; the irreducible surface is the person and the act of authoring trust.

This is not a defect to engineer out. It *is* the perimeter — the security face of the thesis's central figure, the ground that cannot be made intrinsic. "Mitigates, cannot eliminate" is not the disappointing footnote; restated correctly, it is the design goal: make every human-seam attack expensive, observable, reversible, and multi-party; turn single points into quorums and irreversible acts into reversible ones; and accept the one residue by never standing on the body alone. The strong, intrinsic part of the channel is real and worth building. Everything it cannot close is handed up to Part IV, where the limit is shown to be not an engineering shortfall but a theorem.
