# Editorial Notes

These notes accept and resolve the adversarial reviewer's flags (full record in
`_QA-REVIEW.md`). The reviewer found **no fabricated sources and no invented page
numbers** in any chapter. What follows fixes the two real classes of issue:
(a) interpretive theses stated at a higher confidence than the argument carries,
and (b) two minor citation-form items. Read the chapters with these in force.

## Confidence corrections (read these labels into the text)

- **Part I — *esse ab alio* ≈ *śūnyatā*.** The convergence of conferred-existence
  theology with Madhyamaka emptiness is an **[moderate], argued** equation, not an
  established identity. Note the genuine disanalogy: Madhyamaka denies *every*
  ground, including *ipsum esse subsistens*; the Thomist reading retains an
  ultimate ground that is itself *a se*. The corpus uses the convergence for its
  shared *negative* claim (creatures lack aseity), not for identity of systems.
- **Part I — the Golem/aleph reading.** "The legend is exact about the
  metaphysics" is **authorial gloss**, not a claim the source makes. The
  *emet/met/aleph* motif *illustrates* no-aseity; it does not *evidence* it.
- **Part I — mereological eliminativism ≠ metaphysical no-aseity.** Van Inwagen /
  Merricks deny that *composites exist*; no-aseity says *existents lack
  independence*. These are **structurally analogous, not the same operation**;
  treat the convergence as [moderate].
- **Part II — "generativity and direction are inversely related."** A **proposed
  thesis / author coinage**, not an established law. Read as [moderate].
- **Part II — abiogenesis "is selected by physical law."** Read as **[moderate]**;
  the determinate "is" overstates contestable terrain. The load-bearing point
  ("selection is never criterion-free") does not depend on the strong reading.
- **Part II / III — "the is/ought gap and the authn/authz gap are the same gap."**
  An **argued mapping [moderate]**, the chapter's central analogy, not a
  demonstrated identity. Its evidential weight is the *independent* convergence of
  two fields, not a proof of sameness.
- **Part III — categorical security claims.** "No authentication catches a
  genuinely-formed shaped will" and "enrollment-poisoning is strictly worse than
  TCB-compromise" are **argued architectural positions [moderate]**, consistent
  with the honest spec's framing of the human seam as *bounded mitigation, not
  theorem*. Read "strictly" and the categorical negative with that label.
- **Part III — "more than most authentication systems offer."** Drop or hedge:
  an **unverified comparative generalization**.

## "Theorem" and "proof" (Part IV) — usage caveat

Part IV's "fixed-point **theorem**" and its talk of what the argument "**proves**"
use those words in a **constitutive / conceptual** sense, *not* as a formalized
mathematical derivation: there is no defined space, map, or proof object. The
claim is that the *type* `free authorship (historical, normative) → present-
physical property (instantaneous, physical)` has no inhabitant other than the
node itself, argued by the unavailability of a constructor for either transit.
This is a **rigorous conceptual result, not a formal proof** — read every
"theorem/proves" in that register.

**Computational irreducibility** (Part IV, §3–4) is invoked as an *informal
gloss* (the standard attribution is Wolfram, which is **not** in the verified
bibliography). It is illustrative, not load-bearing: the history→instant transit
fails even without it, because full-fidelity instantiation produces a new author,
not a captured property.
