## Part I — Conferred Existence
- file: `thesis\part-I-conferred-existence.md` (19813 chars)
- verdict: publish-with-notes; faithfulness: faithful

- oversell flags:
  - Section 3 superlatives 'the strongest available argument for no-aseity' and creation is nihilism's 'most exact confirmation' state a contested interpretive thesis at near-certainty register; the convergence claim equates esse ab alio with sunyata, which Westerhoff/Madhyamaka would not straightforwardly endorse (Madhyamaka denies any ground including ipsum esse subsistens). The text does flag the disagreement-about-ground, so this is argued-position rather than fabricated fact, but the 'complete and stated without softening' framing reads more certain than the comparison warrants — a [moderate] label on the convergence equation would be more honest.

Claim is correctly scoped to 'Merricks reaches' so acceptable, but the surrounding 'whatever one makes of the arguments, their upshot is structurally identical to no-aseity' slightly oversells the structural equivalence between mereological eliminativism and metaphysical no-aseity (one denies composites exist; the other says existents lack independence — not the same operation).
  - Section 2 metaphysical reading of the aleph/emet/met legend ('the legend is exact about the metaphysics,' aleph as 'the exception,' existence hanging 'on an edge so thin it approaches zero — which is precisely what no-aseity predicts') is presented as if the Golem legend confirms a metaphysical thesis. This is the author's interpretive construction, not a claim the source makes; it is unlabeled and phrased as derivation ('which no-aseity predicts') rather than as authorial gloss. Not a citation fault, but the confidence register exceeds what a legend can evidence.
