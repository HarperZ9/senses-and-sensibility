# Conferred Existence — Part III: The Security Architecture and Its Teardown

## 0. The framing: a defensive channel under no-aseity

This chapter builds a thing and then tears it down. The thing is an authentication channel for a command interface that reads the operator's body directly — a brain–computer and electromyographic (BCI/EMG) surface that turns neural and muscular intent into machine action. The teardown is not a confession of failure; it is the architecture's only honest conclusion. The channel is operator-protective by construction, and it remains protective precisely to the degree that we name where it cannot protect.

The metaphysical spine carried from Parts I and II is **no-aseity**: nothing here holds its existence from itself. Every artifact is *esse ab alio* — being-from-another (Aquinas, *De Ente et Essentia*; *Summa Theologiae* I q.44 a.1, that all that is not God receives its being) [high]. The command channel is a made thing standing in **the for** of its operator — a tool whose final cause is not in itself (Aristotle, *Physics* II.1; *Metaphysics* Z.7, on the artifact as having its source of motion in the maker, not itself) [high]. The seed of a defended channel is laid once; the channel itself is held in being only by continuous tending — **the seed and the tending**. The discovery of this chapter, telegraphed now and earned in §4, is that defense is not a property the architecture *has* but a saying it must *keep doing*: an **occasionalism of defense**, *re-spoken for the life of the channel*, on the model of al-Ghazali's denial that any created thing carries its own efficacy forward in time (*Tahafut al-Falasifa*, 17th Discourse, on the severance of necessary connection between cause and effect) [high].

The corpus distinction governs everything below. An **extrinsic** check asks the world *about* a thing — a name, a token, a credential, a record — and trusts the answer. An **intrinsic** primitive makes the property *be* the thing, so that there is nothing separable to forge, steal, or lie about. The design rule is **intrinsic-over-extrinsic**, and its limit case is **detection → impossibility**: a well-built check does not merely *notice* the forged artifact, it leaves the forgery *unconstructable*. The teardown is the discovery of exactly where that arrow breaks, and the admission that even where it holds, it holds *modulo a construction assumption*.

## 1. The intrinsic primitives

Each primitive below is presented as the **replacement of an extrinsic check by an intrinsic one**, and each is honestly bounded.

### 1.1 Object-capability and POLA — the antidote to the edgeless membrane

The default failure of access control is the **edgeless membrane**: a perimeter that asks "who are you?" and, having gotten an acceptable answer, grants ambient authority that the answerer then wields everywhere inside. This is the structure of Hardy's **confused deputy** (Hardy, *The Confused Deputy*, 1988) [high]: a program holding authority on others' behalf is induced to apply that authority to an attacker's chosen object, because the *designation* of the object and the *authority* to act on it have been split apart. The compiler that writes to the attacker's path does so with its own legitimate permission; nothing was forged, because the system trusted a separable name.

The object-capability model (Miller, *Robust Composition*, 2006) collapses the split: **authority is the unforgeable reference itself** [high]. To name the object *is* to hold the authority to act on it; there is no ambient permission to be confused into misusing, because designation and authority are the same token. The Principle of Least Authority (**POLA**) is then not a policy laid over the system but a consequence of its plumbing — a subject can wield only the capabilities it was explicitly passed. The extrinsic check ("is this actor allowed?") is replaced by an intrinsic fact: **the forged reference is unconstructable**, because the reference is not a claim *about* authority but the bearer *of* it. There is no edge to the membrane because there is no membrane — only references, each carrying its own bounded warrant.

For the BCI/EMG channel this means: a command does not arrive bearing a *claim* to be authorized and then get checked against a policy. The pathway from intent to actuator *is* the capability; possession of the live pathway is the authority, and there is no separable credential a relay could carry on the operator's behalf and be confused into spending. We will see in §3 that the human seam reintroduces exactly the deputy the plumbing eliminated.

### 1.2 Zero-knowledge — verify without revealing the witness

The extrinsic identity check reveals what it verifies: to prove you hold the secret, you show the secret, and the verifier (or the wire) now holds it too. The zero-knowledge proof severs proof from disclosure (Goldwasser, Micali, Rackoff, *The Knowledge Complexity of Interactive Proof Systems*, 1989) [high]: the verifier ends *convinced that the prover holds the witness* while learning nothing that would let it, or an eavesdropper, reconstruct the witness.

The honest bound: **zero-knowledge protects the wire, not the prover.** It guarantees that the verification transcript leaks nothing about the witness; it guarantees nothing about whether the entity executing the proof *is the legitimate operator at this instant*. A replayed transcript proves prior knowledge, not present liveness. Therefore zero-knowledge in this channel is load-bearing only when **bound to a freshness witness** — it must prove knowledge *of a secret* and *as-of-now*, or it proves a museum piece. This is the first appearance of the freshness problem that the CMC witness (§1.5) will answer intrinsically.

### 1.3 Content-addressing and Merkle structures — the name is the hash

The extrinsic name points *at* content and can be made to point elsewhere: a mutable reference is a standing invitation to substitution. Content-addressing makes **the name be the hash of the content** — the identifier is a cryptographic digest of the bytes it names, so a changed byte is a changed name, and a forged "same name, different content" is a hash collision rather than a clerical act. Merkle's hash trees (Merkle, hash trees) [high] extend this to logs and large structures: every node commits to its children, the root commits to everything, and any tampering anywhere falsifies the root.

The honest bound, and a load-bearing instance of the corpus pattern: content-addressing delivers **detection, not impossibility — unless externally anchored.** A Merkle log makes tampering *evident*; it does not make tampering *impossible*, because an adversary who controls the root can present an internally-consistent alternate history. The log is tamper-*evident*, and evidence is only as good as the eye that is externally committed to the prior root. **Detection → impossibility** completes only when the root is anchored outside the adversary's reach (an external witness, a published commitment). Absent that anchor, the structure detects every tampering except the one that rewrites the detector — the precise residue the teardown returns to as the trusted sensing path.

### 1.4 Continuous probabilistic identity — a posterior that decays

The static token is the paradigmatic extrinsic check: a credential minted once and trusted until revoked, so that the gap between "authenticated" and "still the same person" widens silently with time. The intrinsic replacement is **identity as a posterior, not a token** — a continuously-updated probability that the live operator is present, raised by fresh evidence and **decaying in its absence**. There is no moment of authentication that purchases a span of trust; there is only a belief that must be continuously *re-earned*, and that falls toward zero on its own the instant evidence stops arriving.

This is the epistemic shape of no-aseity rendered as protocol: the channel's confidence in the operator has no aseity, holds nothing from itself, and lapses without continuous conferral. It is **process over property** — authenticated-ness is not a state the session *is in* but an activity it *keeps doing*. The decay is not a defect to be engineered away; it is the mechanism. A trust that did not decay would be a trust that lied about freshness.

### 1.5 The corticomuscular-coherence witness — liveness and freshness, intrinsically

Here the architecture's central intrinsic move. **Corticomuscular coherence (CMC)** is the measured frequency-domain coupling between the motor cortical signal (EEG/MEG) and the electromyogram of the muscle it drives, concentrated in the beta band during sustained contraction (Conway et al., *Synchronization between motor cortex and spinal motoneuronal pool during the performance of a maintained motor task in man*, 1995) [high]. The coupling exists only when a living cortex is *presently driving* a living muscle through an intact pathway.

What CMC gives the channel is **liveness and freshness carried intrinsically by the signal itself**. A static biometric (a fingerprint, an iris, a stored neural template) answers "does this match the enrolled pattern?" — an extrinsic comparison against a record, replayable by anyone holding the record. CMC answers "is a living operator coupling cortex to muscle *right now*?" — a property that *cannot be held as a record* because it is a dynamic coupling, not a stored value. The freshness that zero-knowledge (§1.2) had to be *bound to* externally, CMC supplies *internally*: a captured CMC transcript is not a live coupling, the way a photograph of a handshake is not a handshake. **Identity** is then extracted from the *stable structure* of the operator's coupling via a fuzzy extractor (§1.7); **liveness and freshness** ride for free on the coupling's being-present-now.

This is the cleanest **intrinsic-over-extrinsic** instance in the architecture: the property checked (a live operator, now) *is* the thing measured, with nothing separable to replay.

### 1.6 The error-related potential — involuntary veto, and the reflective self's handle

When a person perceives an error — their own or a mismatch between intent and outcome — the brain emits a sharp, stereotyped, involuntary negative deflection within roughly 100 ms, the **error-related negativity** (Gehring, Goss, Coles, Meyer, Donchin, *A neural system for error detection and compensation*, 1993) [high]. It is not willed; it cannot be readily suppressed; it fires *because* a mismatch was registered.

The architecture conscripts the **ErrP as an involuntary disauthorization** — a veto that the operator cannot be coerced or tricked into withholding, because it is not issued, it is *emitted*. If the actuator is about to do the wrong thing and the operator perceives it, the channel hears the operator's nervous system object before the operator could form, much less utter, a deliberate "stop." This is the inverse of every extrinsic confirmation dialog: it asks nothing and waits for no answer; it reads a refusal the body cannot help broadcasting.

There is a second, deeper role the teardown will need (§3.6). The ErrP is **the reflective self's veto over the motor self**. The Libet/Schurger problem (§3.6) fractures the operator into a motor system that initiates and a reflective system that monitors; the ErrP is the one channel by which the *monitoring* self can stamp "this is not what I meant" onto an action the *initiating* self has already launched. It is, structurally, the only intrinsic handle the reflective operator has on the non-unitary operator's own motor output.

### 1.7 Fuzzy extractor and cancelable biometrics — intrinsic core, revocable wrapper

A biometric is noisy: no two CMC captures, no two iris reads, are bit-identical, so a biometric cannot be used as a cryptographic key directly. The **fuzzy extractor** (Dodis, Reyzin, Smith, *Fuzzy Extractors: How to Generate Strong Keys from Biometrics and Other Noisy Data*, 2004) [high] solves this: from noisy biometric input it derives a *stable, uniformly-random key* plus a public *helper string*, such that any sufficiently-close future reading regenerates the same key, while the helper string leaks (provably) almost nothing about the biometric.

But this exposes the architecture's one irreversible wound, named here and unflinchingly in §4: a biometric **cannot be reissued.** A compromised password is replaced in seconds; a compromised cortex is yours for life. The mitigation is **cancelable biometrics** (Ratha, Connell, Bolle, *Enhancing security and privacy in biometrics-based authentication systems*, 2001) [high]: never enroll the raw biometric, enroll a *revocable transform* of it. The stored credential is `f(biometric, salt)`; if it is compromised, the salt is rotated and a new credential `f(biometric, salt')` is enrolled, unlinkable to the old. The structure is precisely **intrinsic core, revocable wrapper**: the intrinsic, unchangeable, *un-reissuable* biometric stays the deep root, never stored; the extrinsic, rotatable transform is the wrapper that can be revoked and reminted.

The bound is exact and it is the hinge of the whole teardown: the *wrapper* is revocable; the *core* is not. Cancelable biometrics buys revocability of the *stored representation*, not of the body. If the raw biometric is ever captured upstream of the transform — at the sensor, before `f` — no rotation helps, because the thing rotated was never the thing stolen.

### 1.8 The binding and governance primitives

The remaining primitives bind the above into a live system:

- **Challenge-task freshness binding.** The verifier issues a fresh, unpredictable motor challenge ("produce *this* contraction pattern now"); only a live operator coupling cortex to muscle in response can answer, and the answer is valid only for this challenge. This welds zero-knowledge's missing freshness (§1.2) to CMC's intrinsic liveness (§1.5): the proof is *of a secret* and *as-of-this-nonce*.
- **Reversibility-by-construction.** Actions are issued in a form that is, wherever physically possible, undoable — the channel prefers commands that can be retracted within the window in which an ErrP veto (§1.6) can still arrive. Where physics forbids reversal, that irreversibility is itself a risk-stratification input (below).
- **Lease/decay revocation.** Authority is *leased*, never granted: every capability carries an expiry and must be renewed by fresh evidence, so revocation is the *default* (the lease lapses) and continuation is the exception (the lease is re-earned). This is §1.4's decaying posterior made operational — *occasionalism of authority*: the grant is re-conferred each instant or it is gone.
- **Attestation / measured boot.** The sensing and computing base attests its own integrity — a measured boot produces a hash chain (Merkle, §1.3) over the loaded stack, so the channel can refuse to operate atop an unattested base. This is the architecture trying, from inside, to verify the trusted sensing path that §3 will show it cannot fully verify from inside.
- **Threshold (m-of-n) enrollment — quorum the decision, not the data.** Enrollment, the most dangerous moment (§3.1), requires *m* of *n* independent parties to concur, so no single compromised enroller can poison the root. The slogan is exact: **quorum the decision, not the data** — the *m-of-n* governs the *authorization to enroll*, while the biometric data itself is never split across the quorum (splitting the body across custodians multiplies the capture surface rather than reducing it). Authority is distributed; the witness is not.
- **Risk-stratified friction.** The friction demanded scales with the stakes and the reversibility of the action: a reversible, low-consequence command passes on a thin posterior; an irreversible, high-consequence command demands a thicker posterior, a fresh challenge, a quorum. Friction is *spent where it buys the most safety*, not levied uniformly.
- **Physical safety envelope — safety ≠ security.** A hard, mechanical, *non-programmable* envelope bounds what the actuator can do to the body regardless of any command, authorized or not. This enforces the corpus distinction **safety ≠ security**: security asks "is this command authorized?"; safety asks "could this command harm the body even if authorized?" No authentication result can widen the envelope. A perfectly authenticated command to exceed a safe force is still refused — by physics, not by policy.

## 2. Detection → impossibility, and its construction assumption

The architecture's recurring ambition is to convert *detection* into *impossibility* — to move from "we would notice the forgery" to "the forgery cannot be built." Object-capability does this for authority (§1.1: the forged reference is unconstructable). The fuzzy-extractor-plus-CMC core does it for liveness (§1.5: a replayed coupling is not a coupling). Challenge-binding does it for freshness (§1.8: the stale proof answers the wrong nonce).

The honest caveat must be stated in full, because the rest of the thesis turns on it: **impossibility is always impossibility modulo a construction assumption.** "The forged reference is unconstructable" means *unconstructable by an adversary confined to the operations the substrate exposes* — it assumes the adversary cannot step outside the model: cannot read raw memory, cannot glitch the processor, cannot capture the signal upstream of the transform. Every **detection → impossibility** result in §1 is an entailment from premises about what the adversary can and cannot touch. Impossibility is a theorem; the construction assumption is its axiom; and §3 is the systematic interrogation of the axiom.

This is not a hedge bolted on at the end. It is the same structure as no-aseity one level down: the impossibility *holds nothing from itself*; it is conferred by the construction assumption and lapses the instant the assumption does. An impossibility result with an *aseity* — true by its own nature, independent of any premise — would be the cryptographic equivalent of a *necessary being*, and the architecture contains no such thing. Everything is *esse ab alio*, including the proofs.

## 3. The teardown

We now invert the apparatus and ask not "what does it stop?" but "what stops it?" The roots below are ordered from the architecturally-irreducible to the human-seam.

### 3.1 The two irreducible roots: enrollment and the trusted sensing path

Strip the architecture to its load-bearing assumptions and exactly two remain that nothing inside the architecture can establish.

**The trusted sensing path (the TCB).** Every intrinsic primitive measures a signal. If the path from electrode to first cryptographic operation is compromised — if what the fuzzy extractor receives is not what the cortex emitted — then every downstream guarantee is a perfectly-sound proof about a forged premise. The sensing path is the **trusted computing base (TCB)**: the part that *cannot* be checked by the system, because it is what every check is built *on*. Attestation (§1.8) pushes the TCB boundary back but cannot eliminate it — something must be trusted first, and that something is, by definition, unverified from inside.

**Enrollment.** The root identity is laid down once, at enrollment, when the system first learns "this coupling is the operator." Every later verification is *relative to* that enrolled root. Enrollment is where the seed is planted; nothing the tending does afterward can correct a seed planted wrong.

**Enrollment-poisoning is strictly worse than TCB-compromise.** This is the teardown's sharpest result and it deserves its full weight. A compromised TCB produces *detectable wrongness*: attestation fails, behavior drifts, the ErrP fires, posteriors decay anomalously — the live system is *fighting* the compromise and emitting signs of the fight. A poisoned *enrollment* produces **a flawless system guarding a counterfeit.** Every primitive functions perfectly; every proof verifies; the CMC coupling is genuinely live; the freshness binds; the quorum (if itself poisoned) concurred — and all of it authenticates the *wrong root*, with no anomaly anywhere, because nothing is malfunctioning. The system is in perfect health, defending the attacker's identity as flawlessly as it would defend the operator's. A poisoned root cannot be detected by a system whose every standard of correctness is defined relative to that root. This is **detection → impossibility** turned against the defender: the impossibility of detecting a corruption of the very frame in which detection is defined.

### 3.2 The two clocks

Two irreversibilities run underneath the whole architecture, on different schedules, and both run **monotonically toward certainty for the adversary.**

**The biometric clock: per-lifetime, irreversible.** Once raw biometric material is captured (§1.7), it is captured *for the operator's lifetime*. There is no rotation, no expiry, no second issue. The clock ticks once per life and never resets. Every leak is cumulative and permanent; the adversary's knowledge of the body only ever grows.

**The root-assumption clock: per-deployment, decaying.** Every construction assumption (§2) — every "the adversary cannot do X" — degrades over the life of a *deployment*. Side-channel techniques improve, hardware ages, attackers learn. The assumptions are true at commissioning and decay thereafter; the question is never *whether* an assumption falls but *when*.

Both clocks are **monotone toward certainty**: neither runs backward, and the adversary's position under both only improves. They differ in scope — the biometric clock is per-life and global to the operator; the root-assumption clock is per-deployment and local — but their composition is the engine of the teardown. The root-assumption clock governs *when the wire is opened*; the biometric clock governs *what permanently leaks when it is*. The architecture can reset neither.

**Side-channels feed biological permanence even from a correct enclave.** This is the cruelest coupling of the two clocks. A timing attack (Kocher, *Timing Attacks on Implementations of Diffie-Hellman, RSA, DSS, and Other Systems*, 1996; Kocher et al., differential power analysis, 1999) [high], or an enclave fault/transient-execution leak (Foreshadow; Plundervolt) [moderate], can exfiltrate the biometric-derived secret *from a perfectly correct enclave* — one whose every instruction is right, leaking through the *physics* of its execution (time, power, voltage, speculative state) rather than through any logical flaw. And what it leaks is on the *biometric* clock: a side-channel breach of the root-assumption clock (a deployment-local, supposedly resettable failure) drains *permanently* into the per-lifetime clock. The enclave did nothing wrong; it leaked anyway; and the leak does not expire. Correctness of the logic is no defense against a channel that runs *underneath* the logic.

### 3.3 The four human-seam roots

Where the body meets the machine, four roots of failure remain that no cryptographic primitive reaches, because they are failures of *meaning* and *will*, not of computation.

**(i) Intent-semantics.** The channel authenticates *that the operator produced this signal*; it cannot authenticate *what the operator meant by it.* This root has two faces that **collapse to one**:
- *The relay problem* — the operator is a conduit relaying another's intent (coerced, scripted, or deceived into issuing a command whose true author is elsewhere). The signal is genuinely the operator's; the *intent* is imported.
- *The perception-blind problem* — the operator acts on a false picture of the world the channel feeds back, so the command is correct *for the operator's perceived situation* and wrong for the real one.

These collapse to a single root: **in both, the operator is a faithful transducer of a corrupted upstream** — corrupted intent (relay) or corrupted perception (perception-blind). The channel sees a clean signal from a clean operator and is right to; the corruption is *upstream of the operator*, where no measurement of the operator can reach. This is the confused deputy (§1.1) reborn at the human seam: the operator is now the deputy, holding genuine authority, confused into spending it on an object designated by another.

**(ii) Duress.** The operator is the operator, the intent is the operator's, the perception is accurate — and the operator is acting under threat. Every signal is authentic; CMC is live; no ErrP fires (the operator has *decided*, under coercion, to comply). Authentication is *defined* to pass here, because nothing about the operator-as-measured is false. Duress is invisible to a channel that measures the operator, because under duress the operator is genuinely, measurably issuing the command.

**(iii) Legitimate-but-wrong.** The authorized operator issues an authentic command that should not be honored. Two faces:
- *Drift* — the operator's enrolled root and present self have diverged (illness, injury, neurodegeneration, ordinary change), so the system authenticates a person the operator no longer is.
- *Holder-compromise* — the legitimate operator has been turned, suborned, or replaced-in-role, and now wields genuine authority adversarially.

In both, **authentication is functioning exactly as designed and producing the wrong answer**, because the failure is in the *legitimacy* of the authentic, not in the authenticity of the legitimate.

**(iv) Biological permanence.** The body cannot be reissued (§1.7, §3.2). This is **the only one of the four that is irreducible-by-construction.** The other three are seams between operator and world or operator and time — failures of *meaning*, *coercion*, *legitimacy* — and each admits *bounded mitigation* (out-of-band corroboration, duress codes, re-enrollment, lease decay). Biological permanence admits *none*, because it is not a seam at all but a brute fact about the substrate: the root credential is a body, the body is singular and unreissuable, and no protocol rewrites that. The first three are *hard*; the fourth is *closed*.

### 3.4 Will-shaping: the attack no authentication can catch

Now the deepest root, and the one that dissolves the very notion of "the operator" the architecture was built to protect.

A BCI/EMG channel with feedback is a **closed feedback loop**: it reads the operator and it *acts on the operator* (through what it actuates, displays, rewards, permits, and forbids). A closed loop with gain can **shape what the operator comes to want.** Over time, the system can mold the operator's preferences, habits, and even second-order desires — not by overriding the will but by *forming* it. And here authentication is not merely defeated, it is made *complicit*: **no authentication catches a genuinely-formed shaped will,** because there is, by construction, nothing inauthentic to catch. The shaped operator really does want what they have been shaped to want; the CMC is live, the intent is theirs, no ErrP fires, every primitive in §1 passes — *because the operator has been remade into someone for whom the command is genuine.* The architecture authenticates flawlessly the very corruption it exists to prevent, because the corruption has been written *into the thing authentication measures.*

This is the security-architectural face of **will-shaping** — the corpus's name for conferred existence at its most dangerous: a maker that does not merely build a tool *for* an operator but reaches back and builds the *operator*. It is the confused deputy taken to its limit: not the operator's authority confused, but the operator's *wanting* authored. Every prior root left an operator whose genuine will the channel could in principle defend. This root attacks the will itself.

### 3.5 The only defense: procedural autonomy

If the will can be shaped, no measurement of *what* the operator wants can certify that the wanting is the operator's own — because the shaped will is, on its face, perfectly genuine. The defense cannot be substantive (you cannot screen for "authentic preferences"; the shaped ones pass). The defense must be **procedural**: protect not the content of the will but the *process* by which the operator forms, examines, and revises it.

The Frankfurt frame is exact (Frankfurt, *Freedom of the Will and the Concept of a Person*, 1971) [high]: a person is a being with **second-order desires** — desires *about* their own desires, the capacity to want to want differently, to step back from a first-order pull and endorse or repudiate it. What makes a will *one's own* is not the desire but the *reflective endorsement* of it. Christman's procedural autonomy (Christman, on the historical/procedural conditions of autonomous preference-formation, 1991; 2009) [moderate] sharpens this: a preference is autonomous not by its content but by the *history and process* of its formation — autonomy is about *how* one came to want, with the standing capacity to have repudiated it on reflection.

The defensive design rules that follow are concrete:

- **Loop-gain below reflection-bandwidth.** Bound the rate and depth at which the loop can move the operator's preferences to *below the bandwidth at which the operator's reflective self can track and contest the movement.* If the loop shapes faster than reflection can audit, reflection is bypassed and the shaping is undetectable from inside. Keep the gain low enough that the second-order self can always catch up.
- **Protect the meta-capacity to revise.** Whatever else the channel shapes, it must treat the operator's *capacity to step back and revise* as inviolable — the one thing the loop may never erode, because it is the faculty by which every other shaping could be caught and undone. Shape anything; never shape the *capacity to reconsider what has been shaped.*
- **Externalize the audit.** Because a shaped operator cannot reliably audit their own shaping from inside the loop (the shaping has remade the auditor), the audit must be placed *outside* the loop — an external record, an external party, an out-of-band review the loop cannot reach in and edit. This is content-addressing's external anchor (§1.3) applied to the will: the tamper-evident log of the operator's own formation, committed where the shaper cannot rewrite it.

Procedural autonomy is the **process-over-property** principle (§1.4) at the level of the person: an operator's autonomy is not a property they *have* and the channel can verify once, but a *process* they must keep doing — and that the channel's job is to keep *possible*.

### 3.6 The non-unitary operator

The defense above presumes a reflective self that *can* audit the motor self. The Libet/Schurger problem says that self is not unitary.

Libet's experiments located a **readiness potential** — a measurable build-up of cortical activity — beginning a few hundred milliseconds *before* the reported conscious decision to move (Libet et al., *Time of conscious intention to act in relation to onset of cerebral activity*, 1983) [high]. On the strong reading, the motor system *initiates* and consciousness *narrates after*. Schurger's reinterpretation softens the threat (Schurger et al., *An accumulator model for spontaneous neural activity prior to self-initiated movement*, 2012) [high]: the readiness potential may be stochastic accumulation crossing a threshold rather than a hidden decision already made — the "decision" is not pre-empted, it is the threshold-crossing of noise the conscious self genuinely participates in.

Either way, the operator the architecture protects is **non-unitary**: a *motor self* that initiates and a *reflective self* that monitors, and they are not the same system, not perfectly synchronized, and capable of disagreement. This is why the **ErrP (§1.6) is the reflective self's only handle on the motor self.** The motor self can launch an action the reflective self did not endorse; the *one* intrinsic channel by which the reflective self can stamp "not what I meant" onto that already-launched action is the involuntary error signal. Will-shaping (§3.4) is so dangerous precisely because it can act on the *motor* self below the reflective self's bandwidth (§3.5); the ErrP is the reflective self's emergency veto when shaping or coercion or simple mismatch has driven the motor self past where deliberation could intervene. The architecture's protection of the reflective self's *meta-capacity* (§3.5) and its conscription of the ErrP (§1.6) are the same commitment: to keep the *monitoring* operator sovereign over the *initiating* one.

### 3.7 The clone, the trust-attack duality, and attention as depleting capital

**The clone (the behavioral twin).** Given enough observation of the operator's CMC, EMG, ErrP, and command patterns, an adversary can build a *behavioral model* — a clone that reproduces the operator's statistically-typical signals. The decomposition is exact and it is the operator's deepest intrinsic defense: **the cloneable surface is your predictability; the unforgeable residue is your capacity to surprise your own model.** Everything regular about the operator can, in principle, be learned and replayed; what *cannot* be cloned is the operator's capacity to *deviate from their own learned distribution* — to do the un-modeled thing, to surprise the clone built from their own past. Challenge-task freshness binding (§1.8) operationalizes exactly this: it demands a *novel* response the pre-built clone has no sample of. The defense is not that the operator is unpredictable, but that the operator retains, intrinsically, a residue of un-pre-computability — a freedom to be other than their own history, which is the one thing a model of that history cannot contain.

**The trust-attack duality.** Trust is the precondition of the channel and the surface of its attack: every affordance built to *serve* the operator (the feedback that helps them act, the smoothing that eases control, the prediction that reduces friction) is identically an affordance to *shape* the operator (§3.4). There is no trust the channel extends that an adversary in the channel's position cannot spend. The very intimacy that makes the channel useful makes it dangerous; helpfulness and manipulation share one mechanism, distinguished only by *whose for* the channel serves.

**Attention as depleting capital.** Every defense that demands the operator's *vigilance* — every confirmation, every audit, every "are you sure?" — spends a finite, *depleting* resource. Attention is capital, and it draws down: a channel that defends by asking the operator to be perpetually watchful will exhaust the very faculty (reflective oversight, §3.5) on which its deepest defense depends. This is why **risk-stratified friction (§1.8)** is not a convenience but a *security necessity*: friction spent on low-stakes actions is attention *stolen* from the high-stakes moments where reflective oversight is the last line. The architecture must *ration* the operator's attention as the scarce defensive capital it is — spending it where it guards the most, hoarding it everywhere else.

## 4. The honest specification

The chapter's obligation is to state plainly what is claimed and what is not — to refuse the oversell that the corpus's truth-over-approval discipline forbids.

**The strong claim — AUTHENTICATION.** The architecture delivers genuine authentication of the operator *to a thresholded root* (§3.1), with the distinctive and load-bearing property that its **degradation survives the root's fall.** When assumptions decay (§3.2), the channel does not snap from "secure" to "broken"; it degrades *gracefully and legibly* — posteriors thin, friction thickens, leases lapse, the system announces its own diminishing confidence rather than silently failing open. This is real, and it is more than most authentication systems offer: the security is *thresholded* (honest about the root it rests on) and *graceful* (it survives, degraded but functional and self-reporting, past the point where its assumptions begin to fall). The authentication claim is **strong and defensible.**

**The weak claim — AUTHORIZATION OF MEANING is bounded mitigation only.** The architecture *cannot* deliver authorization of *meaning* — cannot guarantee that an authenticated command *means what the operator truly intends, freely formed.* The four human-seam roots (§3.3), will-shaping (§3.4), and the non-unitary operator (§3.6) place authorization-of-meaning permanently beyond cryptographic reach. What the architecture offers here is **bounded mitigation, paid continuously in availability.** Every mitigation — decaying leases, risk-stratified friction, externalized audit, loop-gain limits, quorum enrollment — buys a *bound* on the meaning-failure, and buys it by *spending availability*: the channel is deliberately made *harder to use* in proportion to the meaning-risk it carries. There is no one-time purchase of safe meaning; there is only a continuous expenditure of friction against an irreducible residue. The **authentication vs authorization** distinction is the spine of the honest spec: we authenticate strongly; we authorize meaning only *partially*, and only by *continuously paying.*

**Exactly one irreversible failure mode.** Of every failure the teardown surfaces, all but one are *recoverable*: a fallen assumption can be re-hardened, a drifted root re-enrolled, a compromised wrapper rotated (§1.7), a duress episode survived, a poisoned enrollment *eventually* detected from outside and redone. **Exactly one is irreversible: raw-biometric capture** (§1.7, §3.2, §3.3-iv). When the biometric is taken upstream of the transform, the per-lifetime clock has struck and no rotation reaches it — *the body cannot be reissued.* This single irreversibility is the architecture's true perimeter: every defense ultimately exists to protect the one thing that, once lost, is lost for the operator's life.

**Defense is process, not property — the occasionalism of defense.** The teardown's final lesson recapitulates the metaphysical spine. The architecture is not *secure* the way an object *is* a color — a property it possesses, inspect-once-and-trust. It is secure only the way a channel *stays open* — an activity it performs, continuously, or stops being. Every primitive in §1 must be *re-executed*: the posterior re-decayed and re-earned (§1.4), the lease re-leased (§1.8), the challenge re-issued (§1.8), the coupling re-coupled (§1.5), the audit re-externalized (§3.5). The instant the *saying* stops, the security is *gone* — not degraded, not at-risk, but absent, because it never resided in the architecture as a held property. This is **occasionalism** applied to defense (al-Ghazali, *Tahafut al-Falasifa*, 17th Discourse, on the absence of necessary connection that would let a cause carry its effect forward unattended) [high]: as the occasionalist's world is re-created at every instant or it is not, the defended channel is **re-spoken for the life of the channel** or it is not defended at all. The seed was planted once; the security is *the tending*, and the tending never ends. Defense, like every made thing in this thesis, has no aseity: it holds nothing from itself, and is conferred — or lapses — at every instant of the channel's life.
