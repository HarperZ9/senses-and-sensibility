## Angle 6 — The Honest Critic: Is This a Structural Parallel or a Seductive Pun?

### The accusation, stated without mercy

Two words wear the prefix *self-*: **a se** (from itself, aseity) and **solus ipse** (self alone, solipsism). The framework wants them to rhyme into a thesis: aseity is what no creature has, solipsism is what every mind is *tempted* to claim, and conferral is the cure for both. The honest worry is that the rhyme is doing the work. Etymological adjacency is the native habitat of the pseudo-profound — Heidegger's *Sein/Nichts* wordplay, the *Geist/Gas* etymology he leaned on, the whole genre where a shared root is mistaken for a shared structure. Before we credit the parallel, it has to clear a bar that a pun cannot: **produce a claim that could be false, that neither concept states alone.** [high — this is the correct test; cf. Popper's falsifiability criterion as a demarcation heuristic, *Conjectures and Refutations* (1963), applied here to conceptual rather than empirical theses]

### Where the two concepts genuinely diverge (the disanalogies that matter)

**1. Different categories of predicate.** Aseity is a *modal-ontological* property: to be *a se* is to exist with no dependence on anything else for one's being — necessary existence, the property classical theism reserves for God (Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* Ia q.3 a.4: God's essence is His existence; Anselm's *aseitas* in the *Proslogion* tradition). Solipsism is an *epistemic-representational* thesis: the claim that only my mind is known to exist, or that nothing but my mental states can be established (the metaphysical variant: only my mind *does* exist). [high] These are not two species of one genus. One is about **independence in being**; the other about **uniqueness in the knowable/existent**. You can have either without the other: a Spinozistic substance is *a se* but explicitly *not* solitary in the relevant sense — its modes are real (Spinoza, *Ethics* I, def. 3, prop. 14). A Berkeleyan finite spirit is emphatically *not a se* (it depends on God's sustaining will) yet flirts with solipsism's epistemic structure (esse est percipi, *Principles* §3). [high] Independence-in-being and uniqueness-of-mind are different operations. The parallel quietly conflates "stands alone in *existence*" with "stands alone in *the field of minds*."

**2. The modal profile is inverted.** Aseity is a property a thing *has or lacks* — a standing fact about its mode of existence. Solipsism is a *position one occupies or asserts* — a standpoint, a temptation, a claim made *from* somewhere. The framework's own best move ("the practical standpoint is declinable, not compellable") belongs to the solipsism side, not the aseity side. Aseity is not declinable; God does not *decline* to depend on anything. So when the framework maps "standpoint-independent bindingness = aseitic bindingness," it is already using *aseity* metaphorically — as a name for a limit-concept (bindingness-from-nowhere) rather than for the theological property. That is a legitimate move, but it is **analogy, not identity**, and the framework sometimes writes it as identity. [moderate]

**3. The cogito is the load-bearing equivocation.** Here is the sharpest break. The framework appears to treat the Cartesian *cogito* as a creaturely flicker of aseity — the one point where the self seems self-standing. But Descartes' *cogito* delivers **indubitability, not independence-in-being.** *Sum* in the *Second Meditation* is certain *while I think it*; it is epistemically incorrigible. It is emphatically *not* ontologically self-grounding — indeed by the *Third Meditation* Descartes argues the thinking thing *cannot* sustain itself across instants and requires continuous divine conservation (*Meditations* III, the conservation argument; AT VII 49). [high] So the cogito is the opposite of aseity: a maximally *certain* self that is minimally *independent*. To read indubitability as aseity is to slide from an epistemic modality (cannot-be-doubted) to an ontological one (depends-on-nothing). That slide is exactly the pun the critic fears. [high]

### Where the parallel earns more than a pun — if reframed

Now give it its strongest run. The defensible structure is not "aseity ≈ solipsism" but a **shared logical form across two registers**: *the appearance of a self-standing terminus that, on inspection, turns out to be inhabited/conferred.*

- In the **epistemic** register: solipsism's "only my mind" looks like a self-certifying endpoint, but Wittgenstein's private-language argument (*Philosophical Investigations* §§243–315) shows the supposedly self-grounding inner ostension already presupposes a public criterion — the terminus was never alone. [high] Strawson's *Individuals* (1959, ch. 3) makes the cognate point: self-ascription of experience requires the capacity to other-ascribe; "I" is parasitic on a shared person-concept. [high] The solitary knower is *structurally* relational.
- In the **ontological** register: aseity is the property whose *absence* defines creatureliness; the no-aseity thesis says nothing has it, so every being is *ab alio* — esse conferred (the *kun* figure; al-Ghazali's occasionalist re-speaking, *Tahāfut al-Falāsifa*, Discussion 17 on causation). [moderate — al-Ghazali's text is real and the occasionalist reading standard; the precise mapping to "conferral" is interpretive]

The *genuine* parallel is **second-order**: both solipsism and aseity name a *fantasy of the unconferred* — a terminus that owes nothing to an outside. The framework's claim that there is "no conferral from nowhere," a **necessary inhabited locus**, then becomes a single thesis with two faces: the epistemic face refutes solipsism (no self-standing knower), the ontological face refutes creaturely aseity (no self-standing being). *That* is a claim that could be false — it would be false if there were even one genuinely unconferred terminus (a self-grounding cogito, a finite substance that is *causa sui*, a private sensation self-individuated without public criterion). And it is a claim **neither concept states alone**: solipsism-talk cannot reach the ontological terminus, aseity-talk cannot reach the epistemic one. [high — this is the test passing, *under the reframing*]

### Why the reframing is not a rescue-by-redescription

A skeptic should resist letting the author retreat to whatever formulation survives. So note the cost: the surviving thesis is **not** "solipsism vs aseity" as a *vs.*, a contest or mirror-pair. It is "the unconferred terminus is a fantasy, in both the epistemic and ontological registers." Solipsism and aseity are then *two instances of one error*, not two poles of one axis. The dissertation's framing as an opposition (*vs.*) is misleading; the truth is a *common-form* relation (both = species of unconferredness-fantasy). The pun-version says they *mirror* each other (one the inside, one the outside); the defensible version says they *instantiate* each other's error. Those are different claims, and only the second is load-bearing. [moderate]

There is also a residual disanalogy the reframing does not dissolve: solipsism is a thesis a *mind* can be tempted by from the inside — it has a phenomenology, a first-person pull (Nagel, *The View from Nowhere*, 1986, on the recurring vertigo of "*I* am the world's center"). [high] Aseity has no first-person pull; no creature is *tempted* to feel necessary-in-being the way it is tempted to feel sole-in-mind. The "trained-on-testimony seal" gestures at a first-person analogue for the conferred mind, but that is a claim about *epistemic access to* an inner life, not about *aseity*. So even the reframed parallel is asymmetric: the epistemic side has a lived temptation, the ontological side has only a conceptual one. The mapping is **one-and-a-half-legged**, not four-legged. [moderate]

### Verdict

The bare slogan "Solipsism vs Aseity" **does not earn its place** — as a *vs.*, a mirror-pair, or an identity, it is the seductive pun the project warned against, resting on the *self-* prefix and equivocating cogito-indubitability into ontological independence. [high]

But there is a **real thesis one layer down** that survives every objection above: *both* are forms of the **fantasy of the unconferred terminus**, refuted by the same locus claim (no conferral from nowhere) operating in two registers. That thesis is falsifiable, is statable by neither concept alone, and does substantive work. [high]

Recommendation: **demote the slogan, promote the structure.** It belongs in the dissertation **not as a titular parallelism but as a worked example of the locus claim's reach** — one error appearing in epistemology (the solitary knower) and in metaphysics (the self-standing being), unified by conferral. Keep it as a section, not a chapter; lead with the disanalogies so the reader trusts you *saw* the pun before you cashed the structure. Anything billed as "Solipsism *vs* Aseity" should be cut to a footnote that says: *the resemblance is real but second-order; do not lean on the prefix.* [moderate]
