## Verdict: Does Not Survive the Thin/Thick Dilemma

### The dilemma applied

**Horn 1 — Thin.** Read "occupied-at-an-index" as "has a spatiotemporal location." This is trivially true of every physical event. Radioactive decay, digestion, and neural firing are all "indexed" in this sense. The Spencer-Brown/Shannon machinery adds no modal content: "selection-against-alternatives" with a spatiotemporal index just is a physical event described in information-theoretic vocabulary. The seam between (C) and (Z) collapses to "event occurring vs. not occurring." That is pratityasamutpada relabeled. No distinctively metaphysical work is done; the contribution shrinks to the membrane identity observation alone.

The defense is not simply impaled on Horn 1. It does more than spatiotemporal indexing: the claim is specifically about selection-against-alternatives as a structural property, which is not shared by all physical events. A rock rolling downhill has a location but does not select among alternatives in any non-trivial sense. So "thick" is doing something. Horn 1 does not flatten the defense entirely — the defense avoids the purely trivial reading.

**Horn 2 — Thick.** Read "occupied-at-an-index" as the mark's interior constituting a genuine standpoint — a selecting perspective, a "here" with modal weight (the event could not occur as conferral without there being a side occupied). The defense claims this is extracted from the occurrence's own structure rather than imported. Here the dilemma bites.

The Spencer-Brown calculus is a formal language. Its primitives — marked state, void, the cross — are defined within the calculus. When the defense writes "Arriving at a side = occupying it" (Step 3), it treats the formal interior of a Spencer-Brown expression as the physical interior of a real-world event. That equation is not established; it is assumed. The conceptual-formal structure of the calculus carries the thick index by construction — that is what the calculus is for. But the physical event of, say, a retinal cell responding differentially to wavelengths is a physical event. That it implements something we describe as selection-against-alternatives is a description we impose. The thick structure (mark, interior, standpoint) belongs to the description, not demonstrably to the event independent of description.

### Where the defense's claimed escape fails

The defense invokes the Aristotelian elenctic move: you cannot signify "conferral-without-standpoint" because signification IS drawing a distinction. This establishes forcing at the level of discourse and speech acts. Anyone who utters or conceptually deploys "conferral" thereby enacts an index — at saṃvrti, as the defender correctly limits the claim. But the physicalist's target question is prior to and independent of discourse: does the physical sorting-event carry thick-index structure antecedently to any description? The elenctic proof does not touch this. It shows the denier cannot coherently deny in discourse; it does not show the physical occurrence has thick intrinsic structure.

### The precise location of the failure

The bridge argument is valid within the conceptual-formal register. "Conferral" analytically requires a mark; the mark analytically requires an interior; the interior just is an index. This is a clean conceptual entailment. The argument does not presuppose the index as a premise — it derives it from the concept's content. So the presupposition verdict is "unclear" rather than straightforwardly "presupposes": the failure is not that the index is assumed but that a further assumption — register-transparency — is required and never justified.

Register-transparency: the assumption that what holds by conceptual-formal necessity within the Spencer-Brown/Shannon framework holds of the physical event it purports to describe. Without register-transparency, the extraction of the index from the concept yields a result about what "conferral" means in our vocabulary, not about what the physical occurrence is in itself. The thought-to-being crossing that the physicalist flags is not in the argument's surface logic — it is in this background assumption. The defense performs it silently in Step 3 and Step 5, asserting that "the index IS the mark's interior" as if the formal and physical registers were the same register.

### What the defense actually earns

- At the level of conceptual analysis: the argument shows that "conferral" and "index-occupied" are co-extensive by meaning. Genuine result; not trivial.
- At the level of speech-act / conventional forcing: the elenctic move establishes that any enactment of denial is itself an instantiation of the thing denied. Genuine result; corresponds to FORCES-CONVENTIONALLY.
- At the level of brute physical occurrence independent of description: nothing is established. The register-transparency assumption goes unjustified and is exactly the thought-to-being crossing the lens demands be closed.

### Summary

The defense reaches FORCES-CONVENTIONALLY and stops there — not because the Madhyamaka concession forces that ceiling (the Madhyamaka objection is a separate lens), but because the physicalist dilemma forces it independently. The thick index is real within the conceptual-formal register; it does not transmit to the physical occurrence without a premise the defense does not supply. The disjunction (C)/(Z) that makes the seam exhaustive is exhaustive within the formal calculus and within the register of discourse, but not demonstrably so at the level of brute physical events described thinly. The forcing argument is bounded to the conventional level from both sides of the objector pair — the Madhyamaka and the physicalist converge on the same ceiling by different routes.
