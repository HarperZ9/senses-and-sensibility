The bridge argument (Steps 1–6) presupposes the index and does not survive the circularity test. Step 7 independently reaches a genuine conventional forcing result but does not salvage the bridge.

**Where the index enters.** Step 4 defines actualization as "the collapse of indeterminate potency into a specific act." The word "collapse" encodes directionality: there is a vertex the act lands at, a THIS-position already baked into the Aristotelian definition. Step 6 then replies to the ownerless-occurrence objection by saying a "dependently-arisen determination still occupies the contrast-vertex relationally." This substitutes "occupies" for "co-arises" without argument. The Madhyamika grants co-arising and denies occupation — occupation just is the asymmetric THIS-positioning they reject. Relabeling it "relational occupation" does not dissolve the asymmetry; a relational THIS-side is still a THIS-side.

**Why this is presupposition, not derivation.** The defense needed to show that symmetric co-arising is incoherent without a vertex — i.e., that "indexed-at-nothing occurrence" is self-undermining from premises the opponent already grants. Instead the framework definition supplies the vertex as part of what "actualization" means, and the argument then unpacks that definition. Any interlocutor who accepts the Aristotelian act/potency framework will find the inference natural; the Madhyamika opponent rejects the framework's basic asymmetry structure and has not been given a reason to accept it from their own commitments.

**What Step 7 earns.** The conventional forcing argument is structurally different and genuinely stronger: the denier's own enacting act is real at samvrti, and at that level it has the contrast-structure Step 5 describes. This does not invoke act/potency; it invokes what the Madhyamika already grants at the conventional level. That is a real result. But it is the ceiling, not a vindication of the bridge. P1 as stated is a general entailment ("any act of conferral... is performed as this discrimination... indexed at a standpoint") — the conventional forcing argument establishes that the denier cannot coherently deny the index at samvrti, not that the index is entailed in the stronger sense the original P1 requires across all levels.

**Verdict.** The bridge presupposes the index. The defense reaches FORCES-CONVENTIONALLY as its honest ceiling. The contribution is real but bounded: the seam (conferral entails a contrast-vertex) is demonstrated at the conventional level; the ultimate-level claim concedes to Madhyamaka on its own terms.
