<!-- lens: Circularity auditor: does the defense demonstrate the incoherence of "indexed-at-nothing occurrence" without already assuming the index? -->

The defense does not survive the circularity audit.

**Where the index enters.** Step 2 imports it via Husserl's Nullpunkt; Step 4 seals it with the internal-form claim ("no thinner sense of 'the act occurs' strips givenness out"). Both moves are internal to the Husserlian-Henrian framework. Neither is derived from anything the Madhyamaka opponent concedes prior to the argument.

**What the opponent actually grants.** The Madhyamaka objector grants: a dependently-arisen event occurs, conventionally designated "conferral," spatiotemporally located in the thin sense every event is. That is the full extent of her pre-argument commitments. She does not grant that conventional designations analytically carry phenomenological structure; she grants only that they are empty prajnaptis applied to streams of dependent arising.

**Why the internal-form claim fails the audit.** The claim is: there is no thinner sense of "the act occurs" that strips givenness out. This is true given the Husserlian analysis of acts. It is not true given only the opponent's vocabulary of conventional designation + dependent arising. The defense has not shown that "conferral" as the opponent uses it — a conventional label on a causal stream — analytically contains from-here. It has shown that "act" as the defense analyzes it contains from-here. Importing that analysis as if it were a conceptual commitment the opponent already holds is question-begging.

**Why the performative argument (Response A) does not rescue the defense.** The argument runs: the denier, in denying the index, enacts something, and enactments are from-somewhere. But "enactments carry from-here structure" is the conclusion under dispute, not a datum the opponent is committed to. She can accept: "I produce a dependently-arisen utterance, spatiotemporally located in the thin sense, empty of any thick phenomenological index." Nothing she has already granted forces her to accept the thick reading. The performative argument establishes a self-referential pressure only against an interlocutor who already operates within a framework that treats acts as phenomenologically indexed — which is precisely what has to be shown.

**Level reached.** The defense reaches no independent demonstration. It demonstrates a conditional: IF one accepts Husserlian phenomenology of acts, THEN conferral is indexed. That conditional may be interesting, but it does not demonstrate the incoherence of ownerless occurrence to an opponent who rejects the antecedent. The "conventional level" claim in the defender's own audit is overstated: earning the index at samvrti would require showing that the conventional concept of "conferral" — as any conventional speaker, including the Madhyamaka, uses it — carries from-here. That has not been shown; only the phenomenologist's own concept of "act" has been shown to carry it. The defense therefore presupposes the index rather than earning it, and the seam collapses to pratityasamutpada under a Husserlian redescription.
