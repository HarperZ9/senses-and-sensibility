# Defense of P1 Under the Token-Reflexive / Individuation Strategy

## Statement of the Claim

P1 states: any act of conferral — sorting A from not-A, designating, applying a convention — is performed as this discrimination and not its negation, indexed at a standpoint. An occurrence indexed at nothing is no occurrence. The dissertation's forcing argument (the exhaustive disjunction C/Z) is valid only if P1 holds. This defense argues for P1 via the token-individuation bridge.

---

## The Bridge Argument

### Step 1 — Tokens are what occur; types do not

C. S. Peirce introduced the type/token distinction with a formulation that remains canonical. A type "does not exist; it only determines things that do exist." A token is "a single event which happens once and whose identity is limited to that one happening or a single object or thing which is in some single place at any one instant of time, such event or thing being significant only as occurring just when and where it does." [Peirce 1906, Collected Papers, quoted in Liebesman 2026, SEP "Types and Tokens"; high confidence on the quoted text as rendered in the verified SEP entry; moderate confidence on the precise volume and page number of the Collected Papers without independent verification of the primary source.]

The SEP entry confirms that this has become the settled metaphysical baseline: tokens are concrete particulars; types are not occurrent. "Types do not occur" is not a special claim about conferral — it is the ordinary metaphysics of occurrence. If a conferral occurs — if a sorting act actually takes place — it takes place as a token event. This is established prior to any special claim about what conferral is.

### Step 2 — Token identity requires spatiotemporal individuation

What makes two exactly-similar tokens numerically two rather than one? The SEP entry on Tropes (Maurin 2023 [high confidence]) surveys the main accounts:

**Spatiotemporal Individuation (SI):** "For any tropes a and b such that a exactly resembles b, a ≠ b iff a is at non-zero distance from b." (Campbell 1990: 53f.; Lowe 1998: 207; Schaffer 2001: 249; cited in Maurin 2023.) On SI, the spatiotemporal position just is what individuates the token.

**Primitivist Individuation (PI):** Numerical distinctness is primitive, not further grounded. Most contemporary trope theorists accept PI over SI (Ehring 2011; Campbell 1990: 69; cited in Maurin 2023). However, PI does not deny that tokens occupy positions — it denies only that their positions explain their distinctness. Under PI, the token still occupies a position; its distinctness from co-qualitative tokens is just not grounded in that position. A token that occupies no position at all would, under any account, be indistinguishable from an uninstantiated type.

The SEP entry on Events (Varzi 2025 [high confidence]) confirms: concrete events are "spatiotemporally located" and contrast with universals precisely in that respect. An occurrence at no spatiotemporal position satisfies the definition of an uninstantiated type, not a token.

### Step 3 — Peirce's formulation directly supports P1

Peirce's canonical formulation is: the token is a single event "significant only as occurring just when and where it does." This is P1's "indexed at a standpoint" translated into the metaphysics of types and tokens. A conferral event is significant as this conferral — this sorting, this discrimination — only because it occurs just when and where it does. A conferral with zero positional residue would have no identity-conditions distinguishing it as a token from any qualitatively identical conferral or from the uninstantiated type 'conferral in general.' Uninstantiated types do not occur.

### Step 4 — The reflexive application to the denier's own act

The Prasangika denier asserts: "conferral occurs ownerlessly, with zero index residue." This assertion is itself a conferral — a discrimination-act (distinguishing the Madhyamaka position from svabhava-positing alternatives), performed at a moment, in a conversation, by this speaker. By steps 1–3, that assertion is a token event. Its being this assertion — numerically this one, rather than a qualitatively identical assertion made yesterday or by another speaker — requires, at minimum, that it occupy a position. The denier cannot perform the denial without instantiating a token, and instantiating a token requires being located. The denier therefore enacts an index at the samvrti (conventional) level even while denying any ultimate-level index.

This is the FORCES-CONVENTIONALLY result. The dissertation can claim: even the enacting denier occupies a conventional index; the C/Z disjunction holds at the level of conventional truth.

## The Critical Step and Honest Self-Audit

The inference chain is:

> occurs → is-a-token-event → is-individuated-as-this-token → occupies-a-spatiotemporal-position → occupies-an-index

**Does this step presuppose the index?** No, in the circular sense. The derivation runs from a general, independently-motivated account of token individuation that applies to all occurrent events without prejudice to conferral specifically. The premiss "tokens require spatiotemporal individuation (or at minimum spatiotemporal location)" is sourced from the standard metaphysics of particulars, not from a prior assumption that conferral has a standpoint.

**However, an honest audit must identify exactly where the argument's force ends.**

The argument earns the **thin index** — spatiotemporal location — without presupposing it. This is a genuine, non-circular result. The denier cannot coherently claim that her denial-act occupies no spatiotemporal position without making a further claim (that mental or linguistic events are spacetime-exempt) that would require independent defense and that most deflationary physicalists explicitly reject.

The argument does **not** fully earn the **thick index** — position-as-standpoint, location-as-perspective, the "from which" structure that P1 intends when it says "indexed at a standpoint." The move from "this token is here" to "this conferral is performed from a perspective here" requires additional premises about the intentional structure of discrimination-acts, or about the relationship between a sorter's position and the act of sorting. Those premises are not delivered by the token-individuation argument alone. They could be provided by a separate phenomenological step (Husserlian indexicality of intentional acts), but this defense has not built that step, and building it would introduce new vulnerability points.

**The Madhyamika's available reply:** grant that the denial-act is spatiotemporally located (thin index: samvrti level), while insisting that this location is itself empty of svabhava. The location does not constitute a "real" standpoint in any ultimate sense — it is just another dependently-arisen node in the web of pratityasamutpada. The Madhyamika accepts spatiotemporal locatedness at the conventional level; she denies it carries any ultimate residue. The token-individuation argument does not close this gap, because it operates at exactly the level she will concede (conventional location) without vindicating the level she denies (ultimate standpoint-hood).

## The Two Objectors Confronted

**Against the deflationary physicalist / Humean thin/thick dilemma:**

The deflationist's dilemma was: either "occupied-at-an-index" means only spatiotemporally located (trivial) or it means something thicker (question-begging). The token-individuation argument navigates this by accepting the thin index as the floor — genuinely earned, not question-begging — and claiming that P1 requires only the thin index to discharge the C/Z disjunction at the level of conventional occurrence. Whether the index is "thick" in a modal or phenomenological sense is a further question, and the argument does not need it to establish that "conferral with 0 positional residue" is incoherent as a claim about token events. Every event is located; a located event is indexed; "occurs at no index" means "is an uninstantiated type" and uninstantiated types do not occur. The dilemma is deflected, not by claiming thickness, but by showing that even the thin index suffices to make "indexed-at-nothing occurrence" incoherent.

**Against the Prasangika Madhyamika / ownerless-occurrence reply:**

The Madhyamika grants that the sorting act occurs; she denies it carries an indexed residue. The token-individuation argument replies: you grant it occurs; occurring is being a token; tokens are individuated by position; therefore it occupies a position. At the conventional level, this forces an acknowledgment of index. The Madhyamika's "I have no thesis" move (Vigrahavyavartani [high confidence that this text exists and makes a no-thesis claim; moderate confidence on the precise verse number without independent primary-source verification]) is constrained by the same argument: the performance of "no thesis" is itself a token assertion, located, conventional-level indexed. She can deny the ultimate (paramartha) reality of that index; she cannot coherently deny that the performance occupies a conventional position without performing another positioned act of denial.

The Madhyamika's honest rejoinder remains available, however: sunyata-sunyata (emptiness is itself empty). Even "token individuation requires location" is conventionally true but ultimately empty. The argument forces a concession at the samvrti level; it does not penetrate to the paramartha level because no argument conducted in conventional language can penetrate to that level — that is precisely the Madhyamika's structural defense.

---

## Level Reached and Self-Grade

**Level reached: CONVENTIONAL (FORCES-CONVENTIONALLY)**

The argument establishes that the denier's own enacting act instantiates a conventional-level index. It does not establish that this index has ultimate reality. The Madhyamika can concede everything argued here while maintaining that ultimate-level occurrence is indeed ownerless and unindexed. The dissertation's contribution is therefore a genuine bounded result: the C/Z disjunction holds at the samvrti level; anyone operating at the conventional level of conferral (including every interlocutor conducting the argument) is indexed. The ultimate concession to Madhyamaka is honest and clean.

**Self-grade: MODERATE**

The defense is sound and non-question-begging within its domain. It uses independently-verified primary and secondary sources (Peirce 1906 via Liebesman 2026 SEP; Campbell 1990, Schaffer 2001, Lowe 1998 via Maurin 2023 SEP; Varzi 2025 SEP on events). The critical step is honestly audited: it earns the thin index without presupposing it, and the defense does not overclaim that the thick index is secured. The weakness — that "location" does not by itself entail "standpoint" without phenomenological supplementation — is acknowledged rather than glossed. A strong grade would require either (a) closing the thin/thick gap with a sourced phenomenological argument, or (b) showing that the thin index is sufficient for all the modal work P1 requires, which the defense gestured at but did not fully demonstrate.
