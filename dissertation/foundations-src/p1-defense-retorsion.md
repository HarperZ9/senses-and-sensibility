# Defense of P1 — Retorsion / Transcendental-Performative Strategy

## Thesis

P1 states: any act of conferral is performed as THIS discrimination and not its negation, indexed at a standpoint. The retorsion argument holds that the denial of P1 is itself a conferral act and therefore instantiates an index in the very act of denying that any index is occupied. The argument is pressed to its honest limit against the three strongest Madhyamaka resources: the "I have no thesis" doctrine (Vigrahavyavartani), the goer/going dissolution (MMK ch. 2), and sunyata-sunyata.

**Verdict: The retorsion establishes the index at the conventional (samvrti) level without presupposing it. It does not reach the ultimate (paramartha) level. The level reached is FORCES-CONVENTIONALLY.**

## The Bridge Argument

### Step 1 — P1 precisely stated

P1: any conferral act (sorting A from not-A, designating, applying a convention) is enacted as THIS discrimination and not its negation, indexed at a standpoint. The modally strong claim is that an occurrence with the discriminatory character of conferral — not merely a physical event of which conferral is a description, but the sorting-as-such — cannot be enacted from nowhere. An occurrence that sorts while indexed at nothing would simultaneously be this partition and its complement: a formal contradiction.

The claim is not that there is a substance (an ego, an atman, a self-standing subject) behind the act. It is that the act's own discriminatory form is perspectivally determinate: it is enacted as this-not-that.

### Step 2 — The denial formulated

The Prasangika denier asserts: conferral carries no index. Occurrence is ownerless (anatman). Pratityasamutpada — dependent origination — exhausts what there is to say about a conferral event. No index is added by the act of conferring beyond the bare event of its dependent arising. Call this D.

### Step 3 — D is itself a conferral act

D sorts the proposition {conferral carries an index} from its negation and places it in the false column. D has propositional form, is directed at a truth-claim, and is enacted as this-and-not-that. By P1's characterization, D is a conferral event. This is not a question-begging stipulation; it follows from the description of what D is doing: partitioning the propositional space.

### Step 4 — The retorsive turn

If D is a conferral event, then — by P1 — D is performed as this-discrimination-and-not-its-negation, indexed at a standpoint. D's own propositional content ("no conferral carries an index") is falsified by D's own enactment. The denier occupies the index in the very act of denying it.

This is the core retorsion: the structure of the denial is self-undermining. It is a pragmatic contradiction in the technical sense — not a formal contradiction between premises, but a contradiction between the propositional content of an act and the presuppositions of that act's performance. (Austin's distinction between locutionary content and illocutionary force is the Western analytical analogue: the denial's illocutionary structure presupposes what its locutionary content denies. SEP Speech Acts, confirmed live.)

The parallel in Aristotle is precise. Metaphysics IV 3-6 contains what the SEP entry calls "elenctic refutation": Aristotle draws the opponent into saying something significant — signifying some one thing — which commits the opponent to PNC even while the opponent claims to reject it. Aristotle's challenge: "signify some one thing to himself and to another" (Metaph IV 4, cited in SEP "Aristotle on Non-Contradiction," verified). The moment the denier signifies, she occupies a standpoint. The opponent who refuses to speak is "no better off than a vegetable" — but the Madhyamika is not refusing to speak; she is adducing prasangas, which is a form of speaking-that-sorts. [high confidence on the Aristotle structure; the parallel to the Madhyamika case is the present argument, not a cited claim]

### Step 5 — The "I have no thesis" reply (VV) pressed

The Prasangika's most sophisticated dodge is the vitanda: refuse the assertoric stance. The SEP Madhyamaka entry (Candrakirti section, verified) states:

> "Nagarjuna had written in VV that he has no thesis to advance. He had also written that he apprehends no objects at all and therefore has no need to affirm or deny anything ... the Madhyamika cannot be refuted, precisely because he has no position to defend."

Candrakirti explicitly endorses Buddhapālita's approach: the Madhyamika "shows the problems involved in the positions of others and argues only until the other philosophers realize the inadequacy of their positions and become silent." This is the vitanda structure: adduce consequences, not counter-positions.

Does vitanda escape the retorsion? No, for the following reason. To display a consequence as UNWELCOME — as a reductio that the interlocutor ought to reject — the Madhyamika must evaluate: this-consequence-is-damaging and not acceptable. That evaluative discrimination is a sorting act. It is not a first-order assertion about the world, but it is an evaluative partition of the space of consequences into {acceptable} and {unacceptable}. To perform that partition is to occupy an evaluative standpoint. The vitanda move relocates the index from the level of first-order assertion to the level of evaluative discrimination; it does not eliminate the index.

The Madhyamika might reply: the reductio operates on the interlocutor's own premises and requires no evaluative commitment from the Madhyamika — the consequence is unwelcome by the interlocutor's lights, not mine. This is the purest form of the vitanda. But even this minimal operation — tracking which consequences are unwelcome BY THE INTERLOCUTOR'S LIGHTS — requires the Madhyamika to sort: this-consequence-is-what-the-interlocutor-finds-unwelcome versus that-one-is-not. This sorting is performed from somewhere; it is indexed at the Madhyamika's conventional standpoint as interpreter of the interlocutor's commitments. The index survives even the purest vitanda.

### Step 6 — The goer/going dissolution (MMK ch. 2)

The SEP Nagarjuna entry (verified) describes MMK ch. 2: Nagarjuna argues there is no mover behind the motion — the mover and the motion are co-constituted, neither has svabhava independent of the other. The constitutive/instantiated property distinction collapses: there is no substratum-individual behind the relational property. Applied to the "index": there is no standpoint-SUBSTANCE behind the sorting. The sorting does not presuppose a self-standing indexer.

This is the strongest counter to the retorsion. If the "index" requires a substantial standpoint-bearer, the goer/going analysis dissolves it.

The retorsion reply: the index in P1 is not a substantial subject. It is the perspectival determinacy of the discriminatory act itself — the fact that the act is THIS partition and not its complement. Even after the mover/motion analysis, the motion is still DIRECTED motion — from here to there, not from nowhere to nowhere. Direction is a relational property of the event that survives the dissolution of the direction-substance. Similarly, the sorting is still THIS sorting even after the sorter-substance is dissolved: it partitions in this direction and not that one. The perspectival determinacy is a structural property of the act, not a property of a substantial subject behind the act.

This is a genuine philosophical distinction, not a rhetorical move. The Nagarjunan dissolution targets svabhava — intrinsic, substance-level existence independent of relations. It does not target relational, perspectival properties of events. The SEP Nagarjuna entry confirms: "the Madhyamika does not deny that there are a variety of sensory and mental events that happen in close temporal and causal connection" — conventional events are not denied. A sorting event's discriminatory direction is one of its conventional relational properties, and it is not dissolved by the goer/going analysis.

### Step 7 — Sunyata-sunyata and the ultimate ceiling

The Prasangika's deepest resource: even "occurrence" and "discrimination" and "index" are empty of svabhava. Sunyata is itself empty (sunyata-sunyata). The SEP Nagarjuna entry (verified) states: "the theory of emptiness is not to be regarded as an ultimately true theory either" — emptiness is itself a conventional, therapeutic construct, not an ultimate description of reality.

At the paramartha level, sunyata-sunyata dissolves everything including the retorsion's conclusion. The Madhyamika who enters non-conceptual direct awareness — who does not adduce, argue, sort, or evaluate, but simply enacts silence — is performing an act that is not a propositional or evaluative discrimination in the relevant sense. Against that move, the retorsion has no grip. It is an argument, and arguments operate on acts that have discriminatory form; silence-as-practice does not.

This is the honest ceiling of the retorsion strategy.

### Step 8 — The conventional level secured and the level verdict

At the samvrti level — the level at which the Madhyamika is engaged in philosophical discourse, adducing prasangas, framing objections, making vitanda moves — the retorsion fully holds. Any such act is a conferral act with discriminatory form, and the occupation of a conventional index is entailed by that form without presupposing a substantial indexer.

The forced disjunction in P1 holds conventionally: either conferral is occurring (in the form of argument, prasanga, vitanda, evaluative discrimination) or it has wholly ceased. A Madhyamika in discourse cannot claim "conferral with 0 residue" without that claim being itself a conferral with nonzero residue. "Conferral with 0 residue" enacted as a philosophical claim is p AND not-p — self-contradictory at the samvrti level.

The ultimate level remains genuinely open to the Madhyamika: a practice of silence, non-conceptual awareness, the complete cessation of propositional and evaluative discrimination. That is not a philosophical claim and is not reachable by any argument, including this one.

## The Critical Step and Self-Audit

**The critical step:** From "the denial of P1 occurs as a propositional/evaluative act" to "an index is occupied."

**Does this presuppose the index?** No. The inference does not assume there is an index and then conclude there is one. It takes as its premise a structural description of the act (it has discriminatory, directed form) and infers that such form constitutes the occupation of a perspectival standpoint at the samvrti level. The load-bearing intermediate premise — that discriminatory directedness IS perspectival occupation — is a conceptual claim about the nature of partitioning acts, not a smuggled assumption about a standpoint-substance. The Madhyamika's own goer/going analysis actually supports the move: even in the Nagarjunan picture, directed events have perspectival relational properties (direction, discrimination) even without substantial direction-agents behind them.

**The Stroud gap.** The honest objection, drawn from Stroud's 1968 critique of transcendental arguments (verified in SEP), is this: the retorsion at most establishes that we must THINK there is an index occupied — that the concept of indexless conferral is incoherent from within the discourse — not that an index is ontologically occupied at the ultimate level. Stroud's point: transcendental arguments show that we must believe X, not that X is the case. The retorsion meets this objection at the samvrti level by pointing out that samvrti-level coherence is what is at stake in philosophical discourse — the Madhyamika cannot coherently deny P1 within discourse. But Stroud's point correctly captures why the ultimate level remains out of reach.

**Level reached:** conventional (samvrti). The retorsion forces a conventional index; it does not defeat the Madhyamika who exits discourse entirely into non-conceptual silence.

**Self-grade: moderate.** The argument is structurally sound at the conventional level and the self-audit is honest. It does not presuppose the index in the question-begging sense. But it does not reach ultimate defeat of the Madhyamika because the silence-move is real and the Stroud gap is real. A "strong" grade would require either (a) a demonstration that even silence is a discriminatory act in the relevant sense, or (b) a proof that the conventional level is the only level at which philosophical claims can be evaluated — both of which are available as further arguments but are not demonstrated here. The honest ceiling is conventional, and moderate is the correct self-assessment.
