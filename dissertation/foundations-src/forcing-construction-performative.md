## Construction 3 — The Performative Floor, Pushed to Its Limit

### 3.1 The route stated at full strength

The performative route does not argue *about* the residue; it tries to catch the residue in the act of being denied. Take the strongest possible denial — the Madhyamaka ultimate claim itself: *nothing has svabhava; the dissolution goes all the way to 0; there is no privileged residue.* The performative move is to observe that this claim is a **sorting**: it partitions the field into {has-svabhava} and {empty}, and consigns everything, including itself, to the second cell. A partition is not free-floating. It is performed — there is an act of distinguishing 0 from not-0, ultimate from conventional, residue-asserted from residue-denied. Call the locus of that act the seam. The argument's wager: *you cannot assert "there is no seam" except at a seam.* The denial instantiates the thing denied. This is the Apel–Habermas structure of the **performative contradiction** (Apel 1987; Habermas, *Moral Consciousness and Communicative Action*, 1990) and behind it the Cartesian ur-form: the *cogito* is undeniable not because "I exist" is a self-evident proposition but because the *act* of doubting it re-supplies its truth-condition (Descartes, *Meditation* II; Hintikka's 1962 "performance" reading, **high confidence** on the attribution). The seam is offered as the *cogito* of conferral: whatever else dissolves, the dissolving-act occupies a standpoint.

If that held, it would do exactly what the dissertation needs: force a non-zero locus as the *form* of any conferral (including the conferral of the judgment "0"), conceding that the *content* "0" may be perfectly correct about everything the seam sorts, while denying that the sorting itself can be one of the sorted items without remainder.

### 3.2 Nagarjuna's reply, taken at its strongest

This is where the route is supposed to die, and we must let it nearly die. Nagarjuna anticipated the self-refutation charge two millennia early. In the *Vigrahavyavartani* (vv. 1–4 pose it; vv. 21–29 answer it), the objector says: if all things are empty, your words are empty, and an empty word cannot negate anything — so your thesis self-destructs. Nagarjuna's answer is verse 29: **"If I had any thesis (pratijña), that fault would attach to me. But I have no thesis (nasti ca mama pratijña), therefore there is no fault for me"** (Westerhoff 2010, translation and commentary, **high confidence**). The negation of svabhava is not the *assertion* of a counter-entity ("emptiness") that would then need its own svabhava; it is purely the withdrawal of a misattribution. Hence *sunyata-sunyata*: emptiness is itself empty, the prajnapti "emptiness" is no more self-standing than anything else, and — crucially — it is **self-purging**, the medicine that voids itself with the disease (MMK 13.8: "Those for whom emptiness is itself a view (drsti) are called incurable," **high confidence**; Garfield 1995 ad loc.).

Read against the performative argument, the reply is precise and devastating in its first pass. The performative charge says: *your act of asserting enacts a self-standing seam.* Nagarjuna says: it enacts no such thing, because (i) the **content** is the mere removal of an imputation, which posits nothing, and (ii) the **act**, like every act, is itself empty, dependently-arisen, occupied by nothing with svabhava. There is no standpoint here, only the conventional, momentary, ownerless functioning of a pointing-finger. To say "you are *at* a seam" is, on the two-truths scheme (samvrti/paramartha), to make a **conventional** claim, which Madhyamaka cheerfully grants — and then to illicitly promote it to a **paramartha** claim about a forced ultimate residue, which is exactly the reification the whole system exists to dissolve. The two-truths doctrine is built to absorb this objection: *yes, there is a functioning locus, conventionally; no, it is not an ultimate, forced, non-empty floor.* The performative argument, on this first pass, has proven only that **discourse has a conventional structure** — which Madhyamaka already says — and has not touched the ultimate.

That is the verdict's "undeniability-in-discourse, not existence," restated in Sanskrit. We have to beat it or concede it.

### 3.3 The one real lever: content versus act

The only crack in the reply is the move the task names: **distinguish the content of the assertion from the act of asserting.** Grant Nagarjuna everything about content. The proposition "everything is empty, including this proposition" is, I accept, contentually self-consistent under the self-purging reading; it imputes nothing; verse 29 holds *for the content*. The performative argument must therefore not target content at all. It targets the **event**: that there is a sorting-occurrence, a *this-not-that* being enacted, here, now. And here Nagarjuna's second defense — "the act too is empty, dependently arisen, ownerless" — is doing different work than it appears to.

Notice what "the act is empty" denies and what it does not deny. It denies that the act has **svabhava**: own-being, independence, a self-standing essence. It does *not*, and cannot, deny that the act **occurs**. Dependent-arising (pratityasamutpada) is precisely *occurrence-without-svabhava*; emptiness is not non-occurrence but the *manner* of occurrence (MMK 24.18: "What is dependently arisen, that we call emptiness," **high confidence**). So when the performative argument says "a seam is forced," the question is whether "seam" names **svabhava** (in which case Nagarjuna rightly refuses it) or names **the bare occurrence of a sorting** (in which case Nagarjuna has *already granted* it under the name pratityasamutpada). The dissertation's entire bet rides on this fork, and it must say, without flinching, which horn it takes.

It must take the second. And taking it has an immediate, brutal cost that the dissertation has to absorb on the page: **on this horn, the "residual structural aseity" is not aseity.** Aseity is *a-se*, from-itself, self-standing — the very thing Nagarjuna denies and the very thing the seam, *qua* dependently-arisen occurrence, does not have. The seam occurs *ab alio*, conferred, owned by nothing. So if Construction 3 succeeds, it succeeds by **renaming**: what is forced is not a residual *aseity* but a residual *seity* in the thinnest possible sense — a forced **locus-of-occurrence**, non-zero because it occurs, but with zero own-being. The honest title of the result is not "residual structural aseity"; it is "the forced form of dependent conferral." (This is why §3.6 must ask whether anything survives that Construction 1 did not already own.)

### 3.4 Where the salvage actually lands — and why it is not yet a forcing of *existence*

Run the lever to its end. The performative argument, restricted to the act, now says: *the denial-of-residue is an occurring sorting; an occurring sorting is non-zero; therefore at the moment of denial, not-0.* Is this a forcing of the seam's **existence**, or still only its **undeniability**?

Be exact. What is genuinely forced is a **biconditional indexed to the act**: *wherever a conferral/sorting occurs, a >0 locus occurs* — because the sorting *is* the locus's occurring; they are not two things, one grounding the other, but one event under two descriptions. This is not the transcendental smuggle Stroud warned of. Stroud's objection ("Transcendental Arguments," *Journal of Philosophy* 1968, **high confidence**) is that such arguments establish at most that *we must believe* or *must conceptualize* P, not that P, unless backed by an unargued verificationism; the gap is between the necessity-of-the-concept and the truth-of-the-thing. The salvage slips *under* Stroud's gap precisely **by refusing to be about a state**: it does not say "the seam must exist for conferral to be possible" (a state-to-state conditional, exactly Stroud's target). It says "the conferral-event and the seam-occurrence are the same event." There is no inference from concept to world to be challenged, because there is no inference — only an identity-of-occurrence. *That* is the cash value of the dissertation's "relocation to seams/relations not states" (Construction's specific #1): it is what lets the performative argument duck Stroud, because Stroud's wedge needs two terms to drive between.

But — and this is the limit, stated against the dissertation's own interest — the identity is **only co-extensive with conferral-events**. It forces not-0 *exactly when and where* there is conferral, and says nothing the instant conferral ceases. So the result is a **conditional, locally-indexed floor**, not an unconditional one. And that is, read correctly, not a defeat: it is *precisely the disjunctive forcing* the dissertation wanted (#3). The exhaustive disjunction — *either conferral (→ seam, >0) or cessation (→ 0)* — is now earned rather than asserted, because the performative argument has shown the left disjunct's seam is **non-optional within conferral** (you cannot perform the denial without performing a sorting), while conceding the right disjunct (total cessation of sorting = the Madhyamaka ultimate, nirvana-adjacent) is a genuine, untouched 0. The seam is forced as the **form of the conventional**, full stop. It does not reach into the ultimate, and it should not pretend to.

So: does the performative route push *past* the verdict's "undeniability-not-existence" limit? **Honest answer: no — and yes, on a technicality that must be stated as a technicality.** It does not deliver the existence of a *standing* entity (there is none; that was always reification). It delivers the existence of an *occurrence* whenever there is conferral, which is "existence" only in the thinnest, event-indexed, own-being-free sense. Calling that a breach of the limit would be exactly the overclaim the hostile examiner is waiting for. The correct claim is narrower and survivable: **the verdict's limit is real for any state-reading of the seam, and the only thing that survives it is the relocation to events — which buys not "existence simpliciter" but "non-zero occurrence, co-extensive with conferral."**

### 3.5 Priest's dialetheism: does embracing the contradiction help or hurt?

Graham Priest (*Beyond the Limits of Thought*, 2nd ed. 2002, ch. on Nagarjuna; and Garfield & Priest, "Nagarjuna and the Limits of Thought," *Philosophy East and West* 2003, **high confidence**) reads Nagarjuna as a **dialetheist at the limit**: the ultimate truth is that there is no ultimate truth; the nature of all things is to have no nature; these are not sloppy paradoxes to be explained away but *true contradictions* sitting exactly on the boundary that thought tries to cross and cannot. The "inclosure schema" (Russell-/Cantor-style: a totality, a diagonalizing operation that is both inside and outside it) is, on Priest's reading, instantiated by emptiness itself.

Does this help the forcing argument or hurt it? **It cuts both ways, and the dissertation must claim only the half it can hold.**

- **It hurts the easy version.** If Nagarjuna is *allowed* true contradictions at the limit, then the performative-contradiction charge loses its sting *as a charge*. "You contradict yourself by asserting there is no thesis" is no longer a *reductio* for a dialetheist; it is a feature. Priest hands Nagarjuna a principled way to say: *yes, the act both is and is not a self-standing seam; both, at the limit; this is the expected shape of limit-discourse, not a refutation.* So the route to forcing the seam **via classical reductio is closed** the moment dialetheism is on the table. Any version of Construction 3 that depends on "contradiction therefore your view collapses" is dead against Priest. The dissertation should kill that version itself, in print, before the examiner does.

- **It helps the relocated version — but only by conceding the frame.** Here is the subtle point. Priest's inclosure requires a **diagonalizer**: an operation that produces the object both in and out of the totality. That operation is *performed*. The contradiction is a contradiction *at* a boundary, and a boundary is not nothing — set-theoretically, the diagonal element is a perfectly definite object, the very thing whose definiteness drives the paradox. **The empty set is not nothing** (∅ is a *set*, an object, with a singleton {∅} ≠ ∅; this is the cleanest model of "near-nothing that is not nothing," and it is exact, not metaphor — **high confidence**). So even when we follow Priest all the way to the true contradiction at the limit, the contradiction is *located*: it has the form ⟨in, out⟩ at a boundary the diagonalizer occupies in the act of diagonalizing. Dialetheism does not erase the seam; **it relocates the seam to the boundary and then certifies, in its own formalism, that the boundary is occupied** (that is what makes the limit a *limit* rather than an open edge). The forcing argument can take this: *fine, the contradiction is true; but a true contradiction at a boundary is still a boundary, and the boundary is the seam.* What the argument **cannot** take is any further claim that this boundary is self-standing or ultimate — Priest's whole point is that it is the limit of thought, occupied by the act of thinking the limit, which is once more **conferral, occupied, ab alio.**

The net: **dialetheism removes the performative argument's reductio teeth and simultaneously supplies a formal model in which the seam reappears as an occupied boundary.** That is not a victory for forcing-by-contradiction; it is a redirection back onto the *occupation* claim. Which is the result we must now confront head-on.

### 3.6 The honest verdict: does Construction 3 add anything Construction 1 does not?

This is the question the task insists I not dodge, so here it is without cushioning.

**Largely, no.** Every place where Construction 3 *succeeds*, it succeeds by collapsing into Construction 1 (Conferral Is Occupation, And You Cannot Occupy 0). Trace it: the content/act distinction (§3.3) survives Nagarjuna only by retreating from "content" to "the occurring act"; an *occurring act* is an **occupied standpoint**; that is Construction 1's premise verbatim. The Stroud-evasion (§3.4) works by identifying the conferral-event with the seam-occurrence — an *event*, *occupied*; Construction 1 again. The Priest analysis (§3.5) dissolves the reductio and pushes the seam back onto the occupied boundary — Construction 1 a third time. The performative apparatus (Descartes, Apel, Habermas) turns out to be **scaffolding that delivers the reader to occupation and then has nothing further to add.** Its distinctive resource — *catch the denier in a self-refutation* — is exactly the resource dialetheism neutralizes. Strip the self-refutation, and what remains standing is not "the denial is contradictory" but "the denial is *performed*, and performance is occupation." The word doing the load-bearing is *performed*, and that word belongs to Construction 1.

**What Construction 3 *does* contribute — and it is narrow, real, and worth one paragraph of the dissertation, not a chapter:**

1. **A negative result with teeth:** it *proves the performative route cannot be the load-bearing argument*, because its one unique weapon (reductio-via-performative-contradiction) is disarmed by the strongest available reading of the rival (Priest's dialetheist Nagarjuna). This is worth stating because it forecloses the *obvious* objection an examiner would raise — "isn't your seam just a performative contradiction dressed up?" — by **conceding it and showing the performative framing is the weaker of the two routes.** Pre-empting your own demolition is a contribution to the dissertation's credibility even when it is not a contribution to the thesis.

2. **A sharper statement of what the seam is *not*:** running this route forces the admission in §3.3 that the forced item has **zero own-being** — it is *seity* without *aseity*, a forced locus-of-occurrence, not a residual self-standingness. That admission protects the whole project from the charge of smuggling svabhava back in under a Latin name. It is better to have extracted it here, under maximal pressure, than to have it extracted by the committee.

3. **The hand-off, made explicit:** Construction 3's terminus *is* Construction 1's premise. So this construction's real function in the dissertation's architecture is **to motivate and de-risk Construction 1** — to show that the discourse-level argument everyone reaches for first does not reach existence, and that the only thing it leaves standing when fully pressed is *occupation*. That tells the reader why the weight must shift to the occupation argument, and tells the examiner that the author already walked the performative path to its end and found the floor where Construction 1 says it is.

**Bottom line, labeled.** Confidence that the performative route, *by itself*, forces a non-zero seam at the level of **existence**: **low** — it forces undeniability-within-conferral, which is event-indexed occurrence, not standing existence, and its reductio engine is voided by dialetheism. Confidence that it forces a non-zero seam at the level of the **conventional/transcendental form of any conferral**: **moderate-to-high** — but *only because* it reduces to the occupation claim, i.e., the forcing is really Construction 1's, borrowed. Confidence that Construction 3 adds an *independent* forcing distinct from occupation: **low**. Its honest role is **scaffolding, negative result, and hand-off**, not an independent pillar. If the dissertation needs Construction 3 to carry weight on its own, the examiner's original diagnosis stands and the position should shrink to the membrane identity (authn/authz :: is/ought) as the lone clean result. If Construction 1 carries the weight — and §3.3–3.5 show that *everything load-bearing here is already Construction 1* — then Construction 3 earns its keep precisely by demonstrating that the performative alternative was tried, pushed to its limit, and found to bottom out at occupation. That is the most this route can honestly claim, and claiming more is the exact overreach the verdict was warning against.
