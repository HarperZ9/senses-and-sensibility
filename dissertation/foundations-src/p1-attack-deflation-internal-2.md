## Verdict: P1 does not survive the type-A / Humean lens

### The dilemma applied

The defense's bridge runs: occurrence → tokening (Peirce) → spatiotemporal individuation (SI, Campbell/Schaffer) → occupied-at-index. The two links are claimed to be the deflationist's own commitments, borrowed without addition.

**Link (1) fails under the thin/thick dilemma.**

Peirce's type/token distinction is a distinction about how signs function. Extending it to physical conferral-events requires the event to be identified AS a token of the type "conferral" rather than as a token of "heat dissipation" or "electrochemical gradient" or any other type realized at the same location and time. Nothing in the bare physical occurrence selects the type. The selection is a conceptual act — precisely what the defense is supposed to be establishing, not presupposing.

- THIN: "token" = "spatiotemporally located event." True of every physical event. Does not distinguish conferral-tokens from non-conferral events at the same location. The index earned is the trivial one every physical event already has.
- THICK: "token of conferral" = "this event instantiates the conferral type." Requires the sorting already to have been identified as conferral. Circular relative to P1's task.

**Link (2) is valid but insufficient.**

Grant SI fully: numerically distinct tropes are individuated by spatiotemporal position (Campbell 1990, Schaffer 2001). SI individuates all concrete particulars indiscriminately. At location L, many events are co-occurring; SI distinguishes each from its counterpart at L'. It does not select which of those events is the conferral-event rather than the merely physical substrate-event. The index SI delivers is on the physical particular; it is not the index on the conferral-as-such that the seam requires.

**The modal work the seam requires.**

For C/Z (conferral-occurring vs. conferral-wholly-ceased) to be an exhaustive disjunction that does genuine modal work, "conferral is occurring at L" must be a determinate fact distinct from "some physical event is occurring at L." A type-A physicalist denies there is a further fact: mental/intentional predicates are realized by physical processes, and whether a physical process counts as conferral is not a further fact over the physical — it is a matter of description. So:

- If the defense accepts type-A physicalism: "conferral is occurring" just IS some physical description. The seam is trivial; every physical event token is spatiotemporally indexed; the contribution is pratityasamutpada in Western vocabulary.
- If the defense needs "conferral" to pick out a determinate event-type over the physical: a Humean demands the inference from "we apply 'conferral' here" to "conferral is occurring as such" — and that inference is exactly the thought→being crossing Stroud marks as unlicensed. It reopens the gap inside the predicate.

**Stroud relocated.**

The defense does not close Stroud's gap; it moves it. The original gap: from "we apply the concept C" we cannot infer "C is instantiated." The defense's version: from "this physical event occurs" we cannot infer "conferral is occurring" without the event being identified AS a conferral-token — which requires the concept "conferral" to track a real property. Establishing that real property is the task; the bridge argument assumes it.

### What level the defense reaches

Against the type-A / Humean lens: **none**. The defense does not reach even the conventional level as a stable result, because the seam's C/Z structure, under thin physicalism, collapses to a trivial claim about physical event tokens (every physical event has an index; the seam is just "some physical process vs. no physical process"). The defender's own presupposition audit rated the step "borderline" against the Prasangika; against the Humean, the verdict is sharper — the index earned is the wrong index.

### What would need to be added

A defense that survives this lens would need to establish that "conferral" tracks a determinate event-type whose tokens are not merely physical-process tokens under a description — i.e., a non-reductive account of what makes an event a conferral-event rather than just a physical event we happen to call by that name. That would be a further argument (likely a functionalist individuation principle, or a Kimian argument that mental event types are real), and it is not present in the bridge. Without it, the thin/thick dilemma stands.
