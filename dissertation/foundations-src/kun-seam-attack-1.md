## Kalām Lens Verdict

### The verdict: needs-bounding

Resolution (i) is correct and the kalām lens forces it — but the draft requires four substantive sharpenings before the thesis is internally consistent and adversarially defensible.

### What the kalām argument actually forces

The traversal argument (al-Ghazālī, *Tahāfut*, Discussion 1; Craig, *Kalām Cosmological Argument*, 1979) is the strongest weapon here. An actually infinite past series of events cannot be completed — "traversed from the open end" — to yield a present moment. This is not merely a preference for finitude; it is a claim that a beginningless causal chain generates a present moment only if infinitely many prior events have all occurred, which requires completing an actual infinite. Al-Ghazālī's move is that this impossibility is not just logical but metaphysical. The Madhyamaka dissolution — "the question imports a false presupposition" — does not answer this. It deflects the question of why there is a present moment at all.

The regress among contingent beings is the second pressure point. Every contingent being requires an explanation of its existence from outside itself. A series of contingent beings, however long, is itself contingent as a whole — the series has no self-explanation. The terminating being cannot be contingent, because then it too requires external explanation. It must be *wājib al-wujūd*: necessary in its existence, unable to fail to exist. This is not merely a theological label; it is what the structure of the argument earns.

### The four required sharpenings

**1. Necessary existence, not merely actual.** The draft asserts al-Qayyūmiyya but does not press that the terminus must be *necessarily* existent — that its non-existence must be *impossible*, not merely that it happens to exist. A being that actually exists but could have been otherwise is still contingent, still requires external explanation, still fails to stop the regress. Ibn Sīnā's māhiyya / wujūd distinction is the mechanism: in contingent beings, essence and existence are distinct (the being has an essence that does not include its existence; existence is added to essence from outside). In the necessary being, essence *is* existence; there is no gap into which contingency can enter. The corpus must state this mechanism, not merely use the name.

**2. Resolution (ii) is incoherent, not merely costly.** The draft says resolution (ii) is "coherent but costly." The kalām lens presses harder: the conjunction "groundless + authored" is not a stable position. "Authored" requires an author who exists prior to the chain being authored. That author is either aseic (resolution i in disguise, with the name changed), part of the not-yet-existing chain (impossible — the chain does not exist to author its own origin), or itself groundless and requiring a prior author (vicious regress of positors, each requiring a prior positor before the chain exists). There is no fourth option. The draft's framing of (ii) as a coherent alternative that is simply theologically inconvenient understates the philosophical collapse.

**3. The seity/aseity distinction is a category error of definition, not merely scope.** The draft correctly notes that the forcing argument operates at the conventional level. The kalām lens adds a sharper point: the seity/aseity distinction is defined *relationally* — *a se* means "from itself, not from another"; *ab alio* means "from another." These predicates presuppose a relational domain in which "self" and "other" have referents. Within the created order, everything is *ab alio*, and the forcing argument correctly shows this. But the uncreated source has no relational domain it belongs to — there is nothing outside it relative to which it is "from itself." Applying "seity-not-aseity" to the uncreated source is not merely a scope error (applying a predicate outside the argument's range); it is a definition error (applying a relational predicate where one relatum does not exist). This is the strongest reply to anyone who reads "no aseity anywhere" as including the divine.

**4. Origination criterion prior art must be credited more fully.** The origination criterion — "a trust anchor is sound iff it is marked as stipulated, not derived" — has closer prior art than the draft acknowledges. Van Til's presuppositionalism: the presupposition of God is argued *from*, not *to* — it is the basic posit from which all else is derived, not a conclusion derived from prior evidence. This is the "confessed posit" structure in theological form. Plantinga's properly-basic-belief framework (*Warranted Christian Belief*, 2000): a belief's warrant does not require it to be derived from evidence; it requires it to be produced by properly functioning faculties in appropriate circumstances. The origination criterion is the security-engineering parallel of "properly basic" — not derived, but appropriately marked as basic rather than derived. The draft should cite this lineage to accurately assess the novelty claim.

### What the kalām lens cannot break

The thesis's restriction of no-aseity to the created order is principled, not ad hoc, for reasons the kalām argument itself supplies: the regress among contingent beings terminates at a necessary being, and the necessary being is definitionally outside the domain of contingent beings. The forcing argument's "no aseity anywhere" applies within the domain where the forcing operates — the domain of contingent, conferred, conventional entities. The kalām argument establishes that there must be something outside that domain. The two claims are not in contradiction; they are claims about different domains. The restriction is the correct move. The cost — that the unrestricted formulation is misleading and must be corrected in print — is real, but it is not a philosophical concession; it is an editorial correction.

### Summary of the four bounds the thesis must adopt

| Bound | Why required |
|-------|-------------|
| Assert *necessary* existence, not merely *actual*, at the terminus | Contingent-but-actual still restarts the regress; the argument earns necessity, not mere actuality |
| Mark resolution (ii) as incoherent, not merely costly | "Groundless + authored" has no stable realization; every version collapses into (i) or vicious regress |
| State the category-error-of-definition explicitly | The seity/aseity distinction is relational and defined within the created order; applying it outside is a definition error, not only a scope error |
| Credit Van Til and Plantinga for the origination criterion's prior art | The "confessed posit marked as basic" structure appears in presuppositionalism and the warranted-basic-belief literature before the security-engineering literature |
