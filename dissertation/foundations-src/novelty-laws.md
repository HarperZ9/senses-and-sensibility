### Law (b) — The Residual-Aseity Theorem

**Statement.** Let *no-aseity* be the thesis: for every entity *x*, *x*'s existence, status, authority, and meaning are conferred by something other than *x* (nothing is self-standing). Then the operation "apply no-aseity" can be iterated on any entity *except one*: it cannot be applied to **the conferring seam itself** — the relation-token by which conferral happens — because to dissolve the seam is not to find a deeper conferring ground but to reach equilibrium (no relation, no sorting, "death"). Therefore exactly one thing resists dissolution: not a *substance* (it has no aseity-as-substance; it is a "near-nothing"), but the **necessity that there be a seam**. The seam is the one un-grounded ground.

**Scope.** Any conferral-structured ontology — i.e., any system that models existence/status as relational rather than intrinsic. Universal over entities; singular in its exception.

**Falsification condition.** Exhibit either (i) a self-standing entity that no-aseity cannot dissolve *and that is not the seam* (would break the "exactly one" uniqueness), or (ii) a deeper conferring ground *beneath* the seam that confers the seam's own existence without itself being a seam (would show the seam is not terminal — regress continues), or (iii) a system in which dissolving the seam yields a *richer* structure rather than equilibrium/death (would break the "dissolution = death" identity).

**Verdict: NOVEL-SYNTHESIS (moderate confidence).** The move "the universal solvent cannot dissolve its own container" is a recognizable transcendental form: a condition-of-possibility argument (Kant; and structurally, every self-undermining-skepticism rebuttal — you cannot doubt the doubting). The Madhyamaka-specific worry: the prior frame (Westerhoff/Nāgārjuna) already wrestles emptiness-of-emptiness (*śūnyatā-śūnyatā*) — emptiness is itself empty, NOT a residual self-standing ground. So (b) is in **prima facie tension with its own inherited frame**, and that tension is exactly where novelty could live: if the seam-necessity is genuinely un-empty in a way emptiness-of-emptiness is not, that's a new result; if it collapses into emptiness-of-emptiness, it's inherited and *the corpus has misread its own source* (high-value thing to test in section B). The phrase "near-nothing" is doing the work — it's an attempt to have a terminus that is neither substance nor nothing. **Genuinely novel piece:** the identification *dissolution-of-seam = equilibrium = death* (a thermodynamic/relational reading of metaphysical grounding). That specific identity I do not find in the priors (moderate-to-low confidence on the negative — I can't fully rule out process philosophy precedent, Whitehead/Deleuze).

### Law (c) — The Remainder Law (floats-free criterion)

**Statement.** A claim *C* about a seam *S* (where *S* sorts two states, *A* and *B*) is a **RESULT** (not a re-description) iff *C* names a property *P* such that:
1. *P* **obtains at the seam** (*P* is a property of the conferral-relation, not of *A* or *B* taken alone);
2. *P* is **not entailed by the bare existence of the seam** (you cannot derive *P* from "there is a seam sorting A and B"); and
3. *P* is **not closed by any third-person inventory** of *A* and *B* — i.e., a complete physical/structural description of both sorted states does not entail *P*.
A claim failing any of (1)–(3) is re-description.

**Scope.** Meta-level: it quantifies over *claims about seams*. This is a **demarcation criterion** (a test for resulthood), which makes it the most law-like candidate in the set — it has the form of a *criterion*, like falsifiability itself.

**Falsification condition.** Two ways. (i) Exhibit a *P* satisfying all three clauses that everyone agrees is mere re-description (criterion too weak). (ii) Exhibit a *P* failing a clause that everyone agrees is a genuine result (criterion too strong). A subtler failure: show clauses (2) and (3) are not independent — that "not entailed by the bare seam" already entails "not closed by third-person inventory," collapsing the criterion to a one-clause test (the explanatory gap restated).

**Verdict: NOVEL-SYNTHESIS, strongest law candidate (moderate-high confidence).** Clause (3) **is** the explanatory gap (Levine 1983) / the irreducibility claim (Nagel 1974) / knowledge-argument structure (Jackson) — "no third-person inventory closes it." That clause is *inherited*. The novelty is the **conjunction with clause (1)+(2): relocating the gap from being a property of a STATE (consciousness, the mental) to being a property of a SEAM (the conferral-relation)**, plus turning it into a *general demarcation test* applicable beyond consciousness — to meaning, agency, normativity. That generalization-of-the-gap-to-seams is the move I'd defend as the dissertation's actual contribution. Caveat (the threat to log in section B): a critic will say clause (3) alone does all the work and (1)/(2) are window-dressing — i.e., the Remainder Law is the explanatory gap with extra steps. The defense has to show a *P* that is seam-located but NOT a phenomenal-consciousness gap (the meaning-chapter "A/B undergoing" and agency-chapter "second-personal remainder" are the test cases — they must clear the bar *without* smuggling in phenomenality).

### Law (d) — Orthogonality + Determined-but-Irreducible

**Statement.** Two claims. **(d1) Orthogonality:** the determinism/indeterminism of the dynamics *within* states *A* and *B* is logically independent of (orthogonal to) whether there is an irreducible remainder *at the seam* between them. Formally: {determinism, indeterminism} × {remainder, no-remainder} is a 2×2 with all four cells consistently occupiable. **(d2) Determined-but-irreducible has teeth:** deterministic chaos (a system fully determined by its dynamics yet not predictively/analytically reducible due to sensitive dependence on initial conditions) is a *mathematical witness* that "determined" does not entail "reducible," so the remainder can co-exist with full determinism. Corollary: **conferred sourcehood** (sourcehood that is conferred, neither *causa sui* nor dissolved) occupies a cell the libertarian/hard-determinist dichotomy declares empty, dissolving the dichotomy.

**Scope.** Any agency/sourcehood debate framed as determinism-vs-freedom; more generally any argument that infers reducibility from determinism.

**Falsification condition.** For (d1): show one of the four cells is *necessarily* empty (e.g., prove indeterminism is required for a remainder — then they're not orthogonal). For (d2): show that the irreducibility of chaos is *merely epistemic* (predictive limitation) and therefore cannot witness an *ontological/explanatory* remainder — that the analogy equivocates between "can't compute it" and "isn't entailed by it." For the corollary: show "conferred sourcehood" is covertly either causa-sui or dissolution (the dichotomy was exhaustive after all).

**Verdict: INHERITED frame, NOVEL-SYNTHESIS in the chaos-witness move (moderate confidence; the witness is the weak point).** Orthogonality of determinism from the relevant freedom-property is **classical compatibilism** (Hume, Frankfurt, P.F. Strawson) — (d1) is largely inherited; its contribution is reframing the orthogonal axis as "seam-remainder" rather than "responsibility-conferring attitudes." Conferred sourcehood as a *tertium quid* to causa-sui/dissolution directly answers **Galen Strawson's Basic Argument** (the impossibility of self-causation) — and answering it via *conferral* (your source is real but given to you) is a clean, possibly novel move (moderate confidence it's not already in the compatibilist literature under another name — e.g., "ownership"/"identification" accounts get close). **Weakest link:** (d2). The chaos-witness equivocates with low-to-moderate risk between *epistemic* irreducibility (you can't predict it) and the *explanatory/constitutive* irreducibility the Remainder Law needs (it isn't entailed by the third-person inventory). Chaos is fully entailed by its inventory (the equations + initial conditions); you just can't *compute the trajectory*. So chaos may give "determined-but-unpredictable," not "determined-but-not-entailed." That gap I'd flag as a likely defeater to log.

### Law (g) — The Conferral Invariant

**Statement.** Every conferral-theology (any tradition that grounds existence/status in an act of conferral by a source) instantiates exactly three components: (i) a **word-force** — a performative utterance that brings about what it names; (ii) a **two-state conferral** — a sorting of a before-state and an after-state across the utterance; and (iii) an **irreducible hinge** — a third element, neither word-force nor either state, at which the conferral turns. Cross-tradition witnesses: Abrahamic ⟨*kun* "Be!" / *emet* truth-seal / *alif*⟩; Yoruba ⟨*àṣẹ* generative-force / Ifá / Èṣù the threshold-trickster⟩.

**Scope.** All conferral-structured cosmogonies/theologies. This is the one **empirical, cross-traditional conjecture** in the set — it makes a prediction about the comparative anthropology of religion.

**Falsification condition.** Clean and genuine: exhibit a conferral-theology with a word-force and two-state conferral but **no isolable hinge element** (component iii absent), or one whose hinge is *reducible* to the word-force or to one of the states. Conversely, a tradition where the three collapse to two would falsify the "exactly three" structure. (Risk of unfalsifiability-by-flexible-coding: if "hinge" is defined loosely enough, an analyst can always find a third thing — see verdict.)

**Verdict: NOVEL conjecture (form), but at risk of being NON-FALSIFIABLE in practice (moderate confidence).** As a *structural-anthropological conjecture* this is the most straightforwardly novel item — I'm not aware of a prior claiming this specific tripartite invariant across Abrahamic and Yoruba cosmogony (low-to-moderate confidence on the negative; comparative-religion structuralism à la Dumézil/Eliade is the threatening prior — tripartite structural invariants are *exactly* the Dumézilian house style, so the *method* is inherited even if this *instance* is new). The serious methodological hazard: the hinge (clause iii) is defined as "the irreducible third," which makes it definitionally guaranteed once you decide to find three things — that's the Remainder Law's clause-3 imported into anthropology and risks circularity. To be a real conjecture it needs an *independent* identification of the hinge (e.g., the threshold/trickster *role*, which Èṣù and a truth-seal share) that could have come out absent. **This is the law most in need of a pre-registered coding scheme** before it can claim falsifiability.

### Law (h) — The Membrane Identity: AuthN/AuthZ = Is/Ought

**Statement.** The security-engineering distinction between **authentication** (AuthN: establishing *what is the case* — that a principal is who it claims) and **authorization** (AuthZ: establishing *what the principal may do* — its permissions) is **identical in structure** to the **is/ought gap** (Hume): no inventory of AuthN facts entails an AuthZ permission, exactly as no set of *is*-statements entails an *ought*. The **membrane** (the selectively-permeable boundary that admits some, refuses others) **is** the conferral-seam re-spoken in the security register: it is neither of the two states (authenticated-identity, granted-permission) but the relation that sorts them.

**Statement, general form.** For any system exhibiting a *verification* layer (establishing facts) and a *permission* layer (conferring entitlements), the gap between the layers is a token of the is/ought seam, and no enrichment of the verification layer closes it.

**Scope.** All access-control architectures; by the general form, any fact-layer/entitlement-layer system (law, credentials, citizenship, licensing).

**Falsification condition.** Exhibit an authorization system where permissions are *strictly entailed* by authentication facts with **no additional conferring policy** — i.e., AuthZ is a pure function of AuthN with no irreducible policy input. (Attribute-based access control where the attributes *fully determine* access looks like a candidate counterexample — the section-B test: does ABAC close the gap, or does the *policy mapping attributes→permissions* just relocate the ought?)

**Verdict: NOVEL-SYNTHESIS / illustration (moderate-high confidence it's an instance, not a new law).** The is/ought gap is wholly inherited (Hume). The contribution is the **identification** that a working engineering distinction *already encodes* it — which is illuminating and pedagogically powerful, and the "membrane = re-spoken seam" framing is a genuine application of the corpus's central move. But epistemically this is an **instance of the Remainder Law**, not an independent law: AuthZ is the *P* that obtains at the AuthN/AuthZ seam, is not entailed by the bare seam, and is not closed by a third-person inventory of authenticated facts. So (h) is best read as a *worked example confirming (c)*, and its strongest value is as **evidence for the deeper law** below, not as a standalone result.

### The deeper law — pattern recognition across (b), (c), (d), (h)

Asked whether there's a single law of which the Remainder Law, orthogonality, and the near-nothing are instances. There is a candidate, and I think it's **the strongest claim to be THE law of the dissertation**:

> For any conferral-seam *S* sorting states *A* and *B*:
> there exists an irreducible remainder *located at S* (Remainder Law, c) that

> (3) **cannot itself be dissolved** by the very conferral operation that dissolves *A* and *B*, because dissolving *S* is equilibrium/death, not a deeper ground (Residual-Aseity, b);
> and the seam is a **near-nothing** — it has no aseity-as-substance, yet is the one un-grounded ground (the near-nothing, f).

Read this way:
