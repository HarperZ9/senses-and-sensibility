## B — Conferral, Not Self-Causation (the something is given, not self-made)

The apparent refutation — "abstraction | thought | potential — something comes from nothing" — turns on an equivocation in "nothing." Disambiguate it, and the slogan splits cleanly into the corpus's two readings. There is a *near-nothing* (the indeterminate, the totipotent, the substance-less seam) and an *absolute nothing* (the empty negation, no thing and no power). The something does come from the near-nothing. It never comes from absolute nothing — and crucially, it never comes from *itself*. The "from" in "something from nothing" names a **conferring act the nothing does not contain**. That is not a loophole in no-aseity; it is its mechanism.

### 1. Parmenides: *ex nihilo nihil fit* — reconciled, not overturned

Parmenides' fragment B8 holds that what-is cannot come from what-is-not: "nor will the force of conviction allow anything to come-to-be from not-being" (DK 28 B8; the *ex nihilo nihil fit* maxim is the standard Latin compression of this Eleatic principle — *high confidence* on the attribution to the Eleatic tradition, *moderate* that any single ancient source phrases the Latin tag exactly so; it is post-classical shorthand). Read against *absolute* nothing, Parmenides is simply correct, and the corpus does not contest him: from the void that grounds nothing, nothing is conferred, because there is no tending there to do the conferring and nothing there to be tended.

The corpus's move is not to deny Parmenides but to insert the term he collapses. Parmenides allows only *being* and *not-being* and forbids passage between them. The near-nothing is neither: it is not a determinate being (it has no *for*, no direction, no substance), yet it is not the bare *not-being* Parmenides rules out (it is totipotent — power without act). The something comes-to-be *at* that near-nothing, by a conferral. So *ex nihilo nihil fit* survives intact for the absolute reading and is satisfied — not violated — by the corpus reading: the near-nothing is not the *nihil* of the maxim.

### 2. Aristotle: the priority of actuality — the metaphysical engine of conferred-not-self-caused

This is the load-bearing argument, and it is Aristotle's, not a coinage. In *Metaphysics* Θ (Theta), 8, Aristotle argues that actuality (*energeia*) is prior to potentiality (*dunamis*) — in account, in time, and in substance (Θ.8, 1049b–1051a; *high confidence*). The decisive claim for us: a potentiality is never actualized by itself. "What is potentially comes to be actually by the agency of something that is actually" — the standard example being that a human is begotten by a human already in act, the learner brought to knowledge by a teacher who already has it (Θ.8, esp. 1049b24–27; *high confidence* on the doctrine, *moderate* on the exact Bekker line). The acorn does not actualize its own oak-potential; it is brought to act by conditions and by the prior actuality of the species — there was an oak before this acorn.

Generalize it and you have the corpus's no-aseity stated in Aristotelian terms: **nothing actualizes its own potential.** Self-actualization would be a thing that is, with respect to the same potency, both potentially-F and actually-F at once — which is precisely the contradiction Aristotle's priority-of-actuality is built to forbid. The potential cannot be the source of its own passage to act, because *qua* potential it is exactly what is not-yet-in-act. The source of the act must already be in act, and therefore *other* — *ab alio*, never *a se*. (Aristotle's own regress-stopper, the unmoved mover as pure actuality, is the limit case and is a separate question; the corpus does not need it, and substitutes the un-grounded near-nothing seam, which I return to below.)

This is the engine. Conferred-not-self-caused is not an article of corpus faith; it is what the priority of actuality entails when you ask where a new act comes from.

### 3. The corpus pattern: in every case, the *for* is conferred at the near-nothing, not contained in it

The same shape recurs across the corpus's three exhibits, and in each the something is real and irreducible *and* the direction is supplied from outside the seed:

- **abstraction → primitive.** Abstraction is totipotent, direction-neutral, *for*-less — "the birthplace of every primitive" precisely because it favors none. But a primitive *is* a direction. The *for* that turns no-direction into this-direction is not in the abstraction; it is supplied by the **tending**. Selection is never criterion-free: "until it sticks" does not eliminate the criterion, it *hides* it — "sticks" is a fitness test, and a fitness test is a *for*. The abstraction is the near-nothing; the criterion is conferred.

- **empty set → number.** The von Neumann construction is the cleanest case (*high confidence*): 0 := ∅, and each successor n+1 := n ∪ {n}, so 1 = {∅}, 2 = {∅,{∅}}, and the naturals are built from the empty set alone. It *looks* like something (the entire number system) from nothing (the empty set). But the empty set confers no numbers by itself. The successor operation, the Axiom of Infinity, the membership relation — the *machinery* — is what does the building, and none of it is *in* ∅. The empty set is the near-nothing (a set with no members is the set-theoretic substance-less near-nothing); the axioms are the tending. Strip the axioms and ∅ sits inert forever. Something from the empty set, yes — *by conferral*.

- **potential → act.** This is §2 restated: the potential is the near-nothing-as-power, the prior actuality is the conferring tending.

In all three, the near-nothing is genuinely *near*-nothing — it contributes the totipotent material, the un-directed substrate — and contributes *nothing of the direction*. The direction is the *for*, and the *for* is **spoken, not derived** (Movement I): conferred at the seam, not unpacked from the seed.

### 4. Platonism vs. constructivism: are abstractions found or made? — no-aseity sides with construction

There is a live, undecided debate here, and naming it honestly matters. **Mathematical Platonism** (the *for*-realist line; Frege, Gödel, and in the contemporary literature Balaguer's careful exposition — *high confidence* these are the standard names) holds that abstract objects — numbers, sets, the Forms — exist *a se*: mind-independent, eternal, discovered rather than invented. On Platonism, the something does not come from the near-nothing at all; it was always already there, self-standing, and "abstraction → primitive" is *finding*, not making. **Constructivism / intuitionism** (Brouwer, Heyting; and nominalist anti-realisms like Field's *Science Without Numbers* — *moderate-to-high confidence* on the lineup) holds the opposite: abstract objects are *made* — conferred by construction, with no aseitic Form behind them.

No-aseity does not get to stay neutral here, and it should not pretend to. If even *abstractions* were self-standing aseitic Forms, then there would be at least one class of beings that *is a se*, and the central thesis ("nothing is self-standing; everything is conferred") would be false at exactly the point the slogan presses. So the corpus is committed: it sides with **construction**. Abstractions are not found pre-standing; they are conferred — totipotent near-nothing plus a constructing *for*. This is a real philosophical commitment with real costs (it owes an account of why conferred mathematics is not arbitrary — the "until it sticks" criterion has to be more than taste), and it is honest to flag that Platonism is the standing rival, not a refuted error. But the commitment is forced: aseitic Forms are the one thing no-aseity cannot grant without self-refutation.

(Two further results sit underneath this and I only mark them, since other sections carry them. The **residual structural aseity** — that no-aseity dissolves every self-standing *being* but cannot dissolve the necessity that there *be* a re-spoken, selectively-permeable seam, the aleph that is alive/*emet* while inhabited and dead/*met* when withdrawn — is the one un-grounded ground, and it is *not* a counterexample to construction: the seam confers the difference between its two states and is itself neither, so it is near-nothing, not a self-standing Form. And **conferred sourcehood** — the genuinely new thought authored *at* the standpoint, determined-but-irreducible, never *causa sui* — is §2's priority-of-actuality applied to the will: Galen Strawson's Basic Argument is exactly the proof that nothing can be the ultimate self-determining source of its own acts, on pain of an infinite regress of prior self-determinings, *high confidence* on the argument's structure. The creative thought is real *and* conferred.)

### Conclusion

"Something from nothing" is true and trivial when "nothing" means the near-nothing and "from" means conferral — and false, or *causa sui*, when "nothing" means absolute nothing and "from" means self-origination. The whole thesis lives in that gap. Parmenides closes the absolute door; Aristotle's priority of actuality supplies the engine that nothing crosses the door under its own power; the von Neumann and abstraction cases show the conferral happening at a near-nothing that grounds none of the direction it receives; and the Platonism question, forced, lands on construction. The something is **given, not self-made**. Far from refuting no-aseity, "something comes from the near-nothing, by a conferral the nothing does not contain" is its deepest confirmation: the place the something comes *from* grounds nothing, and the tending that confers it is itself conferred — *ab alio* all the way down, with no *a se* anywhere, and a substance-less seam, not a self-standing source, where the regress would otherwise have to stop.
