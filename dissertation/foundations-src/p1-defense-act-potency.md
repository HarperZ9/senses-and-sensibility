# Defense of P1 via Act/Potency (Aristotelian-Thomist Strategy)

## Thesis under defense

P1: Any act of conferral — sorting A from not-A — is performed AS THIS DISCRIMINATION AND NOT ITS NEGATION, indexed at a standpoint. An occurrence indexed at nothing is no occurrence.

The contested entailment: "occurring" entails "occupied-at-an-index" in a sense stronger than "is a dependently-arisen event."

---

## The strategy

Act/Potency (Aristotelian-Thomist). Conferral is actualization: the passage from indeterminate potency to THIS determinate act. To actualize is to determine this-rather-than-that. Determination IS occupation of a contrast-vertex (what P1 calls an index). "Indexed-at-nothing actualization" actualizes nothing — it is not conferral. So P1 is analytic of "confer."

---

## The Bridge Argument

### Step 1 — Conferral is constitutively discriminative

Conferral is not a neutral physical transaction. Sorting A from not-A structurally requires that something is designated THIS and not THAT. A rock falling instantiates no contrast; it passes from here to there. Conferral is the act where the contrast is the act. This distinguishes conferral from mere causal passage: its product is a determined assignment, not a trajectory.

### Step 2 — Act/potency: actualization IS determinateness

Aristotle's Metaphysics Theta (Book IX, ~1048a–1050b) [citation confidence: high] develops the act/potency distinction as follows. Potency (dunamis) is indeterminate capacity — the not-yet-THIS-nor-THAT state. Act (energeia / entelecheia) is the realized, determined state: the capacity has been settled into a specific realization. The key structural point is not causal but ontological: actuality is determinateness. An undifferentiated potency is precisely the absence of determination. Actualization is the passage from indeterminate to determinate.

This is not (yet) a claim about subjects, standpoints, or minds. It is a claim about the formal structure of what it means for a capacity to be realized.

### Step 3 — Aquinas: act limits and specifies

Aquinas inherits and sharpens this in De Potentia Q.1 a.1 and the Summa Contra Gentiles I.16 [citation confidence: moderate — loci are standard in Thomist secondary literature; exact Latin not verified against a checked edition]. The central entailment: act (actus) is that which limits and specifies potency. To be THIS act is to be NOT the other possible realizations of the same potency. The act occupies a determinate position in the space of possible realizations; it excludes the others. This is not metaphorical. The determinateness of the act is constituted by its contrastive exclusion of alternatives.

### Step 4 — Identification: determination = contrast-vertex = index

To determine this-rather-than-that is to occupy the THIS-side of a THIS/NOT-THAT contrast. Call this the "contrast-vertex." It is the minimal structural requirement for determination to be determination rather than an undifferentiated mass of potency. The index in P1 just IS the contrast-vertex: the structural position from which THIS is THIS (as opposed to not-THAT). It is not a Cartesian ego, not a metaphysical subject, not an intrinsic nature. It is the formal requirement that a determination be a determination.

Without a contrast-vertex: "this-rather-than-that" has no referent for "this." The determination dissolves back into potency — undifferentiated, not yet THIS.

### Step 5 — Apply to conferral

Conferral is the actualization of a discriminative capacity: the capacity to sort A from not-A. When conferral occurs, that capacity is actualized: A is sorted from not-A. By Steps 2–4: that actualization is the occupation of the contrast-vertex A/NOT-A. If there is no contrast-vertex, nothing has been actualized, and no sorting has occurred. But then, ex hypothesi, conferral has not occurred. This is the desired result: conferral entails the occupation of a contrast-vertex (index).

### Step 6 — The ownerless-occurrence reply addressed

The Prasangika Madhyamika (Nagarjuna, Mulamadhyamakakarika; Candrakirti, Madhyamakavatara) [citation confidence: high for textual loci] grants dependent arising (pratityasamutpada) but denies svabhava — own-being, intrinsic nature. The goer/going analysis (MMK II) dismantles the inference from going to a goer with own-being behind it. The Vigrahavyavartani's "I have no thesis" move [citation confidence: high for the text's existence and general argumentative strategy] means the emptiness-claim does not assert its own ultimate standing.

The act/potency strategy does not require svabhava at the object level. The contrast-vertex is relational: THIS is THIS relative to not-THAT. The relativity is not a defect — it is the point. A dependently-arisen determination is still a determination. Its THIS-ness is dependent, but its being-THIS (as opposed to being-THAT) is what makes it a determination and not undifferentiated potency. What would require svabhava would be a determination that is THIS independently of all contrast with THAT. Nothing in the strategy asserts that.

The question is whether dependently-arisen arising requires a vertex or permits a fully symmetrical web. The Madhyamika position is the symmetric web: the contrast exists, but neither side is "more a vertex" than the other; neither has a privileged THIS-position. Against this, the act/potency strategy presses: but which side was actualized? Actualization is asymmetric — potency was realized AS THIS, not AS THAT. The realization lands somewhere. Even in a web of dependences, the landing is asymmetric.

The Madhyamika's reply is to deny the asymmetry at the ultimate level: both sides arise together, neither "lands" more than the other. This is where the framework disagreement is irreducible at the ultimate level.

### Step 7 — The conventional forcing argument

Candrakirti's Prasangika grants conventional reality (samvrti-satya) to discriminative acts, causal sequences, persons [citation confidence: high — this is the defining commitment of Prasangika over Yogacara and Svatantrika]. At the samvrti level, conferral acts, sortings, and determinations are real. The structure of Step 5 applies at samvrti: the conventional conferral act occupies a contrast-vertex conventionally. The index is occupied conventionally.

The denier's own enacting act is a conferral at the samvrti level: the act of rejecting the index is itself a discriminative sorting (index/no-index), which by Step 5 occupies a contrast-vertex conventionally. This does not establish the index at the ultimate level — and the honest result is that it cannot. At the paramartha level, the Madhyamika's sunyata-sunyata (emptiness is itself empty) means no structure, including the contrast-vertex, has ultimate standing. The contribution is bounded at samvrti.

## The Critical Step: Occurs → Occupied-at-Index

The single critical inference is:

**"An actualization has occurred" → "a contrast-vertex (index) is occupied"**

The derivation: actualization = determination (from act/potency analysis) = carving off alternatives = occupying the THIS-side of a THIS/NOT-THAT contrast = occupying an index.

### Self-audit: does this step presuppose the index?

Verdict: **borderline.**

The step is not viciously circular. It does not say "there is an index, therefore there is an index." It derives the contrast-vertex from the structure of determination, which is itself derived from the act/potency analysis. The derivation has substantive premises: the act/potency framework, the analytic connection between actualization and determination, and the claim that determination is contrast-constituting (i.e., that being THIS requires being not-THAT).

However, the step is framework-dependent in a way that matters. The move from "determination" to "contrast-vertex" encodes the claim that determination is asymmetric — that actualization lands on a THIS-side, rather than producing a symmetric arising of both sides. The Madhyamika denies exactly this asymmetry at the ultimate level. They grant arising but deny the vertex precisely because they deny that arising is asymmetric in the way actualization requires.

The act/potency framework cannot establish the asymmetry against a committed denier of that framework without circularity: to say "actualization is asymmetric because it is the passage from potency to act" is to invoke the very framework whose asymmetry is contested.

So the step earns the index for any interlocutor who accepts that acts are asymmetrically determined. It does not earn it for the Prasangika who rejects that asymmetry at the ultimate level without a further argument that the denier's own enacting asymmetry forces the concession — which is the conventional forcing move of Step 7, not the act/potency argument itself.

The act/potency strategy gets to FORCES-CONVENTIONALLY. It does not get to ULTIMATE.

---

## Level reached

**CONVENTIONAL (samvrti)**. The act/potency argument establishes that any conferral, as actualization, occupies a contrast-vertex at the conventional level of analysis — the level at which the Prasangika already grants discriminative acts exist. At the ultimate level, the Madhyamika simply rejects the asymmetry premise that is load-bearing in the step "actualization → occupied-vertex." The honest ceiling is the conventional forcing result: the denier's own enacting conferral-act instantiates a contrast-vertex at samvrti, conceding the index conventionally while the ultimate-level question remains open.

---

## Self-grade

**Moderate.**

The argument is coherent, derives the contrast-vertex with substantive structure, and is not question-begging in the trivial sense. It achieves the forcing result at the conventional level, which is the honest ceiling for a non-question-begging argument against Prasangika. It does not achieve the ultimate result, and the self-audit honestly identifies why: the asymmetry premise is the framework-encoding that the Madhyamika denies. A strong grade would require either a non-question-begging proof of the asymmetry at the ultimate level, or a demonstration that the Madhyamika's own moves at the ultimate level require the asymmetry. Neither is fully delivered here. The conventional forcing argument (Step 7) does useful independent work but relies on the Madhyamika's own samvrti concessions rather than the act/potency strategy proper — so it supplements rather than rescues the core inference.
