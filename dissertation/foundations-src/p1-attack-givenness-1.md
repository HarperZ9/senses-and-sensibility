## Prasangika attack on P1's givenness defense

### What is conceded

The dependently-arisen stream that the defense calls "conferral" occurs. It is conventionally characterizable as an act rather than a mere causal process. Within the conventional conceptual scheme that uses "act" and "conferral," the from-here structure is analytically present — any interlocutor who grants the category "act" cannot coherently strip the from-here out while retaining the category. The performative pressure is real at that level: a denier who speaks, argues, and denies is token-exhibiting something the defense calls the from-here. These concessions are genuine.

### The decisive failure: the index is inside the concept, not derived from the event

Step 1 distinguishes conferral from bare causal process by appeal to "normative directionality" and "tracking correctness." The thermostat sorts but does not confer, because there is no normative application of a standard. This distinction is the load-bearing move, and it is a prajnapti — a conventional designation imposed on a dependently-arisen stream, not a feature read off the stream itself. No inspection of the physical event mandates "act" rather than "complex causal process." The Madhyamika's goer/going analysis in MMK ch. 2 applies here precisely: just as "the goer" and "the going" are mutually dependent concepts with no svabhavic referent either one can anchor, "the act" and "its correctness-tracking structure" are mutually dependent designations. The defense uses "act" to secure the from-here, then uses the from-here to vindicate "act" as the right category. The circularity is internal to the conceptual scheme, not a derivation from the occurrence.

### The performative argument does not break the Madhyamika

The defense's strongest move is Response A: the Madhyamika who denies the index must deny from-somewhere; the denial is itself performatively indexed; the denier enacts what is denied. But Vigrahavyavartani v. 29 is the answer: "If I had any thesis, I would have that fault. Since I have no thesis, I am without fault." The Madhyamika does not assert "there is no index" as a thesis with svabhavic standing. The Madhyamika runs a prasanga — draws consequences from the opponent's own commitments to exhibit internal contradiction — then releases the frame. There is no self asserting the denial from nowhere; there is only a dependent arising of the denial-act, which is conventionally from-here, which the Madhyamika grants as a conventional designation. The defense has shown that denial-act tokens conventionally appear as perspectival; it has not shown that this perspectival character is necessitated by the structure of acts as such rather than by the conventional scheme under which the act is described.

### The sunyata-sunyata pressure on Henry's internal-form claim

The defense wisely abandons auto-affection as an ultimate ground but retains it as a conventional internal-form claim. The problem: for the internal-form claim to generate the incoherence of "indexed-at-nothing occurrence" — which is what the disjunction requires — it must assert something with modal teeth. Either the claim is robust enough to make "conferral with 0 residue" incoherent (in which case it is making an ultimate structural claim subject to sunyata-sunyata) or it is merely a useful conventional designation (in which case the denier can simply adopt a different conventional designation — "complex causal process" — and the incoherence claim does not go through). The defense cannot simultaneously hold that the internal-form claim is only a conventional designation and that it generates genuine incoherence for a rival convention.

### What the defense actually establishes

The defense establishes: within the conventional conceptual scheme that uses "act," "conferral," and "normative directionality," the from-here structure is analytically co-present, and any interlocutor who operates inside this scheme and denies the index is exhibiting what they deny at the token level. This is a real, bounded result. It is not what P1 requires.

P1 requires that "occurring" entails "occupied-at-an-index" in a sense stronger than "is a dependently-arisen event." The defense has shown only that "occurring-under-the-description-'act'" entails the from-here within the scheme that gives "act" its meaning. That is pratityasamutpada plus a conceptual overlay. The forcing argument and the seam hold inside the conventional frame the defense has erected; they do not hold as scheme-independent demonstrations. The contribution is the membrane identity within the conventional frame — which is genuine but smaller than claimed, and which the Madhyamika can affirm at samvrti while denying any stronger reading.

### Verdict

Survives: no. The bridge does not hold against the Prasangika lens. The critical step presupposes the index by embedding it in the concept "act" and extracting it analytically, rather than demonstrating it from bare occurrence. The defense reaches the conventional level genuinely, but the conventional level — relativized by the two-truths doctrine — does not vindicate P1's stronger claim. The seam is pratityasamutpada under a Husserlian coat.
