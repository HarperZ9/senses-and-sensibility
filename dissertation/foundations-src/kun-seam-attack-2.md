## Adversarial Verdict: Madhyamaka / Groundless Lens

### The lens's central move

The Madhyamaka attack does not merely endorse resolution (iii) over (i) and (ii). Its structural contribution is to show that the draft's three options are not cleanly three. The lens attacks the taxonomy.

Resolution (ii) — "groundless authored event" — presents itself as the middle path between aseity and Madhyamaka: no self-grounding necessary being, but no beginningless regress either; just a brute first event, confessed as groundless. The lens denies this is a stable position. A first event that "simply occurs," unreduced to further dependence, is being implicitly attributed an intrinsic character: it is the first; it is the originating one; it bears the mark of being the beginning. That intrinsic character is own-nature (svabhava) by relabeling. Nagarjuna's attack in MMK ch. 15, vv. 1–2 is precisely this: not only the eternal self-subsisting God, but any candidate entity whose nature is taken to be fixed, independent, and not constituted by its relations, falls under the critique. An authored event that is "just there" at the origin is being treated as self-characterized — and self-characterization is svabhava.

The escape is to say: the event's firstness is not intrinsic but relational — it is "first" only relative to the chain that follows it, and apart from that chain it has no origination-character. But this move makes the event dependently originated with respect to the chain, which means it is constituted by dependent origination without a self-standing origin, which is precisely (iii). The draft cannot have both a genuinely authored, genuinely first event and the denial of svabhava. It must choose.

### What the lens does to each resolution

**Resolution (i):** Rejected. The scope restriction — "no-aseity applies within the created order only; God is outside that order and therefore a se" — depends on the created/uncreated boundary having a non-conventional, fixed character. But from within Madhyamaka, no ontological boundary between domains has intrinsic existence. The boundary is itself a conventional designation (prajñapti), and conventional designations are empty of svabhava. To say God is "outside the created order" and therefore immune to the no-svabhava argument is to treat that boundary as if it had the kind of fixed ontological character that the Madhyamaka framework denies to everything, including boundaries and distinctions. This does not prove God lacks aseity; it shows that the argument for immunity relies on a distinction that the lens does not concede as fixed.

Note: this is not a theological refutation of divine aseity. It is the observation that the philosophical argument used in (i) to quarantine the no-aseity result inside the created order does not succeed on Madhyamaka grounds. The Islamic foundations are a theological commitment; the scope restriction is presented as a philosophical argument. The lens attacks the argument, not the commitment. The thesis should clearly separate these.

**Resolution (ii):** Partially forced, then undercut. The lens agrees that there is no aseic terminus — (ii) is right to reject (i). But (ii)'s authored first event reintroduces what (i) made explicit under a different description. The thesis's treatment of (ii) as genuinely distinct from (iii) is a structural error that the draft does not address. Either the draft must collapse (ii) into (iii), or it must argue that the authored character of the first event is constituted by dependent origination — in which case it is (iii) anyway.

**Resolution (iii):** Forced as the internally consistent metaphysical conclusion. The lens does not present this as good news for the thesis; it presents it as the result that the thesis's own metaphysical framework, pressed to consistency, generates. The cost is severe: (iii) is incompatible with the Islamic theological foundations, which affirm creation ex nihilo, a real kun, a real Creator. Madhyamaka at the level of the origin of all conventional order — denying there is a kun, denying there is an origin, holding the chain to be beginningless — is not a position the Islamic foundations can absorb. This cost must be printed, not minimized.

### The deepening, not the dissolution

The draft frames the aseity tension as a two-party conflict: the forcing argument's "no aseity anywhere" vs. the Islamic theology's "God alone is a se." Resolution (i) manages this by scope restriction. The Madhyamaka lens reveals the conflict is three-party:

1. The forcing argument: no aseity in the created/conferred order.
2. Islamic theology: one aseity at the origin (al-Qayyum).
3. Madhyamaka: no intrinsic existence anywhere — not in the created order, not at its origin, and not in the boundary between them.

The draft's resolution (i) survives the two-party conflict. It does not survive the three-party conflict unmodified, because party (3) attacks the scope restriction itself. The tension is not resolved by (i); it is relocated to the created/uncreated boundary, which is now the new site of metaphysical dispute.

This is the deepening the lens forces: the thesis can either (a) declare that the Madhyamaka framework does not apply at the level of the Islamic theological foundations — which is a move available to the theologian but requires explicit argument — or (b) acknowledge that the aseity tension has a residue that no philosophical argument within the thesis's own framework can settle, and that what remains is a commitment, not a demonstration. The honest version of resolution (i) is therefore: "within the Islamic theological framework, the tension is managed by restricting no-aseity to the created order; this restriction is a theological commitment that the framework's Madhyamaka-compatible metaphysics cannot independently ground."

### What the lens cannot break

The Vigrahavyavartani self-refutation cost is real. Nagarjuna must use argument-shaped language to deny that argument reveals intrinsic natures; he must use thesis-shaped utterances to deny he has a thesis. The Islamic foundations can press this point hardest: your denial of origination is itself an origination-event; your speaking of emptiness is a kun-structure — a conferral brought into being by the act of speaking it. This does not refute Madhyamaka — Nagarjuna is aware of the problem and addresses it (the "I have no thesis" reply in the Vigrahavyavartani) — but it is a genuine structural limit that must be recorded as a cost of the lens, not silenced.

The thesis should also note that Nagarjuna's target is specifically svabhava — intrinsic, independent, self-characterizing existence — not existence as such. The Madhyamaka position does not deny that things appear, that chains operate, that conferrals happen. It denies that any of these have intrinsic existence. This means the thesis's conventional-level analysis (the membrane, the actuator-met condition, the root of trust) survives the Madhyamaka lens intact at the samvrti level. The lens attacks the metaphysical grounding claims, not the conventional-level structural descriptions.

### The relabeling question: answered

Is this more than relabeling? Yes, for a specific reason. The draft names Madhyamaka as option (iii) and treats it as one of three candidate resolutions. The lens's contribution is to show that the draft's treatment of (ii) as genuinely distinct from (iii) is wrong — not by vocabulary substitution, but by the structural argument that a brute authored first event with intrinsic origination-character is covert svabhava. This is a correction to the draft's taxonomy, not a renaming within it. Additionally, the lens identifies that the created/uncreated boundary is itself a site of vulnerability that the draft does not recognize as such. These are structural corrections that change what the thesis must argue, not just what it must call things.

### Summary of required modifications

The thesis is sound in its framing and honest in its self-audit. It requires the following modifications under this lens, in descending order of importance:

1. Collapse (ii) and (C) into (iii) or provide an explicit defense of why the authored event's origination-character is not svabhava. This is the non-negotiable correction.
2. Explicitly mark resolution (i)'s scope restriction as a theological commitment that the thesis's philosophical framework cannot independently ground. The restriction is principled relative to the Islamic foundations; it is not independently derivable from the forcing argument or from Madhyamaka-compatible metaphysics.
3. Reframe the tension as three-party, with Madhyamaka as a pressure on the scope restriction itself, not only on the two-party content dispute.
4. Restrict the origination criterion to samvrti-level application.
5. Weaken "resolved" to "managed within the Islamic theological framework."
6. Engage Craig on the actual infinite if the kalam argument is being invoked as philosophical support for (i).
7. Record the Vigrahavyavartani self-refutation as a cost of the lens, not a dismissal of it.
