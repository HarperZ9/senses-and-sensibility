## Prasangika Attack on P1's Bridge Argument

### What is being attacked

The load-bearing step: [conferral occurs] → [a side of a distinction is marked] → [the marked side is occupied] → [an index is occupied].

The defense's claim: this inference is analytic — "conferral" just means "marking a side" by definitional extraction via Spencer-Brown and Shannon, so the index is derived from the occurrence's own internal structure, not imported as a prior assumption.

---

### MMK Ch. 2 (goer/going) applied to the bridge

Nagarjuna's goer/going analysis refuses to locate motion in any substrate — not because motion does not occur, but because positing a "whence" presupposes a fixed bearer. The parallel here: the defense locates the index inside the occurrence by redescribing the occurrence through the Spencer-Brown calculus. But the calculus is not a transparent window onto dependent events. It is a conventional construction that carves any domain into marked and unmarked. The move from "bare dependent occurrence" to "occurrence-as-described-by-Spencer-Brown" is itself a conventional act of sorting. The inference [conferral occurs] → [a side is marked] does not run from the event to its structure; it runs from the event to a conceptual apparatus, and the apparatus is what yields the structure. The "whence" has merely been relocated into the formalism.

---

### VV "I have no thesis" — Step 6 assessed

The defense correctly identifies that even Nagarjuna's text performs a distinction at samvrti, and that the denier's enacting act instantiates a mark at the conventional level. This is the strongest part of the defense. The Prasangika cannot block it without performative cost: any refusal to concede Step 6 would itself be a distinguishing act. Step 6 is genuinely earned and the conventional-level forcing stands.

The Prasangika response, however, is that Step 6 only establishes what it claims to establish: a conventional-level residue. It does not establish that the index is derived from the bare occurrence's own structure rather than from the conventional apparatus applied to the occurrence. The "I have no thesis" weapon, properly deployed, does not refute Step 6 — it refutes the attempt to leverage Step 6's conventional result into a modal claim about occurrence-as-such.

---

### Sunyata-sunyata and the calculus's own emptiness

The defense concedes the argument halts at samvrti. The Madhyamika presses: if the incoherence of "indexed-at-nothing conferral" is only conventional — internal to a formal system (Spencer-Brown) that is itself empty of svabhava — then the incoherence does not constitute the strong modal result P1 needs. P1 is supposed to make "conferral with 0 residue" strictly contradictory, not merely conceptually malformed within one formal vocabulary. At the ultimate level, the Madhyamika says: conferral arises dependently, ownerlessly; the mark-language is projected onto it by a conventional apparatus; strip the apparatus and there is no residue. The mark is sunyata. The index derived from the mark is sunyata. The incoherence claim evaporates at paramarthika.

---

### The circularity at the foundation

Spencer-Brown's primitive is "Draw a distinction." In its canonical exposition (LoF, 1969), this act has an agent, a crossing, an interior, and an exterior. The "marked state" is explicitly defined as the side the act of crossing arrives at — i.e., defined relative to a perspective that is performing the crossing from somewhere. When the defense maps P1's "index" to "the mark's interior," it is extracting the index from a formalism whose foundation already encodes perspective. The argument is:

1. Conferral = Spencer-Brown distinction (by stipulated definition).
2. Spencer-Brown distinction analytically has a marked interior.
3. Marked interior = index.
4. Therefore conferral analytically has an index.

Step 1 is the gap. Establishing that bare dependent occurrence of conferral has the structure of a Spencer-Brown distinction — rather than merely being re-describable in those terms — requires that the Spencer-Brown framework correctly captures the intrinsic structure of the event. Establishing that correctness requires another act of sorting (choosing Spencer-Brown over alternative framings), which either regresses or presupposes index-like structure. The extraction is circular: the index is found in the apparatus because it was placed there when the apparatus was adopted.

---

### Verdict

The defense survives at the conventional level. Step 6's performative-forcing is genuine: any enacting act, including the Madhyamika's own refusal, instantiates a mark at samvrti. The conventional ceiling is real.

The defense does not survive as a demonstration of incoherence independent of the index. The critical inference runs through a conceptual apparatus (Spencer-Brown + Shannon) that encodes perspective at its foundation. Deriving the index from that apparatus and then claiming the derivation is free of presupposition is not available: the presupposition was made when the apparatus was adopted as the correct description of occurrence. The Madhyamika concedes the conventional result and asks for nothing more at samvrti — and at paramarthika, the apparatus itself is empty, so the incoherence claim does not reach through.

**Level reached: conventional. Bridge presupposes the index at the level where P1 needs it to be earned.**
