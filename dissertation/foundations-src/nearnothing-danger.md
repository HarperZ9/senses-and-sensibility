## C — The Aseity Danger (the seductive slide back to *causa sui*)

The slogan is at its most dangerous precisely where it feels most profound. "Something comes from nothing" reads, to the one undergoing it, as *self*-origination — and self-origination is exactly the aseity Movement I denies. So this is not a distant heresy to be guarded against; it is the **default misreading**, the gravity well the corpus's own central result has to climb out of. Name the slide, then cut it.

**The shape of the slide.** Four forms, one error:

1. *The creative thought as its own uncaused source.* The new idea arrives without visible antecedent, so it is taken to *have* no antecedent — to originate in itself. The phenomenology of suddenness is mistaken for the metaphysics of *self*-causation.
2. *The abstraction grounding itself.* Because abstraction is totipotent and direction-neutral — "the birthplace of every primitive" — it gets promoted from *birthplace* to *parent*: as if the seam supplied the *for*, rather than the *for* being spoken into it by the tending. (This is the "until it sticks" smuggle, relocated: the criterion is read back into the criterion-free seed.)
3. *MY thought / MY values as the unconferred origin.* This is Sartre's radical freedom in its strongest form — consciousness as a *néant* that "creates values" *ex nihilo*, each choice an absolute beginning answerable to nothing prior (*Being and Nothingness*, 1943; the lecture-form "man is condemned to be free," 1946). The self is cast as the conferring ground rather than a conferred one.
4. *The operator-sovereignty error*, recurring from earlier in this very project: the standpoint claiming to *be* the source of conferral rather than its *site*. Same structure, local name.

All four assert one thing: **an uncaused causer that is its own cause** — *causa sui*. That is the aseity the corpus dissolved in Movement I. If "something from nothing" requires it, the corpus is refuted. It does not, and it is not.

**The refutation — Strawson's Basic Argument.** The cleanest kill is Galen Strawson's *Basic Argument* (canonical statements: "The Impossibility of Moral Responsibility," *Philosophical Studies* 75, 1994; popularly, "Your Move: The Maze of Free Will," *NYT* 2010 — *high confidence* on text and structure). Compressed:

> (i) You do what you do because of the way you are.
> (ii) To be *ultimately* responsible for what you do, you must be ultimately responsible for the way you are — at least in some mental respect.
> (iii) But to be ultimately responsible for the way you are, you must have *brought it about* that you are that way. And to have done *that* through some prior choice, that choice must trace to a prior way you already were — regressing forever.
> (iv) To stop the regress at yourself, you would have to be **causa sui** — the cause of yourself, originating your own originating nature from nothing prior.
> (v) Nothing can be *causa sui*. "Nothing can be the ultimate cause of itself in any respect." Therefore ultimate self-origination is impossible.

Strawson deploys this against ultimate moral responsibility; the corpus deploys the *same lemma* against the creative self. Premise (v) is exactly NO-ASEITY in another key: nothing is *a se*, the self included. The slide in all four forms tries to make the thinker into its own ground — and the Basic Argument shows that ground does not exist *for anyone*. Note also Nietzsche's earlier verdict (*Beyond Good and Evil* §21, *high confidence*): *causa sui* is "the best self-contradiction that has been conceived so far… a sort of rape and perversion of logic." The creative self as unconferred origin is not a deep mystery; it is a known impossibility wearing the mask of suddenness.

**Why the new feels self-caused — and why the feeling is false.** Resolve this by tying to **conferred sourcehood** and **chaos**, the two results that let the genuinely new be *real* without being *aseitic*:

- *Real (irreducible to antecedents).* The new thought is not deducible from its inputs by any tractable shortcut. This is **computational irreducibility** (Wolfram, *A New Kind of Science*, 2002 — *high confidence* on the concept) and sensitive dependence in deterministic chaos (Lorenz, 1963 — *high confidence*): a system fully *determined* by its prior state can still be such that the only way to know its next state is to *run it* — there is no closed-form function of the antecedents that yields it cheaply. So "irreducible to its antecedents" and "determined by its antecedents" are **compatible**, not rivals. The thought is genuinely new — you could not have read it off the seed — *and* nothing about it floats free of the seam it was tended out of. The suddenness is the signature of irreducibility, **not** of acausality.
- *Not self-caused (conferred at the near-nothing).* The something comes "from nothing" in reading (A): from the **near-nothing** — the totipotent, direction-neutral seam, the substance-less *aleph*, alive (*emet*) only while inhabited and tended. The *for* is supplied by the **tending**, which the near-nothing does not contain — and the tending is *itself conferred*, not self-standing. So the origin is real but it is **off-self**: the thought's source is the standpoint *as a site of conferral*, never the standpoint *as its own cause*.

The disjunction that exhausts the genuinely new: **determined-but-irreducible, or conferred-but-not-self-caused — never aseitic.** Either frame denies *causa sui*; neither denies novelty. That is the whole gap. The slogan is *true* in reading (A) and *lethal* in reading (B), and what separates them is one move: whether the "nothing" grounds the something (B, false — it would have to be a *plenum* masquerading as a void, the error David Albert pressed against Krauss's *A Universe from Nothing*, 2012: the quantum vacuum, with its fields and laws, "is *not nothing* — it is something," *NYT* "On the Origin of Everything," March 2012, *high confidence*), or whether the something is *conferred at* a nothing that grounds nothing (A, true). Krauss's slip and the creative-self slip are the *same slip*: smuggling causal furniture into the void so it can "give rise" to things. Strip the furniture and the void gives rise to nothing — which is the point. *Ex nihilo nihil fit* (Parmenides, via the Aristotelian report; *high confidence*) is preserved, not violated: the seam is not a *nihil* that produces, but a *near*-nothing at which a conferred tending produces.

**The disciplined formula.** The thinker is **thrown / given** — *Geworfenheit*, the conferred condition (Heidegger, *Being and Time*, 1927, *high confidence*; and it is Heidegger's "Why is there something rather than nothing?" — Leibniz's question, *Principles of Nature and Grace* §7, 1714 — that *das Nichts* answers not with a deeper thing but with the prior of all things). The *thought* comes "from nothing"; the *thinker* does not; and the *tending* that confers the thought is conferred in turn. Three layers, none of them self-standing. The creative act is therefore the corpus's **deepest confirmation**, not its exception: the one place where it most *feels* like aseity is the one place where, examined, there is the most conferral and the least self-ground. The new is authored at the standpoint — **determined-but-irreducible, conferred-but-not-self-caused, never *causa sui*.**
