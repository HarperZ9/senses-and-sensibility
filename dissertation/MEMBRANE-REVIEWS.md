# Membrane Paper - Adversarial Reviews

## Lens: Metaethicist (is/ought gap, Searle's counts-as, Hare/Hudson rebuttal) - verdict: needs-work

**Strongest objection:** The paper's defense against Searle proves too much and thereby dissolves its own "identity" claim into a disjunctive analogy. In §5.2 the paper rebuts Searle exactly as Hare/Hudson do: the constitutive rule "valid credential counts as permission" smuggles the ought because the rule "was chosen, enacted, and maintained as a normative commitment" — the acceptance of the institution is the suppressed normative premise. Correct. But notice WHAT KIND of ought that suppressed premise is. Hare/Hudson locate the smuggled premise in the AGENT'S ENDORSEMENT of the institution — a genuinely action-guiding, motivating, prescriptive ought (you only get "you ought to keep your promise" if YOU subscribe to the promising game). The authorization "ought," by the paper's own §7 disclaimer, is emphatically NOT that: "the identity does not extend... to whether a policy is correct, fair, or legitimate"; it is "a logical claim about the structure of the inference... not a normative claim." So the authz "ought" is a bare deontic-logic operator (von Wright's permission, ¬O¬p) with the institutional/policy index held fixed — i.e., an "ought" RELATIVE TO a stipulated policy. But a relativized ought ("permitted-under-policy-P") IS derivable from is-facts plus the descriptive fact that P obtains, with no live normative premise — exactly as "checkmate" is derivable from board-state plus the descriptive fact that these are the rules of chess. Hume's gap is about the underivability of CATEGORICAL, agent-binding oughts from is alone; it does not forbid deriving institution-relative classifications. So the paper faces a fork, and both tines wound the IDENTITY claim: (a) If the authz "ought" is the strong, endorsement-laden Hare/Hudson ought, then the paper has imported normativity the access-control domain does not actually contain — a PDP's "permit" verdict binds no one to do anything and motivates no action; it is a classification, not a prescription — so the mapping is an analogy that overstates the deontics. (b) If the authz "ought" is the weak, policy-relative deontic operator (which §7 concedes it is), then it is NOT the Humean ought at all, because policy-relative permissions ARE derivable from is-facts-plus-rules — the very "is-ought entailment" Searle wanted, here granted by the paper's own relativization — so what survives is the unremarkable point that you cannot derive the permission WITHOUT the policy premise, which is just the trivial logical truth that conclusions need premises, true of chess and grammar and traffic law equally. Either way the claimed structural IDENTITY with Hume's guillotine collapses to a loose analogy: Hume's gap concerns the special resistance of CATEGORICAL moral oughts to naturalistic derivation (which is why moral realism/anti-realism is even at stake), whereas the authn→authz gap is the generic and philosophically cheap gap between any descriptive fact-set and any rule-relative classification built atop it. The paper cannot have it both ways: it invokes the full Humean apparatus (guillotine, conferral, "category error") to borrow gravitas, then in §7 retreats to a deflationary logical-relativity claim to stay safe from objections. The hostile referee's one-line kill: "Your §7 scope-limitation concedes the authz-ought is policy-relative and normatively inert; a policy-relative ought is Searle-derivable from is-plus-rule; therefore by your own lights the gap you've located is NOT Hume's (categorical) gap but the trivial premise-need of any rule-system — you have an analogy dressed as an identity."

## Metaethicist's review: The Membrane Identity

**Verdict: needs-work.** The paper's *engineering* observation is correct and its Searle rebuttal reproduces the right move (Hare/Hudson). But as a *metaethical* claim it equivocates on "ought," and that equivocation is fatal to the headline word **identity**. What survives is a true and useful claim — but it is a humbler one than advertised.

### 1. Is the is/ought mapping an IDENTITY or a loose analogy?

A loose analogy dressed as an identity — **fixable** by reframing, not by abandoning.

The paper claims structural *identity*: the authn→authz step *is* Hume's guillotine, "not merely analogous." To be an identity, the authz "ought" must be the *same kind* of ought Hume's law governs. It is not. Hume's guillotine bites on the **categorical, agent-binding, action-guiding** ought — the kind whose underivability from nature is why moral realism vs. anti-realism is even a live dispute. The paper's own §2 and §7 commit instead to a **policy-relative deontic operator** (von Wright's permission, ¬O¬p; "a logical claim... not a normative claim").

Those are different gaps:
- **Categorical gap (Hume):** no description of nature entails a binding ought. Hard, contested, metaethically loaded.
- **Rule-relative gap (the paper's actual find):** no description of the fact-base entails a permission *without the policy premise*. This is the generic conservativeness-of-logic point (Pigden): valid inference introduces no new non-logical vocabulary. It is shared by **chess** (board-state ↛ "checkmate" without the rules), **grammar** ("ungrammatical"), and **tax law** ("owes $X"). It is philosophically *thin*, not deep.

The authn→authz step instantiates the **second**. So the relation to Hume's *specific* guillotine is analogy (both are instances of the general autonomy-of-the-deontic), not identity. The paper borrows Hume's gravitas for a Pigdenian point.

### 2. The Searle confrontation: bridged, or question-begged?

**The paper gets the verdict right (the conferral smuggles the ought) but for incomplete reasons, and proving it boomerangs.**

Searle 1964 ("How to Derive 'Ought' from 'Is'") claims constitutive rules yield institutional obligations from brute facts. Hare ("The Promising Game," 1964) and Hudson (*The Is-Ought Question*, 1969) reply: the derivation needs a suppressed premise — **the agent's acceptance of / subscription to the institution**. That acceptance is itself prescriptive. So Searle begs the question; the ought was in the accepted rule all along. The paper's §5.2 reproduces this correctly: "the constitutive rule smuggles the ought... the act of enactment is the conferral."

So **the gap is NOT bridged; Searle's move begs the question** — I adjudicate with the paper, against Searle, on the narrow point.

**But here is the boomerang the paper does not see.** The Hare/Hudson smuggled premise is *endorsement* — a motivating, action-guiding commitment ("you ought to keep your promise" only if you subscribe to promising). Does a PDP/policy contain anything like endorsement? **No.** A "permit" verdict binds no one to act, motivates nothing, grounds no praise or blame. It is a classification, not a prescription. So the normativity that the Hare/Hudson rebuttal *needs in order to defeat Searle* is **absent** from the authorization domain. Which means the authz domain sits closer to Searle's brute-institutional *classifications* than to the morally-loaded promising case Hume actually cared about. The paper needs the strong (endorsement-laden) ought to beat Searle and the weak (inert, policy-relative) ought to stay inside its §7 scope limits — **and it cannot have both.**

### 3. The fork (the strongest objection, expanded)

- **Horn A — strong ought:** then the paper imports normativity access control does not contain (PDP verdicts neither bind nor motivate), and the mapping overstates the deontics → analogy.
- **Horn B — weak, policy-relative ought** (what §7 concedes): then permissions *are* derivable from is-facts + the descriptive fact that policy P is in force — the very is→ought entailment Searle wanted, here granted by the paper's own relativization. What's left ("you can't derive it *without* the policy") is the trivial premise-need of any rule-system → not Hume's gap.

Both horns kill **identity**. The §7 scope-disclaimer is the tell: it concedes Horn B in order to be objection-proof, but Horn B is exactly the horn on which the claim stops being Humean.

### 4. What to keep (the sound core)

Do not bin the paper. This is **true and worth saying**: *access control is a clean engineered exhibit of the autonomy of the deontic — the underivability of a rule-relative permission from the descriptive base alone (Pigden's formalization of Hume's law). The PDP/PIP split is the architectural acknowledgment of it; confused-deputy is the predicted failure of collapsing it.* That grounds the engineering payload without the metaethical overreach. Trade the "this IS Hume's guillotine" headline for the Pigden reframing and the paper becomes sound.

### 5. Smaller metaethical defects

- **Wrong modality (§2):** the analogy is built on *permission* (the weakest deontic modality), but Hume's "ought" is *obligation*. "You may" is the least action-guiding verdict there is; it merely fails to forbid. This understates how little deontic content the mapping carries.
- **Citation gap:** the lens names Hare/Hudson; the paper cites neither, nor Searle 1964 (the real target — it cites only *Speech Acts* and *Construction*), nor **Pigden 1989** (dispositive: vindicates the formal point, deflates the profundity). MacIntyre is in the references but unused.
- **Root-of-trust question-begging (§5.1, §8):** calling the regress terminus a "conferral" assumes the conclusion. Operationally a root of trust is a *stipulated brute-institutional IS* ("this key is trusted"). Whether the root is an ought or a stipulated is *is* the contested point; the label settles it by fiat. Relocate the ought-entry to the human act of stipulating (= Hare/Hudson "subscribing"), explicitly.
- **§4.4 over-reach:** "category error" holds only under the strong ought; under the paper's own deflationary reading, mistaking authn-strength for authz is an ordinary invalid inference within the policy table, not a Rylean category mistake.

### Bottom line

The Searle adjudication is right (conferral smuggles the ought; Searle begs the question). But the same Hare/Hudson logic that defeats Searle reveals the authz "ought" is too inert to *be* Hume's ought — so the result is an **analogy** to Hume via the **identity** with Pigden's general autonomy-of-the-deontic. Reframe around Pigden, cite the actual debate (Searle 1964 / Hare 1964 / Hudson 1969), pick a horn, and the contribution stands — smaller, but sound.

## Lens: Honest priority positioning — does the draft overclaim novelty, and does it cite/distance the closest prior art (Searle, Lampson/Abadi)? - verdict: needs-work

**Strongest objection:** The single hardest objection a hostile referee would raise: "The central novelty claim is undefended against the one body of literature that most directly anticipates it, and that literature is not even cited. Lampson, Abadi, Burrows & Wobber, 'Authentication in Distributed Systems: Theory and Practice' (ACM TOCS 1992), and Abadi, Burrows, Lampson & Plotkin, 'A Calculus for Access Control in Distributed Systems' (ACM TOPLAS 1993), construct a formal modal logic in which authentication facts ('A says s', the 'speaks-for' relation A⇒B) are explicitly and provably insufficient to yield an access decision: authorization requires an additional, independently-specified ACL/policy premise. That IS the is/ought separation rendered as a formal calculus — the authentication propositions are descriptive, the access decision requires a separate normative premise that the logic does not derive from the 'says'/'speaks-for' facts. A paper whose entire contribution is 'the authn/authz distinction is structurally identical to a gap requiring a separate bridging premise' cannot claim that identity is 'apparently unattested' while ignoring the foundational formal work that already separated the two layers as a matter of logical entailment. The referee says: either (a) the ABLP calculus already states your identity in everything but the name 'Hume,' making your contribution merely the application of a label, or (b) you must read ABLP and show precisely what their calculus does NOT say that yours does. As written, the draft has done neither, so the novelty claim is unearned, not false."

## Review: Honest Priority Positioning

**Verdict: needs-work.** The paper is intellectually careful and, on the philosophical flank, exemplary in its priority positioning. On the engineering-formal flank it has one decisive hole that, left unfixed, makes the central novelty claim indefensible to a hostile referee.

### What the draft gets right

- **Searle is handled correctly (§5.2).** The constitutive-rule objection is stated at full strength and then properly distanced: the 'counts-as' rule is itself an enacted normative act, so it institutionalizes the conferral rather than dissolving it. This is fair, non-overclaiming priority work and is the model the rest of the paper should match.
- **The ocap objection (§5.1) is handled correctly.** "Relocates, does not eliminate" is the right move and is argued, not asserted.
- **The novelty claim is *formally* modest in its framing** — "identity, not novelty of subject matter" (§3.5) shows the author knows the danger. The problem is that the prose does not hold to its own modesty.

### The decisive defect: the closest prior art is uncited

The lens names Lampson/Abadi for a reason. The **ABLP authentication-logic line** — Lampson, Abadi, Burrows & Wobber, "Authentication in Distributed Systems: Theory and Practice" (ACM TOCS, 1992); Abadi, Burrows, Lampson & Plotkin, "A Calculus for Access Control in Distributed Systems" (ACM TOPLAS, 1993) — is the nearest neighbor to this paper's actual claim, and it appears **nowhere** in §3 or the references. (high confidence on the existence and content of this literature; the 'speaks-for' relation A⇒B and the separate-ACL-premise structure are foundational and widely cited.)

That work already formalizes authentication as a set of modal propositions ("A says s", "A speaks for B") and shows that an access decision is **not derivable** from them without a *separate* ACL/policy premise. That is the authn/authz separation rendered as formal non-entailment — i.e. the structural separation the paper claims to identify is, in the formal access-control literature, prior art. A claim of "apparently unattested as an identity" cannot stand while this work is unsearched and uncited.

### The novelty claim drifts stronger than what survives

The draft oscillates between two claims:
- **Defensible:** "We are the first to *name* this separation as Hume's is/ought gap and bring it under the meta-ethical result that no descriptive premise yields a deontic one."
- **Indefensible:** "The distinction *IS* the gap, structurally, apparently unattested" — read by any referee as a first-sighting claim.

The draft's own §3.1 (von Wright: no normative conclusion from descriptive premises) and §3.3 (XACML PDP/PIP as "the architectural instantiation of the gap") already concede that the general logical form and the architectural separation are prior art. So the residual novelty is the **explicit subsumption / naming**, plus the consequences drawn from it (§4.4 unmeasurability, §6 failure predictions). That is a real but smaller contribution, and the paper should claim exactly it.

### The exact defensible novelty claim (and nothing stronger)

> The separation of authentication facts from the authorization decision is prior art — informally (Saltzer-Schroeder), formally (the ABLP speaks-for calculus, where access requires an ACL premise not entailed by the authentication propositions), architecturally (XACML PDP/PIP), and as a general logical result (von Wright). What this paper contributes is the explicit identification of the required extra premise as **deontic** — a permission, the weak deontic modality — and the consequent subsumption of the authn->authz step under Hume's is/ought result. From that classification, and only from it, follow the unmeasurability result (§4.4: the gap is logical, not epistemic, so no authentication improvement closes it) and the predicted failure taxonomy (§6). The contribution is the meta-ethical classification of an already-separated structure, not the discovery of the separation.

That claim survives the referee. The current one does not.

### Confidence labeling

Per the truth-over-approval discipline: the load-bearing novelty assertion ("apparently unattested") carries **no** confidence label, while incidental citations do. A negative literature claim made without searching the nearest-neighbor literature should be labeled **moderate at best** and scoped to the literatures actually surveyed.

### Bottom line

Fixable, not fatal. Cite ABLP, state precisely what it does *not* say (it never asks whether the missing premise is *deontic*), downgrade the headline from "first sighting" to "first explicit subsumption + its consequences," and label the negative-search claim. Do that and the contribution is honest, defensible, and still non-trivial.
