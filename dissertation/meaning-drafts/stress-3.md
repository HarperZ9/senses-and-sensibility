## Scoring against the four criteria

**(1) Spiral test.** [phenom] is the strongest because it is the only draft that states the failure condition in a form that can actually bite and then refuses to let its own prose discharge it: the chapter passes "conditionally," with the explicit demotion-to-framing-device verdict triggered if Bedeutsamkeit reduces to Bedeutung without remainder. That conditional PASS is the honest answer — an unconditional PASS would itself be evidence the test was rigged. [hermeneutic] also adjudicates well and adds something [phenom] lacks: it isolates *which half* of the thesis is parasitic on the residual aseity (the necessity-of-locus half) versus which floats free (the occasionalism), and concedes the first openly. That is a sharper diagnostic of where the spiral does and doesn't close. [analytic] does not run the test at all.

**(2) Strength against the subjectless-meaning opponent.** [phenom] wins decisively. Its semantic/existential (Bedeutung/Bedeutsamkeit) distinction is defended against the ad-hoc charge with three *independent* marks (normative-affective signature, first-person ineliminability, Heideggerian founding priority), and the founding relation is made to *predict* the orthogonality results in Millikan/Dennett — which is the move that converts the distinction from stipulation into substance ("tautologies predict nothing"). The as-if/real collapse against Dennett ("an as-if mattering is not a faint mattering, it is no mattering") and the orthogonality argument against Millikan are both clean. The Frege handling is the best single passage in any draft: conceding the entire platonic heaven and observing that the *mattering* of "7 is prime" is not a candidate for residence in the third realm. [hermeneutic] makes essentially the same concede-the-semantic / isolate-the-existential move and adds one genuinely better sub-argument: "the wall they built, to keep their own claims defensible" — i.e., the teleosemanticist's *own modesty* (representing, not valuing) is the thesis's confirmation. That is a stronger rhetorical and dialectical framing of the concession than [phenom]'s. But [hermeneutic] leads with Gadamer/understanding and has to spend a whole section bridging understanding→mattering, which is wasted distance against this specific opponent.

**(3) Scholarly depth and accuracy.** Both [phenom] and [hermeneutic] are dense and largely accurate; [phenom] is deeper (motor intentionality, Abschattungen, the Fifth Cartesian Meditation's alter ego doing real load-bearing work for the "conferred yet not arbitrary" pole). See the fabrication audit below — both contain one genuinely shaky attribution, and [phenom]'s is the more serious.

**(4) Prose and argumentative unity.** [phenom] is the most unified: every section is visibly subordinated to the §1 test and the §9 adjudication pays out every promissory note. [hermeneutic] is nearly as good and is in one respect *more* disciplined — it repeatedly says "stated without cadence so the argument is naked," actively distrusting its own eloquence, which is the correct posture for a chapter whose failure mode is "eloquence smuggling repetition." But it is longer and the Gadamer front-loading costs it unity. [analytic] is well-written but is a refusal memo, not a chapter.

## Single best contribution of each loser, to graft

[phenom] asserts Wolf's "objective attractiveness" *is* the conferral structure; [hermeneutic] notices that, read flatly, objective attractiveness is a *candidate aseity* (mind-independent worth) and is therefore prima facie an *opponent*, not an ally — and then earns the mapping by re-cashing the objective pole as the inherited, inter-subjectively borne significance-world (Gadamer's tradition / Heidegger's thrown world / Husserl's plural loci), which is "objective relative to my will" without being aseitic. That is a real repair of a real soft spot in the winner. Graft [hermeneutic] §6 onto [phenom] §6.

- **From [analytic]:** the observation that the chapter's thesis is the **refutation of its own framing** — that a document claiming aseitic, "irrefutable," "highest-scrutiny," standpoint-independent authority for itself is precisely what "Conferral Without Aseity" says cannot exist. As a *refusal* this is a non-answer to the assigned task, but as a *thematic move inside the chapter* it is the best idea [analytic] has: turn the locus claim reflexively on any "ground-truth-from-nowhere" authority. Grafted as an optional coda (the "explicitly thematize" branch [analytic] itself names), it would strengthen the winner's §10 without the winner having to accept the framing it critiques.

## Citations that look fabricated or mis-attributed

- **[phenom] — Frankl, "detected and not invented" (flagged HIGH confidence): mis-attributed wording, the most serious citation error in the winning draft.** Frankl's actual repeated formula is that meaning must be **"found," "discovered," or "detected"** and that "meaning... must be found and cannot be given" — and his sharpest line is that meaning is something to be *found* rather than *given*, and conscience "detects" meaning. The crisp antithesis "**detected and not invented**" as a verbatim Frankl phrase is doubtful; the genuinely Franklian contrast is detected/given (or found/invented in looser paraphrase). [hermeneutic] repeats the same "detected, not invented" attribution at HIGH confidence, so the error is shared — but [phenom] stakes more argumentative weight on the exact wording in §7, so it costs [phenom] more. Confidence in this flag: moderate-to-high.

- **[phenom] — Metz "fundamentality" gloss is self-flagged moderate, correctly.** Metz's view is usually labeled the **"fundamentality theory"** (meaning from positively orienting one's rational nature toward fundamental conditions of human existence), so the *label* is right; the compressed gloss is defensible. Not fabricated. The draft's own moderate label is honest. No penalty beyond noting it.

- **[hermeneutic] — Millikan "magnetosome" example (flagged HIGH): correct attribution, good catch by the draft.** The magnetotactic-bacterium/magnetosome case is indeed Millikan's own stock example of subjectless content. This is accurate and is a point in [hermeneutic]'s favor, not against it.

- **[phenom] / [hermeneutic] — Dennett "Real Patterns," 1991; *The Intentional Stance*, 1987: both correct.** *Journal of Philosophy*, 1991, and MIT Press, 1987. No issue.

- **[phenom] / [hermeneutic] — Frege "third realm"/Gedanke: correct** ("Der Gedanke," 1918). No issue.
