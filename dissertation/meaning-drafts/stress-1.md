The test demands: (a) a **positive** proposition (asserts something *is*, not merely *is not*), (b) **falsifiable** (a describable state of affairs would refute it), and (c) **unstatable in the first negative-metaphysical pass**. Let me adjudicate strictly.

**Draft [analytic]** and the front-matter of **[hermeneutic]** both decline/hedge on framing grounds before producing philosophy. [analytic] produces no chapter at all — it cannot pass a test about what a chapter yields. So the contest is [phenom] vs. [hermeneutic]'s actual chapter body.

VERDICT: The strongest draft is **[hermeneutic]**, and it **PASSES — but only on one of its two falsification fronts, and conditionally**, exactly as it itself concedes.

Single best candidate proposition (verbatim, from [hermeneutic] §9):

> *Existential meaning is the continuous, temporally re-bestowed significance-for that obtains only at a thrown, inhabited locus; it is withdrawable without any subtraction of fact and re-inhabitable without any addition of fact, because what loss and recovery alter is the re-speaking, not the inscription.*

Why this is the best candidate (stronger than [phenom]'s):

[phenom]'s headline proposition — "existential meaning has a necessary inhabited locus; mattering is a two-place relation whose second place is ineliminable" — is the weaker of the two, and [phenom] half-knows it. Its sole falsification condition is "subjectless mattering," and its own §9 admits the killing objection it cannot fully answer: the condition may be analytic ("mattering" glossed as "significance-for-someone," then declared to need a someone). [phenom]'s defense is that the existential/semantic distinction is a "phenomenological invariant" that "predicts independent results." But that defense is itself contestable re-description: it predicts the *orthogonality* of function to mattering, which is just the distinction restated, not an independent measurement. [phenom] earns only "conditional PASS" and the condition it names (irreducibility of the existential to the semantic) is one it concedes it cannot establish. That makes its positive content vulnerable to collapsing back into the first-pass negation ("no aseitic meaning") wearing phenomenological clothes — which is precisely the FAIL mode.

[hermeneutic]'s proposition is strictly stronger because it adds a component **[phenom] lacks**: the **occasionalism / withdrawal-without-subtraction** claim, and it correctly identifies that *this specific component* is what carries the PASS. Its decisive move is in §9: it distinguishes the two halves of its own thesis and concedes the *necessity-of-locus* half is "continuous with the residual aseity" (i.e., re-description), while isolating the *temporal re-bestowal* half as genuinely new content not derivable from the bare locus claim.

How the best candidate could be false (genuinely falsifiable — this is what earns the PASS):

The "withdrawal-without-subtraction" front is empirically refutable and not by construction: show that existential meaning-loss *always* co-varies with some loss of fact (and meaning-return with some gain of fact) — i.e., that significance never drains while the facts hold steady. The phenomena of grief/depression/conversion are real test data that could go the other way; a demonstration that significance-change is type-identical to belief-change or affect-change would sink it. This is a describable state of affairs that would refute the claim. That makes it falsifiable in a way the locus-half is not.

What is still ONLY re-description (the strict residue — and it is substantial):

1. **The entire necessity-of-locus clause** ("obtains only at a thrown, inhabited locus") is, by the draft's own admission, the residual structural aseity restated. "Meaning needs a someone" is the negative-metaphysical "no conferral from nowhere" turned around. [hermeneutic] is honest that it "inhabits" rather than "dissolves" this ground. So roughly half the headline proposition is re-description.

2. **The hermeneutic material (§2) and the Heidegger/Merleau-Ponty bridge (§4)** add no falsifiable content over the first pass — they re-describe "conferred" as "standpoint-bound understanding/embodied coping." Illuminating, but [hermeneutic] itself never claims these sections carry the PASS; they are scaffolding. As positive *testable* content they are nil.

3. The **"withdrawal-without-subtraction" claim itself flirts with re-description** unless its empirical edge is real. [hermeneutic]'s own closing note flags the unclosed gap: a "sufficiently clever Dennettian" could redescribe withdrawal-without-subtraction in subjectless functional terms (significance-state changes as functional-state changes with stable propositional facts). If that redescription succeeds, the "without subtraction of *fact*" clause smuggles in a tendentious narrowing of "fact" (propositional only), and the claim collapses to "affective re-speaking changed" — which is re-description of the locus-activity, not independent content. The draft names this gap but does not close it.

Strict bottom line:

The chapter PASSES because **one** isolable proposition — the temporal occasionalism of meaning (withdrawable without subtraction of fact / re-inhabitable without addition of fact) — is positive, names a refuting state of affairs, and is **not** generable from the first pass's static modal-negative vocabulary (which has no tense and is compatible with there being no meaning at all). That single component clears all three bars. Everything else in the proposition — the necessity-of-locus, the hermeneutic and phenomenological apparatus — is re-description, and the draft is unusually honest in conceding so. The PASS is therefore real but narrow, and stands only if the "without subtraction of *fact*" clause survives the Dennettian reduction the draft admits it has not closed. If that reduction succeeds, the PASS reverts to FAIL and the spiral demotes to a framing device.

Verdict: **PASS (narrow, conditional)** — carried entirely by the occasionalism-of-meaning clause, not by the locus claim, which is re-description.
