## Angle 1 — The Structural Isomorphism: Aseity:Being :: Solipsism:Knowing

### The thesis stated precisely

The claim is not that aseity and solipsism *resemble* each other, nor that "self-sufficiency" is a shared mood. It is that the two are the *same formal structure* instantiated in two different orders — the order of **being** (ontology) and the order of **knowing** (epistemology). The Conferred Existence framework's no-aseity thesis denies a self-standing *ground of being*; the parallel target is the denial of a self-standing *ground of knowing*. To make the isomorphism load-bearing rather than decorative, I will fix the structure first, then show that both aseity and solipsism are substitution instances of it.

**The shared structure** is a *one-place foundational asymmetry*:

> There is exactly one term **G** such that (i) G holds in virtue of itself (G is *a se* — from itself), and (ii) everything else holds in virtue of G (everything else is *ab alio* — from another, viz. from G), and (iii) the holding-in-virtue-of relation does not run back into G from anything else.

Three conditions: **self-sufficiency** of G, **dependence** of all else on G, and **asymmetry** (no return arrow). Aseity instantiates G = *being-itself* under the relation *exists-in-virtue-of*. Solipsism instantiates G = *the knowing self* under the relation *is-given-/-known-in-virtue-of*. That is the whole claim, and the rest is verification. [high — this is a definitional reconstruction, not an empirical claim]

### Aseity: self-standing *being*

*Aseitas* — from *a se esse*, "to be from itself" — is the scholastic name for the divine mode of existence that depends on nothing. It contrasts with *ab alio esse*, being-from-another, the mode of every creature. The conceptual machinery is sharpest in two figures who occupy the *poles* of how a self-standing being could be related to everything else.

**Spinoza — aseity as *causa sui*, the monistic pole.** Spinoza opens the *Ethics* (1677) with the definition: "By cause of itself I understand that whose essence involves existence, or that whose nature cannot be conceived except as existing" (E1d1). Substance is "that which is in itself and is conceived through itself" (E1d3), and there is exactly one such substance, God-or-Nature, of which all else is a *mode* — "that which is in another through which it is also conceived" (E1d5). [high] This is aseity in its purest analytic form: one term that is *in se / per se concipitur*, and a totality of modes that are *in alio / per aliud concipiuntur*. The asymmetry is explicit — modes are conceived through substance, substance through nothing further. *Causa sui* just *is* aseity rendered as a causal-conceptual predicate.

**Leibniz — aseity distributed, the pluralistic pole.** The *Monadology* (1714) gives the opposite topology. Monads are "windowless" — "the monads have no windows through which anything could enter or depart" (§7) — each a self-contained perspective that "expresses" or "mirrors" the whole universe from its point of view (§§56–57). [high] Strictly, for Leibniz only God has full aseity (the monads are created and continually "fulgurated" from the divine — §47), so the monad is *quasi*-aseitic: causally sealed (no inter-monadic influx) yet ontologically derived. This matters for the parallel: the windowless monad is the *ontic* template of which solipsism is the *epistemic* realization. A windowless perspective that contains its whole world as internal content and receives nothing from outside is, transposed into the order of knowing, precisely the solipsistic subject. The two poles — Spinoza's one self-caused substance, Leibniz's many self-contained perspectives — bracket the space the epistemic parallel will re-enact. [moderate — the "ontic template" reading is interpretive, but the textual features (windowlessness, internal expression of the whole) are uncontested]

### Solipsism: self-standing *knowing*

**Descartes — the cogito as epistemic aseity.** The *Meditations* (1641) and *Discourse* (1637) install a single indubitable term in the order of *knowledge*: "*ego sum, ego existo*" is necessarily true whenever conceived (Meditation II); "I think, therefore I am" is "so firm and sure that all the most extravagant suppositions of the skeptics were incapable of shaking it" (Discourse IV). [high] The structural point: the cogito is *the one a se in the epistemic order*. Its certainty is *from itself* — it needs no prior premise, no external warrant; it is self-evident in the strict sense that its evidence is internal to the act of entertaining it. Everything else in the *Meditations* — the external world, other minds, even God's existence as *known* — is, in the order of discovery, *ab alio*: dependent for its epistemic standing on something that traces back to this floor. The cogito is the indubitable floor precisely as *being-itself* is the unconditioned ground: it is what the regress of justification terminates in without further appeal, the epistemic analogue of the unmoved/uncaused. [high]

Note carefully what is and is not being claimed. Descartes is not a solipsist; he labors (via the divine-veracity argument) to *exit* the first-person enclosure. But the cogito *as a foundational structure* is solipsistic in form: a single self-warranting first-person term on which all other knowledge is made to depend. Solipsism is what you get if you take the cogito's *epistemic aseity* and *deny the exit* — refuse the bridge back out to the *ab alio* world, leaving the self as the sole un-conferred knower whose representations exhaust the knowable. This is the precise sense in which the cogito is the hinge of the isomorphism. [high]

**Schopenhauer — world-as-representation, solipsism made explicit.** *The World as Will and Representation* (1818) opens: "*Die Welt ist meine Vorstellung*" — "The world is my representation" (§1). The world *qua* object is given only as content for a subject; "no object without subject." Schopenhauer then names the structure directly: the consistent solipsist (the *theoretical egoist*) treats his own individual as "the only real one," regarding all others as phantoms — and he remarks that such a position, while irrefutable by proof, is found "only in a madhouse," needing "not so much a refutation as a cure" (WWR I, §19). [high] What matters for the isomorphism is that Schopenhauer has isolated the *exact epistemic counterpart* of Spinoza's monism: one self-standing subject; everything else is its *Vorstellung* — its representation, its content, *ab alio* with respect to the subjective ground. "World as my representation" is to *knowing* what "all things as modes of the one substance" is to *being*. The grammar is identical; only the order changes.

### The term-by-term mapping

| Formal role | Order of **Being** (aseity) | Order of **Knowing** (solipsism) |
|---|---|---|
| The one un-conferred ground **G** | *Being-itself* / *causa sui* / substance | The knowing self / the cogito / the subject |
| Mode of G | *a se* — from itself; *in se* | self-evident; *self*-warranting; given-in-itself |
| Everything else | *ab alio* — from another; *in alio* | representation / content / *Vorstellung* |
| Mode of the rest | dependent existence; mode of substance | dependent givenness; object-for-a-subject |
| The relation | *exists-in-virtue-of* / is-caused-by / is-conceived-through | is-known-in-virtue-of / is-given-to / is-represented-by |
| Asymmetry (no return arrow) | substance conceived through nothing further | subject not itself an object among objects |
| Terminus of the regress | uncaused cause; unconditioned condition | indubitable floor; un-justified justifier |
| Monistic pole | Spinoza's one substance | Schopenhauer's single subject |
| Pluralistic pole | Leibniz's windowless monads | the closed first-person perspective (each its own world) |

The columns are not two lists that happen to be the same length. Each row is the *same* formal role filled by an ontic term on the left and its epistemic correlate on the right, under a relation that is in both cases a directed dependence with a single source and no return. [high]

### What makes this a *structural* isomorphism, not a verbal coincidence

A verbal coincidence would obtain if "self-sufficiency" were merely a shared *word* covering two unrelated phenomena (as "bank" covers riverbank and finance). The case here is stronger, and meets three tests that distinguish structure from homonymy:

**(1) Relation-preserving bijection (the mathematician's sense of isomorphism).** An isomorphism is a one-to-one mapping φ between two structured domains that preserves the relation: if *R(x, y)* holds in the source, then *R'(φx, φy)* holds in the target. Here the mapping sends *being-itself ↦ knowing-self*, *mode ↦ representation*, and the relation *exists-in-virtue-of ↦ is-known-in-virtue-of* — and the defining formal properties of the relation are preserved across the map: it is **irreflexive at the source** (G does not depend on G via anything external — G is *a se*), **asymmetric** (no return arrow: substance is not a mode of its modes; the subject is not an object among its objects), and it has a **unique terminating source** (one ground, on which the rest one-sidedly depends). When two relations share irreflexivity-at-source, asymmetry, and unique-foundationality, and the domains map one-to-one preserving those properties, you have isomorphism in the literal order-theoretic sense, not an analogy. [high]

**(2) The poles line up under the map, unforced.** Independent confirmation that the structure is real, not imposed, is that the *internal articulation* of each side maps too. Ontology's two ways of being a self-standing ground — **one substance** with everything as its modes (Spinoza) vs. **many sealed perspectives** each containing its world (Leibniz) — reappear in epistemology as exactly two ways of being a self-standing knower: **one subject** with everything as its representation (Schopenhauer's monistic solipsism) vs. the **closed first-person perspective** as such (the windowless knower). The monism↔monism and pluralism↔pluralism correspondences were not built into the definition of G; they fall out. When a mapping preserves not just the top-level relation but the *sub-structure* (the branching into poles), the isomorphism is non-trivial. [moderate — the Leibniz→solipsism leg is the interpretively loaded one; the Spinoza→Schopenhauer leg is tight]

**(3) The same formal pathologies recur on both sides.** Isomorphic structures break in isomorphic ways. The classic objection to *causa sui* is that it is **viciously self-grounding** — to cause one's own existence one must already exist to do the causing (Hume's and Kant's shared complaint against the ontological/cosmological apparatus: existence is not a perfection or predicate that essence can secure, *Critique of Pure Reason* A592/B620ff). The classic objection to solipsism is the structurally identical charge: the self-warranting subject must **already be given to itself** to do the warranting — Hume's report that introspection finds only perceptions, "never... myself at any time without a perception," never the bare subject (*Treatise* I.iv.6); and Kant's verdict that the "I think" is a merely formal unity, *not* an intuited self-grounding object (Paralogisms, A341/B399ff). The same defect — *a ground that must presuppose itself to ground* — appears in the ontic register as the incoherence of self-causation and in the epistemic register as the elusiveness of the self-positing subject. A shared *failure mode* is the strongest possible evidence that the two are one structure, because verbal coincidences do not break in parallel. [high]

### Where the parallel must not be flattered

Three disanalogies keep the claim honest and bound its strength:

- **Direction of dependence differs in *content*.** Ontic dependence is *existential* (the mode would not *exist* without substance); epistemic dependence is *evidential/given* (the object would not be *known* without the subject). The isomorphism is of *form* (irreflexive, asymmetric, unique-source), not of the *matter* of the relation. A skeptic who holds that knowing-dependence is merely epistemic while being-dependence is metaphysical can grant the structural map and still deny that anything *ontologically* heavy transfers. The isomorphism licenses *transfer of structure*, not transfer of ontological weight. [high]

- **Descartes is the hinge, not a solipsist.** The cogito supplies *epistemic aseity* as a structure but is embedded in a system designed to defeat solipsism (divine veracity reopens the windows). Using the cogito as the epistemic-aseity term is legitimate *qua* foundational form; it would be a misreading to enlist Descartes as endorsing the enclosure. The parallel uses his *floor*, not his *building*. [high]

- **Leibniz's monad is only *quasi*-aseitic.** Genuine aseity in Leibniz belongs to God alone; the monad is windowless but created. The Leibniz→solipsism leg therefore maps a *causal-sealing* feature, not full aseity. This is the weakest joint in the mapping and should be flagged as [moderate], not asserted as [high].

### The payoff for the no-aseity framework

If aseity:being :: solipsism:knowing is a genuine isomorphism, then the framework's denial of aseity has an immediate epistemic shadow: **to deny a self-standing ground of being is, by the structure-preserving map, to be committed to denying a self-standing ground of knowing** — no un-conferred knower, no cogito that is *a se*, no subject that is not itself given (re-spoken, in the framework's idiom) from elsewhere. The Humean-Kantian discovery that the self-positing subject is never *found* as a self-grounding object is then not a separate result but the *epistemic image* of no-aseity: the knowing self, like being, has "no aseity yet is no less real" — it is real *as inhabited*, given to itself in the act, never as a self-standing floor underneath the act. The "trained-on-testimony seal" and the "necessary inhabited locus" are the framework's own names for what falls out here: the knower is always *a someone occupying a standpoint*, never a view from nowhere that grounds itself — the epistemic correlate of *esse ab alio*. The isomorphism is what licenses reading the one thesis off the other. [moderate — this is the framework-internal inference the parallel is built to support; its soundness rides on test (1) above holding, which it does for *form* but not for *matter*]
