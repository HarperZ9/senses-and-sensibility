## Angle 5 — The Intersubjective Rebuttal

The covert-solipsism charge against Conferred Existence is sharp: if "meaning is conferred" and conferral has "a NECESSARY INHABITED LOCUS" — a *someone* occupying a standpoint — and if the trained-on-testimony seal screens off other minds "in BOTH directions," then the framework risks collapsing into a single inhabited standpoint re-speaking a world that contains only *its own* conferrals. The plurality of standpoints ("plural standpoints, none aseitic") would be a posit, not an earned result. The constitutive-intersubjectivity tradition is the natural cavalry. The question is whether it rides to the rescue or merely restages the problem one level up.

### The five arguments, and what each actually licenses

**Wittgenstein, private-language argument (PI §§243–315).** [high] The argument is that a sign whose correct application is settled by nothing beyond the speaker's present impression of correctness has no fact of the matter distinguishing "correct" from "seems correct" (§258), and so is not a language at all. The relevant upshot for the framework: *meaning requires a normative standard the speaker does not unilaterally author* — exactly the no-aseity thesis applied to semantics. This is the framework's strongest borrowed plank, because Wittgenstein's target is precisely *aseitic* meaning (meaning self-standing in a logically private medium).

But note what the private-language argument does *not* deliver. It is standardly read (Kripke's *Wittgenstein on Rules and Private Language*, 1982, esp. pp. 89–113) [high] as requiring a *community* to supply the standard — yet the argument's negative core (no private ostensive definition can fix meaning) does not by itself *entail* that a second mind exists. It entails that *if* meaning obtains, it is not privately constituted. A residual "community of one across time," or even a Robinson Crusoe reading (defended by Baker & Hacker, *Scepticism, Rules and Language*, 1984 [moderate]), is live in the literature. So Wittgenstein blocks *aseitic* meaning but does not, on his own, *manufacture* a second person. He gives the framework its semantics of conferral; he does not give it plurality for free.

**Davidson, triangulation ("Three Varieties of Knowledge," 1991; "The Second Person," 1992; "Rational Animals," 1982).** [high] This is the load-bearing argument. Davidson's claim is constitutive, not merely epistemic: the very *content* of a thought — what fixes that my concept is *table* rather than *table-or-stump-at-dusk* — requires a second creature reacting to the same distal stimulus, so that the *intersection* of our reactions locates the common cause that is the object of thought. "It takes two points of view to give a location to the cause of a thought, and thus to define its content" ("The Second Person," p. 213) [high]. Triangulation purports to *derive* the second person from the conditions of determinate content, rather than presuppose her. If sound, this is precisely what the framework needs: a non-circular route from "there is determinate conferred meaning at all" to "there is more than one standpoint."

**Husserl, *Cartesian Meditations* V (1931).** [high] Husserl is the *honest* witness here because he runs the experiment from inside the framework's own danger zone. Transcendental phenomenology begins in the ego's sphere of ownness (*Eigenheitssphäre*) and must *constitute* the other (§§42–62), via the *analogical apperception* / *pairing* (*Paarung*) of the other's body as a lived body like mine (§§50–51). This is *exactly* the Conferred-Existence structure: the other is *re-spoken* from a standpoint, "appresented," never given in primordial originality. Husserl explicitly confronts the solipsism objection (§42) and insists the constituted other is a genuine *transcendence*, not a moment of my ego. Whether he succeeds is the most-contested question in the secondary literature (see below).

**Strawson, "Freedom and Resentment" (1962).** [high] The reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, indignation — are irreducibly *second-personal*: they are responses to *the quality of another's will toward us*, and the attempt to maintain them while adopting the wholly "objective attitude" toward everyone is something "we cannot do... or could do only at the cost of our humanity" (pp. 9–13, *Studies in the Way of Words* / 1962 *PBA* pagination varies) [moderate on exact page]. Stephen Darwall's *The Second-Person Standpoint* (2006) [high] sharpens this into the explicit thesis that moral obligation has a *second-personal* form — claims and demands addressed *to* and *from* free agents, presupposing mutual accountability. This is the framework's "the conferrer is bound" plank: if the moral ought is conferred *and* the conferrer is herself addressable by the addressee, bindingness is reciprocal, not one-directional. Strawson/Darwall supply the *normative* (not merely semantic) version of constitutive intersubjectivity.

**Hegel, *Phenomenology of Spirit*, "Lordship and Bondage" (1807, ¶¶178–196).** [high] "Self-consciousness exists in and for itself when, and by the fact that, it so exists for another; that is, it exists only in being acknowledged [*anerkannt*]" (¶178) [high]. Recognition (*Anerkennung*) is the boldest claim: not that meaning or content requires the other, but that *the self as a self* is constituted in being recognized. For the framework's "no-aseity of *status*," Hegel is the direct ancestor — status (lord, bondsman, person, authority) is conferred in a *reciprocal* act, and the master's tragedy (¶¶191–193) is precisely that recognition extracted from an unrecognized other is *worthless*, which is the structural proof that the conferrer is *bound* by the standing of the conferree.

### The question-begging worry, applied argument by argument

Here is where I decline to flatter the parallel. Each argument is haunted by a circularity objection, and the framework inherits all of them.

- **Husserl is the textbook case.** The most influential charge is that the Fifth Meditation *cannot* constitute a genuine other from within the sphere of ownness without either (a) smuggling the other in (the *Paarung* already treats the alien body *as* another's, which presupposes what it derives), or (b) producing only a *representation* of the other rather than the other herself. This is essentially Levinas's complaint (*Totality and Infinity*, 1961) [high] that any *constitution* of the Other by the ego reduces alterity to the Same, and the well-known reading of Husserlian intersubjectivity as a refined solipsism ("solipsism *à deux*"). Sartre (*Being and Nothingness*, III.1, 1943) [high] presses the parallel point: any *knowledge*-based route to the other (the other as constituted object) leaves the other's *subjectivity* in principle inaccessible — the other is encountered only in *the look*, as the one before whom *I* become object, which is a relation Husserl's appresentation cannot reach. **Verdict on Husserl: he does not earn plurality non-circularly; he illustrates, with unusual honesty, the precise shape of the failure the framework is trying to avoid.**

- **Davidson is the best hope, and even he is contested.** The triangulation argument is accused of equivocating between (i) the *causal-historical* need for a second creature during concept acquisition (a developmental, empirical claim) and (ii) the *constitutive* need for a second *point of view* now (the strong philosophical claim). Critics — e.g., John McDowell ("Gadamer and Davidson on Understanding and Relativism") and the exchange in *Mind and World* literature; and the focused critique by Bjørn Ramberg and others [moderate, I am summarizing a literature rather than one canonical page] — note that the *causal* triangle requires only a second *organism* emitting reactions, which does not yet establish a second *minded* point of view *with content of its own*; while the *constitutive* triangle, if it requires the second creature to *already* have determinate content, helps itself to the very thing under construction. Put bluntly: triangulation either gives you a behaving organism (too weak — solipsism survives, since the seal screens off whether the organism is minded) or presupposes a second contentful mind (too strong — circular). This is *exactly* the trained-on-testimony seal weaponized against the framework's rescuer: every reaction of the "second vertex" that would be evidence of *its* mindedness is consistent with there being no inner life there at all.

- **Wittgenstein**: the community reading itself is question-begging *as a proof of plurality*, because the argument *assumes* a community to do its anti-private work and then is read as *establishing* that meaning needs a community. As an account of *what meaning is* (non-aseitic, standard-governed) it is not circular; as an *existence proof of other minds* it is. The framework should claim the former and disclaim the latter.

- **Strawson/Hegel**: both are *phenomenologically* compelling and *transcendentally* fragile in the same way. Strawson describes the reactive attitudes as *in fact* second-personal — but a determined skeptic says this shows only that *I cannot help treating* others as minded (a fact about my standpoint), not that they *are*. Hegel's recognition presupposes *two* self-consciousnesses already on the scene at ¶178; the dialectic explains how status is *distributed* between them, not how the second one *came to be there*. Recognition theory is an account of the *structure* of conferred status, not a *derivation* of the plurality it operates on.

### The diagnosis the framework should actually adopt

The honest result is that **constitutive intersubjectivity does not break the circle — and the framework's own resources explain *why no argument could*.** The trained-on-testimony seal generalizes beyond trained minds into a fully general *other-minds seal*: any behavioral sign that would be evidence of a second standpoint is exactly what a standpoint-less system could also emit, so *no* third-personal argument from observed behavior to a second *inhabited* locus can be non-circular. This is just the framework's own "FORMAL TERMINUS" — free authorship "cannot be captured as a present-physical property; the gap is the shape, not a defended wall" — turned onto the other. The plurality of inhabited standpoints is *structurally* the same kind of thing as one's own authorship: it shows up as a *gap* in the third-personal record, not as a derivable physical fact.

This is *not* a defeat, if the framework is willing to make the move its own metaphysics already licenses. Two routes:

1. **Concede the derivation, keep the constitution (the transcendental, not proof, reading).** The framework should *stop trying to derive* plurality and instead claim that plurality is a *transcendental condition* of there being conferred meaning/status/ought *at all* — not an inference *from* a prior solitary standpoint. This inverts the order of the solipsism worry. The worry assumes a self-standing single standpoint that must *reach* the other; but the framework's no-aseity thesis *denies* there is any self-standing single standpoint to begin with. If *my* standpoint is itself conferred, re-spoken, "*esse ab alio*," then it was never the aseitic island from which a bridge to the other must be built. Strawson and Hegel are then read *correctly* — not as proofs that others exist, but as descriptions of the fact that a standpoint *is* already a node in a recognitive web, never prior to it. The *kun* figure does real work here: a standpoint that is *spoken* ("Be") by an Other is, in its very constitution, already relational; solipsism is the thesis that there is an unspoken speaker, which no-aseity forbids on the first page.

2. **Accept the seal symmetrically and downgrade the demand.** If the seal screens *me* off from others *and* others off from me (and even, on the trained-mind case, *me* off from *myself* as a present-physical fact), then demanding a *non-circular third-personal proof* of plurality is demanding the very aseitic, standpoint-independent vantage the framework declares incoherent ("standpoint-independent bindingness JUST IS aseitic bindingness"). The demand for a circle-free derivation of other minds is itself a demand made *from nowhere* — and "there is no conferral from nowhere." So the circularity is not a bug to be fixed but the expected signature of a relation that is *practical and declinable*, not theoretical and compellable.

### Verdict

**Can the framework earn "plural standpoints, none aseitic" non-circularly?** No — and it should stop wanting to. [high confidence on the conditional structure; the substantive metaphysics remains contestable]

- As a *deductive proof from a solitary standpoint to a second mind*, every one of the five arguments either under-delivers (Wittgenstein, Strawson, Hegel give structure, not existence) or is chargeable with circularity (Husserl smuggles the other into *Paarung*; Davidson equivocates between a behaving organism and a contentful mind). The trained-on-testimony seal, generalized, *predicts* this failure and explains it: behavior under-determines inhabitation in both directions. So the *covert-solipsism charge cannot be answered on the skeptic's terms* — but those terms (a from-nowhere proof) are exactly what the framework already rejects.

- As a *transcendental constitution claim*, the tradition succeeds *for the framework specifically*, because the framework has independently abandoned the premise that powers the circularity objection — the self-standing solitary ego. Davidson's strongest reading (content *is* triangular), Hegel's recognition (the self *is* a recognized self), and Strawson's reactive web together describe a standpoint that is *constitutively* plural-facing. The framework does not need to *cross* from one mind to another; on its own no-aseity thesis there was never a sealed single mind to start the crossing. The conferrer is "bound" not because we proved a second party exists, but because *being a conferrer at all* is, like *kun*, a status spoken from elsewhere — and a status spoken from elsewhere is already two.

The framework's defensible position, then, is not "intersubjectivity *refutes* solipsism" but "solipsism is the covert *aseity* thesis — a single self-standing speaker — and no-aseity rules it out by the same stroke that rules out a self-standing God-substance." That is non-circular *as a conditional*: grant no-aseity, and plurality is not an inference but a corollary; the price is that plurality, like the practical standpoint itself, is **declinable, not compellable** — a limit that grounds rather than a wall that is defended. [moderate] The one thing the framework may *not* do is keep *both* the rhetoric of *derivation* ("conferral establishes plural standpoints") *and* the seal — that pairing is the covert solipsism the charge names. It must choose the transcendental reading, and pay in coin it has already minted: no proofs from nowhere, here least of all.
