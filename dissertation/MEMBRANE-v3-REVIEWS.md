### Issue 1 — §7.2 disclaimer is still over-broad (substantive; sentence-level fix)

**What is broken.** Section 4.3 correctly locates the live normative entry at the human act of policy stipulation and characterizes that act as prescriptive in the full Hare/Hudson endorsement sense. This is necessary for the boomerang resolution. Section 7.2 then states: "the framing does not claim that the authorization 'ought' has prescriptive force on human agents." Policy stipulation is a human act within the authorization domain. So the framing does make a prescriptive claim — about the stipulator, not the PDP verdict. The appended sentence at the end of §7.2 ("the live prescriptive entry appears one level up: at the human act of policy stipulation") is the right content but does not repair the over-broad disclaimer sentence that precedes it. A cold referee reads the disclaimer, then reads §4.3, and concludes the paper contradicts itself.

**Fix.** Replace the disclaimer sentence with: "The framing does not claim that the policy's permission verdict — the PDP's 'permit' output — has prescriptive force on human agents; that verdict is a policy-relative classification that binds no one and motivates nothing directly. The live prescriptive entry, where the Hare/Hudson endorsement analysis applies, is the human act of policy stipulation (§4.3)." The disclaimer is then scoped correctly to the PDP verdict, not to the authorization domain in general.

### Issue 2 — The "and only from it" clause is asserted, not argued (substantive; paragraph of new argument required)

**What is broken.** Section 3.6 states: "From that classification, and only from it, follow: (1) the unmeasurability result... (2) the failure taxonomy." This is the paper's load-bearing novelty assertion. Section 6.1 argues that the engineering slogan does not "directly generate" the unmeasurability result and distinguishes the architectural recommendation ("keep PDP and PIP separate") from the logical theorem ("you could not collapse them even if you wanted to"). This distinction is correct but asserted. The paper does not exhibit a case where reasoning from the engineering architecture and reasoning from the deontic framing reach different conclusions, which is what would demonstrate non-equivalence rather than merely assert it.

For the unmeasurability result, the argument is defensible: the engineering recommendation is normative-about-design; the unmeasurability result is a claim about inferential form. But the paper needs to make this explicit — show that PDP/PIP necessity says "maintain separation as a design discipline" while the deontic framing says "the deontic operator cannot be generated by any descriptive base, regardless of how that base is organized." Those are genuinely different claims with different logical status.

For the failure taxonomy, the gap is larger. Hardy (1988) characterizes confused-deputy entirely from engineering description. The ambient-authority and token-scoping literatures do the same for their respective patterns. The paper asserts that the deontic framing unifies these under "a single formal structure" but never states that the engineering literature treats them as distinct failure modes requiring separate remedies. Without that premise, the unification claim has no target to unify against.

**Fix for unmeasurability (§6.1).** Add one sentence making the logical-status distinction explicit: "The PDP/PIP separation is an engineering recommendation whose justification is pragmatic (maintainability, auditability, reuse); the unmeasurability result is a consequence of inferential form (the deontic operator cannot be introduced by any descriptive base), whose justification is logical. An engineer could accept PDP/PIP necessity as a design constraint while believing that sufficiently strong authentication would eventually substitute for policy — the unmeasurability result rules this out at the level of inferential structure, which the engineering recommendation does not."

**Fix for taxonomy (§6.2).** Add one paragraph before the three case analyses: "Hardy (1988), the ambient-authority literature (Miller, Shapiro), and OAuth/XACML architectural documentation each characterizes its respective failure pattern independently, with independent remedies: per-request authorization checks for confused-deputy, capability discipline for ambient authority, server-side token validation for token-as-fact-vs-grant. The deontic framing reveals their common formal structure — an IS-predicate serving as premise for a permission conclusion the autonomy of the deontic bars it from supplying — and from that common structure follows a single class-level defensive criterion (no IS-fact may serve directly as authorization premise without an explicit deontic bridge, instantiated as PIP/PDP separation enforced as a design invariant) rather than three independently discovered patches."

### Issue 3 — Hare/Hudson endorsement parallel to institutional actors is assumed (minor; one paragraph or footnote)

**What is broken.** The Hare (1964) analysis was formulated for the individual promisor whose personal endorsement of the promising institution supplies the suppressed prescriptive premise. The paper applies this to "system administrators, security architects, and organizational decision-makers" without arguing that institutional normative acts have the same endorsement structure as individual ones. This gap is not fatal — a plausible parallel argument exists — but the paper asserts the parallel rather than making it.

**Fix.** Add a footnote or parenthetical in §4.3: "The application of Hare/Hudson to institutional actors requires that organizational normative commitments carry analogous endorsement structure to individual promising. The parallel is defensible: an organizational policy decision is not a mere registration of fact but a choice about what ought to be permitted, binding the organization to maintain the policy as a normative constraint on system behavior — the institutional analog of the promisor's subscription to the promising institution. A full treatment of institutional agency is beyond this paper's scope."

### Issue 5 — Logic system unspecified (minor; one clause)

**What is broken.** Section 2.1 states the autonomy of the deontic "has the status of a theorem about the conservativeness of standard deontic logics." This is correct for monotonic deontic logics (KD and standard extensions). It does not hold for defeasible or non-monotonic deontic logics. A metaethics or formal-logic referee will ask which system is operative.

**Fix.** In §2.1, add a parenthetical after "standard deontic logics": "(specifically, monotonic systems in the von Wright tradition; defeasible deontic logics are not at issue here, as access-control policy evaluation does not require defeasible reasoning for the Membrane Framing's claims)."

### Decisive issues (v2 problems fixed in v3)

**1. "And only from it" — the taxonomy half is not defended against the ocap community's prior design-level unification.**

The abstract, §3.6, and §9 all assert that the failure taxonomy follows "only from" the deontic naming. For the unmeasurability result this claim is defensible: the type-gap argument (ABLP establishes non-entailment but cannot classify whether the gap is an information deficit or a type deficit; the deontic naming is what answers that) is sound and survives hostile reading.

For the failure taxonomy, the "only from it" is not defended against the existing design-level unification in capability-security research. The fix has two parts: (a) hedge the "only from it" clause consistently across abstract, §3.6, and §9 to "so far as the literatures surveyed establish, only from the deontic naming"; and (b) add a paragraph in §6.2 after the class-level invariant explicitly acknowledging the ocap community's prior design-level unification and distinguishing it from the paper's inference-type unification. The distinction is: the ocap unification is design-level ("use unforgeable references, require explicit grants") — it identifies what to build without making the formal structure of the error explicit. The deontic framing's unification is inference-type — all three failures are instances of a single illicit inference pattern (IS-predicate as authorization premise) — and the class-level defensive criterion follows from naming the inference type, not from the engineering history of the failures. These are compatible; the deontic one is more abstract and generates the invariant from logical structure rather than from accumulated failure experience. Both parts of the fix are applied in v3.

**2. §6.1 ABLP gap-type argument is asserted rather than demonstrated.**

§6.1 claims ABLP "has no resource for distinguishing a measurement deficit from a logical one." This is the right claim but it is stated as a conclusion, not argued. ABLP's modal logic does establish non-entailment; the question a hostile referee will press is why that non-entailment result does not itself distinguish information-type from type-type gaps. The answer — ABLP does not ask what *kind* of object the missing premise is, so it cannot classify the gap as type-based rather than information-based — needs to be stated explicitly. Fix: one sentence making explicit that ABLP establishes *that* the ACL premise is not derivable but does not answer *why* (information deficit vs. type deficit), and that the deontic naming is what provides the answer. Applied in v3.

**3. §5.2 — the "materialized grant" formulation invites the "reading normativity in from outside" objection.**

Calling a capability "an ought made bearer-portable" is philosophically evocative but will draw the objection that a capability is a bit-pattern with hardware-enforced access control — a purely descriptive object. The paper's available response is correct (the token's *content*, what it authorizes, is the record of a prior normative decision, not a signal-property of the holder) but was not stated in v2. The fix distinguishes capability *validity* (a purely descriptive IS-fact: is this a genuine unforgeable reference?) from capability *content* (what operations it authorizes, which is the record of a prior normative decision). Applied in v3.

**4. §2.2 — no forward-reference to §4 at the Searle concession.**

§2.2 states the Searle move as a concession ("a policy-relative permission is in principle derivable from is-facts plus the descriptive fact that policy P is in force") without indicating that a response is coming. A linear reader encounters what looks like an undefended concession. Fix: "— stated here as setup, addressed fully in §4." Applied in v3.
