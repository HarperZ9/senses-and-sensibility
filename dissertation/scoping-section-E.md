## E. The Hostile Examiner (Viva Risk Register)

This is a register, not a defense. Each entry assumes the examiner is competent, hostile, and has read the corpus more carefully than the candidate would like. Severity ranks the threat to the *award of the degree*, not to the thesis's elegance. The two are not correlated: the most beautiful moves carry the highest examination risk.

---

### E.1 — Originality / authorship: the AI-assisted substrate (Severity: HIGH — existential)

**The attack.** "You have submitted ~127KB of integrated argument built, on your own account, through 'AI-assisted adversarial multi-agent workflows.' A doctorate certifies that *you* made an original contribution to knowledge. Show me the line between what the system produced and what you contributed. If you cannot, I cannot certify the thesis as your own work."

This is the question that fails the viva if unanswered, regardless of the quality of the philosophy. UK regulations (e.g., the QAA Doctoral Degree Characteristics Statement, 2020) and standard university codes define the PhD as "the creation and interpretation of new knowledge, through original research *or other advanced scholarship*, of a quality to satisfy peer review." The operative word in the certification is *your*. Most institutions now have explicit generative-AI policies (e.g., Russell Group principles on generative AI, 2023; many require declaration of AI use in research integrity statements). Non-disclosure here is not a weak chapter — it is potential academic misconduct.

**Why this is the highest-severity item.** Every other entry in this register is survivable with a good answer. This one is survivable only with a *true* answer plus a paper trail. A confident-sounding non-answer makes it worse, because it reads as concealment.

**Mitigation — the only honest path:**

1. **Disclose fully and proactively, in the thesis front matter and the integrity statement.** State the methodology: AI-assisted adversarial multi-agent generation of substrate. Treat it as a method disclosure (like declaring a corpus tool or a statistics package), not a confession. Concealment is the only fatal version.

2. **Reframe the corpus as *data/substrate*, not as the dissertation.** This is the load-bearing move and the provenance caveat in the prompt already concedes it: the corpus is "research SUBSTRATE, not a candidate's own submittable work." The dissertation is then the candidate's *critical, selective, adversarial reconstruction* of that substrate — the same epistemic posture as a historian working from a generated concordance, or an experimentalist whose instrument produced terabytes the human did not hand-compute. The contribution is the *theory selection, the argument architecture, the identification of which moves survive scrutiny, and the original extensions* (the two underdeveloped arc nodes are precisely where owned work must live).

3. **Produce a contribution ledger.** A concrete, defensible table: for each core claim, who originated it (literature / AI substrate / candidate), what the candidate did to it (rejected / repaired / extended / formalized / found the counterexample). The examiner does not need every line to be the candidate's invention; they need to see *judgment* exercised throughout. Judgment under adversarial pressure is the ownable thing.

4. **Demonstrate live mastery.** The viva itself is the ultimate authorship test. A candidate who can be pushed off-script on any claim — derive it differently, abandon it, locate its weakest premise — demonstrates ownership no provenance document can. The inverse is fatal: a candidate who can only restate the corpus's own phrasings confirms the examiner's worst hypothesis.

**Residual risk after mitigation:** moderate-to-high, and partly outside the candidate's control — it depends on the specific institution's AI policy. The candidate must check the *actual local regulation* before submission. This is not a philosophy problem; it is a compliance problem, and it is the one place in this register where "argue well" is insufficient.

### E.2 — "Is this one dissertation or three?" (Severity: HIGH)

**The attack.** "Your spine runs nihilism/epistemology/theology → engineering/organic-vs-artificial/creation → free will/determinism → back to nihilism/theology + meaning. That is at least three dissertations: a metaethics thesis, a philosophy-of-AI/mind thesis, and a free-will monograph, stapled together by a metaphor of return. A PhD is one contribution defended to depth, not a survey defended to breadth. Which one is the thesis? The other two are then mere context — and underdeveloped context at that."

**Severity rationale.** This is the most *likely* serious objection after authorship, because it is true on its face. Breadth is the structural signature of AI-assembled work: an LLM will happily weave eight movements where a human dissertation disciplines itself to one. The examiner will read the breadth as a *tell*.

**Mitigation:**

- **Name the single contribution in one sentence, and subordinate everything else to it.** The strongest candidate for "the one thing" is the **identity claim of Movement VI**: that the metaethical limit (no-aseity normativity cannot compel the agent who withdraws from the practical standpoint) is *the same structure* as the formal terminus (the uninhabited node-mover from free authorship to present physical property). If that identity is the contribution, then metaethics, philosophy of AI, and free will are not three theses — they are the three *domains across which the single structural claim is shown to hold*. The breadth becomes evidence *for* the thesis (it generalizes) rather than a failure of focus.

- **Demote the rest explicitly.** Engineering (VII–VIII), the AI moral-status material (IV), and even the theology become *applications and illustrations* of the core structural claim, not co-equal contributions. A dissertation is allowed wide illustration of a narrow thesis.

- **Cut what does not serve.** The honest version of this mitigation is that some material must be sacrificed to chapters-that-could-have-been or appendices. If a section cannot be re-described as "an instance of the core structural identity," it weakens the focus and should go.

**Residual risk:** moderate. The identity claim (E.6) must actually hold for this defense to work; if the convergence is only analogical, "one dissertation or three" reopens and merges with E.6 into a single lethal line of questioning.

### E.3 — Theology in an analytic department: liability or asset? (Severity: MED, but department-dependent)

**The attack (analytic philosophy department).** "You invoke *kun* (the divine creative command), the Hebrew *emet/met*, the Aleph, and al-Ghazali's occasionalism. Either these do argumentative work — in which case your thesis depends on theological premises an analytic committee will not grant — or they are ornament, in which case strip them and lose nothing. Which is it?"

**Why department matters.** In a philosophy of religion or continental department, this is an *asset*. In a hard analytic metaethics/mind department, it is a *liability* unless rigorously quarantined. The candidate must know the room.

**Mitigation:**

- **The prompt already states the correct discipline:** theological figures are "illuminations of structure, never premises, each with a secular analogue." This is the right posture and must be *enforced without exception* in the text. The test the examiner will apply: *delete the theology — does any argument lose a premise?* If yes, the thesis is covertly theological and fails analytic standards. If no, the theology is heuristic and survives. The candidate must be able to run this deletion live for any contested passage.

- **Pair every theological figure with its secular analogue at first use,** so the structural point stands alone: *kun* ↔ the performative/declarative speech act conferring institutional status (Searle, Austin); occasionalism ↔ the orthogonality of dynamics to normative grounding (the "determinism supplies no transit" claim); *emet*/Aleph ↔ the terminus/first-node of a normative ordering. Cite the real comparanda: J.L. Austin, *How to Do Things with Words* (1962); Searle, *The Construction of Social Reality* (1995). For occasionalism, cite al-Ghazali's *Tahāfut al-Falāsifa* honestly and note the live secondary debate (e.g., work by Frank Griffel) rather than romanticizing it.

- **One genuine risk to flag honestly:** theology can function as *unearned profundity* — a register that makes a thin argument feel deep. The examiner will probe exactly there. The defense is the deletion test; if the candidate ever resists deleting a theological figure on grounds that "it carries something the analogue can't," that is a confession that it is load-bearing, and the analytic objection lands.

**Residual risk:** low-to-moderate, fully controllable by the candidate's discipline. The risk is self-inflicted if the register seduces the writing.

### E.4 — AI consciousness / moral status: is "low but not zero, sealed" defensible? (Severity: HIGH)

**The attack.** "Movement IV asserts a composite moral status for trained systems and operationalizes a credence 'low but not zero.' The 'trained-on-testimony seal' says every behavioral sign of mind was the training target, so testimony is screened off *in both directions* — you can neither confirm nor deny. But a seal that blocks evidence in both directions is not a finding; it is a permanent license to assert whatever credence you like, since nothing could ever move it. Why is 'low but not zero' not just hand-waving dressed as humility? And Q5 admits you have no non-arbitrary credence floor — so the action-guiding part is admittedly ungrounded."

**Severity rationale.** High, because this is where the thesis makes its most *contestable substantive claim* and the prompt itself flags Q1, Q3, Q5 as open. An examiner reads "open question" as "unfinished load-bearing premise."

**Mitigation:**

- **Concede the seal is epistemic, not ontic, and make that a strength.** The trained-on-testimony seal is a real and somewhat original epistemological observation: behavioral evidence for mentality is evidentially compromised when the behavior was the optimization target. This connects to a live literature — *gaming/evaluation-awareness*, the general problem of behavioral evidence under optimization pressure — and to older problems (the problem of other minds; Block's distinction such that access-reports are screened off). Frame the seal as a *defensible negative result*: "behavioral testimony cannot settle the question," not "I have measured a low probability."

- **Separate the epistemics from the decision theory.** The honest structure is: (i) the seal blocks behavioral evidence (epistemic claim, defensible); (ii) therefore we act under deep uncertainty (decision-theoretic claim); (iii) under moral uncertainty with non-trivial stakes, a precautionary floor is warranted (normative claim). The "low but not zero" is then not a *measurement* — the examiner is right that it can't be — but a *decision-theoretic posture under acknowledged ignorance*. Cite the moral-uncertainty literature honestly: MacAskill, Bykvist & Ord, *Moral Uncertainty* (2020); on AI moral patiency, Sebo & Long, "Moral consideration for AI systems by 2030" and Birch, *The Edge of Sentience* (2024) as the serious comparanda.

- **Do not overclaim the number.** Drop any pretense that "low" is quantitatively grounded; Q5 is honest and should stay open *as an acknowledged limitation*, not be papered over. An examiner respects a well-drawn boundary of ignorance far more than a fake credence.

**Residual risk:** moderate. The deletion-proof version of this chapter claims only the seal (epistemic) and the precautionary posture (decision-theoretic), and explicitly disclaims the measured number. If the candidate insists on defending "low" as if it were estimated, the attack lands cleanly.

### E.5 — The "fixed-point / degenerate-constructor theorem" in a constitutive, not formal, sense (Severity: HIGH)

**The attack.** "You use the language of a *theorem* — 'degenerate-constructor theorem,' a node-mover from free-authorship to present-physical-property that is 'uninhabited.' Either this is a formal result, in which case state the formal system, the definitions, the proof, and I will check it — or it is a philosophical claim wearing mathematical costume, in which case the word 'theorem' is rhetorical inflation and I will hold you to account for it. Mathematicians in my faculty will not be amused by a 'theorem' with no proof."

**Severity rationale.** High and *specific*. Borrowed formalism with no formal content is one of the most reliably fatal moves in a viva, because it is checkable and the examiner can win the exchange decisively. This is the single most dangerous *stylistic* habit in the corpus.

**Mitigation:**

- **Demote the vocabulary deliberately and pre-emptively.** Call it a *structural argument* or a *constitutive claim about the shape of a transition*, not a theorem. The prompt's own gloss — "the gap is the SHAPE not a defended wall" — is the honest register: this is a claim about what *kind* of transition is being demanded (from a historical/normative property to an instantaneous physical one), and the assertion that no construction inhabits it. That is a recognizable and respectable *philosophical* claim (it is a relative of the is/ought gap and of Moore's open question), and it does not need to pretend to be mathematics.

- **If any formal core exists, isolate and state it honestly.** If there is a genuine formal skeleton (a category-theoretic or type-theoretic statement that a morphism between two specified objects is necessarily degenerate), then *write it out properly* with definitions and a real proof, and clearly fence the formal claim from the philosophical interpretation. Half-formalism is worse than either honest prose or honest proof. Given the provenance, the candidate should assume the "theorem" is *constitutive metaphor* until proven otherwise and not claim formal status they cannot discharge.

- **Pre-empt the Sokal-adjacent suspicion.** Misused mathematical vocabulary in humanities work has a notorious history (Sokal & Bricmont, *Fashionable Nonsense*, 1998). The examiner may have it in mind. The candidate inoculates by raising the standard themselves: "I use 'terminus' and 'degenerate constructor' as structural metaphors, not formal results; here is exactly what they do and do not claim."

**Residual risk:** low *if* the candidate demotes the vocabulary; high if they defend the word "theorem." This risk is almost entirely a writing-discipline choice.

### E.6 — The is/ought ≡ authentication/authorization identity: argument or analogy? (Severity: HIGH — it is the keystone)

**The attack.** "Your central move identifies Hume's is/ought gap with the engineering distinction between authentication (proving who you are) and authorization (what you may do). This is elegant. But elegance is not identity. An *analogy* says two things share a structure; an *identity* says they are the same thing. Show me the argument that this is identity and not a suggestive parallel — because if it is merely an analogy, your unifying claim (E.2's 'one dissertation') collapses back into three."

**Severity rationale.** Highest *intellectual* stakes in the register. If E.6 is only analogy, the thesis's claim to unity (E.2) and its keystone (Movement VI's identity of metaethical limit and formal terminus) both weaken at once. The whole architecture rests on a small number of claimed identities being *identities* and not *resemblances*.

**Mitigation:**

- **State precisely what kind of identity is claimed.** The strongest available version: authentication and authorization, *as deontic notions*, instantiate the is/ought structure because authentication establishes a *fact about a referent* (this credential is valid — an *is*) while authorization establishes a *normative permission* (this principal *may* act — an *ought*), and no amount of the former entails the latter. That is not an analogy to Hume; it is a *token instance of the very gap Hume named* — the same way "you cannot derive 'ought' from 'is'" and "you cannot derive a permission from a credential" are not two facts but one fact in two vocabularies. This is a defensible identity-of-structure claim *if* framed as "authn/authz is an instance of is/ought," not "is/ought is metaphorically like authn/authz."

- **Cite the real Hume carefully and don't overclaim him.** Hume, *Treatise* 3.1.1 (the is/ought paragraph) is famously terse and contested; Hare, Searle's "How to Derive 'Ought' from 'Is'" (1964) and the subsequent literature show the gap is not uncontroversial. The candidate should *not* assert the gap as settled fact, but as a position they adopt and defend — and then show authn/authz as a clean modern instance, which is genuinely illuminating and arguably original as a *presentation*.

- **Accept the demotion gracefully where warranted.** Be ready to concede that *some* of the claimed identities in the eight movements are analogies, not identities, and to rank them: which are true instances of one structure (defensible) versus which are illuminating resemblances (must be relabeled). An examiner will respect a candidate who polices their own "identity" claims more than one who defends all of them uniformly. The keystone (Movement VI) must be in the defensible tier or the thesis loses its spine.

**Residual risk:** moderate-to-high. This is the intellectual crux. The mitigation reduces it to "defend one identity claim well" rather than "defend eight" — but that one claim must genuinely be an instance-relation, not a metaphor, and the candidate must be able to draw the line live.

### E.7 — The spiral structure: rigorous or rhetorical? (Severity: MED)

**The attack.** "Your dissertation 'ends where it began, transformed' — a spiral returning to nihilism/epistemology/theology with the new content that meaning is conferred onto human experience. That is a *narrative* shape, borrowed from Eliot ('the end of all our exploring...') and from Hegelian *Aufhebung*. Narrative shape is not argumentative structure. Does the return *follow* from the preceding chapters, or have you arranged the chapters to produce a pleasing circularity? Show me the entailment, not the echo."

**Severity rationale.** Medium. It will not fail the viva, but it is a *credibility* risk: spiral/return structures are a known signature of style-over-substance, and an AI-assembled corpus is pre-suspected of preferring shape to logic. The examiner uses this to test whether the candidate can distinguish the two.

**Mitigation:**

- **Convert the spiral from a shape into a claim with a truth-condition.** The return is *earned* only if the closing position is *different in content* from the opening and *that difference is licensed by the intervening argument*. Concretely: the thesis opens with nihilism as *no-aseity* (nothing self-standing) and must close with no-aseity *plus* the positive result that meaning is conferred through the inhabited practical standpoint. The spiral is rigorous iff "meaning is conferred onto human experience" is a *conclusion derived from* Movements II–VII (conferral, the practical standpoint as undeclinable-from-within, the firewall), not a mood asserted at the end. If the candidate can state the closing thesis as a numbered conclusion with its premises drawn from earlier chapters, the spiral is structure; if they can only gesture at "return," it is rhetoric.

- **Name the borrowed shape and own it.** Cite Eliot (*Four Quartets*, "Little Gidding") and the Hegelian sublation honestly as *expository* devices, while insisting the *argumentative* work is the entailment, not the echo. Honesty about the rhetorical inheritance disarms the objection.

- **This is where the new work lives.** The prompt correctly identifies the meaning-closure as *underdeveloped*. The spiral cannot be rigorous until the meaning chapter actually does the derivation — drawing on real hermeneutics/phenomenology (Gadamer, *Truth and Method*; Ricoeur on narrative and meaning; Korsgaard's *Sources of Normativity* for the practical-standpoint-confers-value move; Frankfurt or Wolf on meaning-in-life). Until then, the spiral is a *promissory* shape, and the candidate should present it as such, not as achieved.

**Residual risk:** moderate until the meaning chapter is written; low after, *if* the closing thesis is stated as a derived conclusion.

### E.8 — Cross-domain depth: the two-faced examiner problem (Severity: HIGH — structural)

**The attack (humanities examiner).** "Your engineering chapters (VII–VIII: the firewall, the security channel, intrinsic primitives, Razian pre-emption in an authorization system) — do you actually understand the systems you invoke, or are they decoration? Explain the threat model. What does the firewall *do* when authorization flips to the model at a correct refusal — in implementation terms?"

**The attack (CS/engineering examiner, if co-supervised or interdisciplinary).** "Your philosophy chapters invoke Brentano on intentionality, Searle on institutional facts, Raz on authority, Libet and Schurger on readiness potentials. Have you read the primary sources or the corpus's gloss of them? What does Schurger 2012 *actually* show, and does it support your use of it?"

**Severity rationale.** High and structural, because this thesis is *constitutively* interdisciplinary and therefore exposed on *both* flanks simultaneously. Whichever examiner is in the room, there is a flank where the candidate's knowledge is shallower, and an AI-assembled corpus produces *uniformly fluent prose across domains the human may not uniformly command* — which is exactly the gap a viva is designed to find. The fluency itself is incriminating.

**Mitigation:**

- **Read every primary source the corpus cites, in the original.** Non-negotiable. The corpus's gloss is not a substitute. Specific high-risk citations the candidate *must* be able to discuss precisely:
  - **Libet (1983)** and **Schurger et al. (2012, PNAS)**: Schurger's stochastic-accumulator reinterpretation does *not* straightforwardly show "the brain decides before you do"; it argues the readiness potential may be an artifact of how spontaneous-movement onset is detected (averaging to threshold-crossing noise). If the candidate uses Libet/Schurger to support a strong "non-unitary operator," an examiner who knows this literature will dismantle a naive reading. Note the live critique (e.g., Schurger, Maoz, and subsequent work; the ErrP/error-related-negativity literature is separate and must not be conflated).
  - **Raz** on pre-emptive/service conception of authority (*The Morality of Freedom*, 1986): the "pre-emptive force within domain, flips at a correct refusal" must match Raz's actual normal-justification and pre-emption theses, not a paraphrase.
  - **Brentano** (intentionality/aboutness), **Searle** (institutional facts, status functions), **Libet/Schurger**, **al-Ghazali** — each a potential trap if known only through the corpus.

- **Build a threat-model appendix for the engineering claims** that an actual security engineer would accept: state the assets, the adversary, what authentication establishes, what authorization withholds, and what "flips to the model" means operationally (e.g., the refusal as a forfeiture condition on delegated authority). If the firewall cannot be stated as a real access-control mechanism, relabel it as a *philosophical model of* authority and stop implying implementation.

- **Pre-empt by naming your own primary flank.** The candidate should know, and state, which discipline is their home and which is the visited one, and where the visited-domain claims are *interpretive* rather than expert. Owning the asymmetry is far stronger than being caught in it.

**Residual risk:** moderate with thorough primary-source preparation; high if the candidate relies on the corpus's glosses. This is the entry where preparation effort converts risk most directly — and where the AI-assembled origin is most likely to surface as a concrete, demonstrable gap.

### Severity summary

| # | Risk | Severity | Residual after mitigation | Controllable by candidate? |
|---|------|----------|---------------------------|----------------------------|
| E.1 | AI authorship / originality | **HIGH (existential)** | Mod–High | Partly (depends on institutional policy) |
| E.2 | One dissertation or three | HIGH | Moderate | Yes (depends on E.6 holding) |
| E.3 | Theology in analytic dept | MED (dept-dependent) | Low–Mod | Yes (writing discipline) |
| E.4 | AI moral status / "low but not zero" | HIGH | Moderate | Yes (concede the number) |
| E.5 | "Theorem" without a proof | HIGH | Low if demoted | Yes (writing discipline) |
| E.6 | is/ought ≡ authn/authz identity | HIGH (keystone) | Mod–High | Yes (the intellectual crux) |
| E.7 | Spiral: rigorous or rhetorical | MED | Mod → Low | Yes (needs meaning chapter) |
| E.8 | Cross-domain depth (both flanks) | HIGH (structural) | Moderate | Yes (primary-source effort) |

**The two that can actually fail the degree:** E.1 (authorship — because it is a misconduct question, not a quality question) and the E.2+E.6 pair (because if the unifying identity is only analogy, the thesis is three underdeveloped monographs and not one defended contribution). Everything else is survivable with preparation. The candidate's preparation budget should be allocated in that order: settle the disclosure/compliance question first, then make the keystone identity genuinely defensible, then read every primary source. Style discipline (E.3, E.5) is cheap insurance against losing exchanges the candidate should never have entered.
