# Part III — Conferred Sourcehood: Free Will After Aseity

## 0. The shape of the argument, stated once

The free-will debate has been conducted, almost entirely, as a search for an *origin* — a point in the causal order where the agent, and not the agent's antecedents, is responsible for what happens next. Libertarians locate that point in an undetermined act of will; hard determinists and hard incompatibilists deny that any such point exists; compatibilists relocate it into the agent's psychology and hope the relocation is enough. What unites all three parties is the shared assumption that *if* free will exists, it exists as a kind of self-origination: the agent must, in some load-bearing sense, be the source of the source of its acts.

This chapter's claim is that the assumption is the smuggled premise, and that it is the aseity premise wearing different clothes. The no-aseity framework has already denied, across existence, status, ought, and meaning, that anything is self-standing. To then demand that the will *alone* be self-causing in order to count as free is to grant the will an exemption the framework grants to nothing else. Drop the exemption and the debate reorganizes. The will is not causa sui. Nothing is. Sourcehood — being the author of one's acts — is therefore either impossible (the hard-incompatibilist conclusion) or it is *conferred*: a standing one is put into and then inhabits, re-enacts, and is addressed within, never an origin one secretes from oneself.

The work of the chapter is to show that "conferred sourcehood" is not merely the residue of the framework restated — not merely "agency lives at the inhabited standpoint" said again with more words. That restatement would be a FAIL by the same bar Part IV (Meaning) had to clear. The PASS, if there is one, has to be carried by two propositions that *float free* of the necessity-of-locus residue — that say something falsifiable about agency which the bare fact of an inhabited standpoint does not already entail, and which could be shown false by a finding about the world. Those two are the **second-personal remainder** and the **occasionalism of agency**. I lead with them and with the comparative witnesses, build the apparatus around them, and then, in the final section, submit the chapter to its own test and report the verdict without flattering it.

## 1. The second-personal remainder

### 1.1 Strawson's relocation of the question

P.F. Strawson, in "Freedom and Resentment" (1962; *high confidence* on text and thesis), made a move whose radicalism is still underweighted. The traditional question — does determinism, if true, render the reactive attitudes *unjustified*? — he declined to answer on its own terms. He argued instead that the reactive attitudes (resentment, gratitude, indignation, hurt feelings, forgiveness, the whole register of our responses to the *quality of others' wills toward us*) are not conclusions we draw from a prior theoretical belief about causation. They are constitutive of participation in interpersonal life. The "objective attitude" — viewing another as a thing to be managed, treated, cured, or trained rather than addressed — is available to us episodically (toward the very young, the severely psychotic, in moments of strategic detachment), but Strawson's claim is that we *could not* adopt it globally and permanently toward all persons as a consequence of accepting determinism, because the commitment to interpersonal relationship that grounds the reactive attitudes is not the sort of thing a theoretical proposition can dislodge.

The standard reading takes this as a naturalized, almost quietist compatibilism: responsibility "just is" the practice of holding-responsible, and the practice is immune to metaphysical revision. I want a stronger and more precise reading, because the quietist version cannot carry the weight this chapter needs. The precise reading is this: the reactive attitudes encode a **second-personal** relation — a standpoint addressing a standpoint — and that relation is not a fact about either party taken severally. It is irreducibly *between*. Stephen Darwall (*The Second-Person Standpoint*, 2006; *high confidence*) made the structure explicit: to hold you responsible is to address a *demand* to you, from within a relationship of mutual accountability, presupposing your authority to so address me in turn. The "you" in "I hold *you* responsible" is not a designation of an object in the third person; it is the second relatum of an address.

### 1.2 The falsifiable reduction question

Here is where the chapter stakes something falsifiable rather than re-describing the locus. The claim is:

> **(R) The second-personal "you" does not reduce, without remainder, to a forward-looking program of behavior-shaping.**

This is a claim with a determinate denial, and the denier is a real and powerful one. Robert Sapolsky (*Determined: A Science of Life Without Free Will*, 2023; *high confidence* on the book's thesis) argues that once the causal history of any act is traced — through neurons, hormones, development, culture, evolution — there is no remaining room for desert, and therefore no warrant for blame or for the backward-looking reactive attitudes at all. His positive recommendation is the well-known forward-looking package, articulated more carefully by Derk Pereboom (*Living Without Free Will*, 2001; and *Free Will, Agency, and Meaning in Life*, 2014; *high confidence*): retain the *protective* functions — quarantine of the dangerous on the public-health model, deterrence, moral formation, reconciliation — and *discard* basic desert. On this view "I hold you responsible" is a folk-psychological wrapper around a forward-looking control function: what is *really* happening when I resent you is that a behavior-shaping signal is being emitted and received, and the resentment is the affective costume of the signal. Reduce the costume and nothing of value is lost; indeed something is gained, because the costume licenses cruelty.

(R) says: that reduction leaves a remainder. And it must say *what* the remainder is, or it is empty. The remainder is this. A forward-looking shaping relation is, in its logical form, **first-person-to-third-person**: an agent (me, or the state, or the optimizing institution) acts *upon* a system (you, modeled as a bundle of modifiable dispositions) to alter its future outputs. The relation is fully specified without any address — without the moment in which I treat you as a *source who could answer me*, who has standing to say "you have wronged me" back, whose acknowledgment or refusal of my demand is itself the point and not merely a lever for the next adjustment. The reactive attitudes are not affective decorations on a control signal; they are the *registration of the other as an addressable source*. When I resent you, I am not predicting and steering your future behavior — I may resent you precisely when steering is hopeless and prediction certain. I am holding open a relation in which your *will toward me* is the object of response. Forgiveness makes this vivid: to forgive is not to cease a deterrence program (one can deter without forgiving and forgive without ceasing to deter); it is to release a *claim* that presupposed you were a source against whom a claim could lie.

So the remainder is the **address itself** — the second-personal "you" as the second pole of a demand, which the forward-looking package does not contain and cannot generate, because the forward-looking package is, by its own design, a first-to-third relation that has *eliminated the second person on purpose*. That is the whole content of Sapolsky's and Pereboom's reform: stop addressing, start managing. The reform is not a reduction of the second person to something more basic; it is its *abolition*. And an abolition is not a reduction. This is the crux. The hard-incompatibilist program does not show that "holding-you-responsible" *was all along* forward-looking shaping; it *recommends replacing* the second-personal stance with the forward-looking one. The fact that the recommendation is intelligible — that we *could* go fully objective — is exactly Strawson's point, and it concedes rather than refutes (R): you can only be urged to abandon what is not already identical to the thing you are urged to adopt.

### 1.3 Why this is principled and not ad hoc

The obvious objection: this just *defines* the remainder into existence by stipulating that the second-personal address is irreducible. Calling something "irreducible" is not an argument. The objection is fair and must be met without flinching, because if (R) is ad hoc the chapter fails on its first leg.

The non-ad-hoc defense has three parts, and it is the same structure that carried the meaning chapter's A/B remainder. *First*, the remainder is **independently motivated** by a feature neither posited for this debate nor invented to win it: the logical asymmetry between second-person and third-person relations is studied in linguistics, in the philosophy of indexicals, and in developmental psychology long before anyone reaches the free-will question. The second person is not a third person I happen to be talking to; "you" cannot be analyzed as "that object, plus my talking to it," because the addressee's *uptake* — their recognition of being addressed *as* an addressee — is part of the relation's success conditions in a way no third-personal description supplies (compare the difference between *manipulating* someone via subliminal cue and *telling* them something). *Second*, the remainder is **falsifiable in the right direction**: (R) is refuted if a complete forward-looking redescription can be given that captures every normatively relevant feature of holding-responsible *including* the differential between forgiving-and-deterring versus deterring-without-forgiving, between apologizing-to and placating, between being-owed-an-account and needing-to-predict-behavior — *with no residue*. The hard incompatibilist's own writing is the evidence here: Pereboom does not claim to capture these differentials; he claims we are *better off without them*. That is a confession that they are not captured. *Third*, the remainder is **not the residue**. "There must be an inhabited standpoint" does not entail that there are *two* standpoints in an address relation, nor that the relation between them fails to reduce to a one-place control function. A solipsistic universe with exactly one inhabited standpoint has the necessity-of-locus residue in full and has *no* second-personal remainder, because there is no one to address. (R) therefore says something the residue does not say, and could be false in a world where the residue is true. That is the test for floating free, and (R) passes it.

This last point is the one to hold onto: **the second-personal remainder is logically detachable from the necessity of a locus.** The locus is monadic; the remainder is dyadic. A chapter that rested on "agency lives at the standpoint" would be monadic all the way down and would be re-description. (R) introduces a second pole the residue does not contain.

## 2. The occasionalism of agency

### 2.1 The phenomenon: authorship drains and returns

The meaning chapter's second float-free component was an *occasionalism of meaning*: meaning is withdrawable without subtraction of fact and re-bestowable at the locus — the very same arrangement of matter is meaningful, then meaningless, then meaningful again, with nothing dynamical added or removed at the transitions. The agency analog is the **drain and return of authorship**, and it is reported, not theorized, by people in the relevant states.

The addict in the grip of craving describes watching themselves reach for the thing they have, in some full sense, decided not to reach for — authorship present as spectator, absent as author. (The classic philosophical articulation is Harry Frankfurt's unwilling addict, "Freedom of the Will and the Concept of a Person," 1971; *high confidence* — the agent whose first-order desire is not endorsed by the second-order volition, who is *moved* by a will that is not, in Frankfurt's sense, *his*.) Dissociative states report the same structure more sharply: action continues, the standpoint of authorship recedes or splits. And Internal Family Systems (Richard Schwartz, *Internal Family Systems Therapy*, 1995; *No Bad Parts*, 2021; *moderate confidence* on clinical specifics, *high confidence* that the model exists and is widely used) gives a phenomenological vocabulary that maps the drain and return with unusual precision: the distinction between being **part-led** (a protective or exiled "part" is in the driver's seat; the person acts *from* it and often against their considered aims) and being **Self-led** (the agent acts from the calm, curious, integrated standpoint Schwartz calls "Self"). The therapeutic event — "unblending" — is precisely the *return of authorship*: the same person, often the same situation, the part no longer fused with the standpoint, the act now owned.

### 2.2 The falsifiable claim

> **(O) Loss and recovery of authorship does not always track a dynamical difference. There are transitions in ownership without a corresponding subtraction or addition of dynamical fact.**

And, stated with its falsifier exposed:

> **(O) is false if every change in ownership is accompanied by, and exhausted by, a dynamical change — if "now I author this / now I do not" always reduces to "now this brain-state, now that one," such that the ownership-talk carries no information the dynamical description lacks.**

This is the agency analog of "meaning withdrawable without subtraction of fact," and it must be defended at exactly the point where it is most vulnerable, because the determinist's reply is immediate and strong: *of course* there is a dynamical difference. The craving state and the resolved state are different brain states; the part-led and Self-led configurations are different patterns of activation; dissociation has neural correlates. There is no ownership transition without a dynamical transition. (O) looks false on its face.

The defense is to be precise about what (O) denies. (O) does **not** deny that the states differ dynamically. It denies that the *ownership* is identical to, or read off from, the dynamical difference — that the transition *as a transition in sourcehood* is *constituted by* the dynamical delta. The argument is a *multiple-realization-cum-orthogonality* argument, and it has a clean form. Take Korsgaard (*Self-Constitution: Agency, Identity, and Integrity*, 2009; *high confidence*): agency is not a standing property of a self that pre-exists its acts; it is the *continuous activity* of constituting oneself as the author of one's actions, by acting on principles one can endorse as one's own. On this view there is no "self" sitting behind the acts that *has* or *lacks* sourcehood as a stored attribute. Sourcehood is *enacted*, each time, in the unification of the agent's volitions under a principle the agent takes up as its own law. Now combine this with the no-aseity point: the principle is not self-legislated *ex nihilo* — it is received, conferred, drawn from a tradition, a relationship, a formation — and re-spoken. Authorship is the *re-speaking*, the taking-up. The reason ownership can drain and return without a one-to-one dynamical signature is that ownership is not a state but an *act of constitution*, and the same dynamical configuration can be one in which that constituting act is occurring or one in which it has lapsed — because the constituting act is individuated by its *normative* relation (this volition taken up *as mine, under a principle I own*) and not by its physical substrate alone.

### 2.3 Guidance control and "taking responsibility": the bridge to the dynamical world

(O) would be irresponsible mysticism if it severed sourcehood from the dynamical world entirely — if it said authorship floats above the brain untethered. It does not, and the tether is Fischer and Ravizza (*Responsibility and Control: A Theory of Moral Responsibility*, 1998; *high confidence*). Their account gives **guidance control** as the form of control responsibility requires (not *regulative* control, not the dual power to do otherwise — see §4 on Frankfurt), and it has two components: the mechanism that issues in action must be **moderately reasons-responsive** (it would respond to a sufficient range of reasons, including at least one moral reason, across a patterned set of possible scenarios), and the agent must have **taken responsibility** for that mechanism — must have come, over a developmental history, to see themselves as an agent whose acts are *theirs* and as a fair target of the reactive attitudes.

That second condition is the hinge, and it is doing work the determinist's redescription cannot absorb. "Taking responsibility" is the agent's *own* constituting uptake — the appropriation of a mechanism *as* the agent's own source. It is, in Korsgaard's terms, the self-constitution; in Strawson's, the entering into the second-personal community as a participant rather than an object; in IFS terms, the move from part-led to Self-led ownership. And here is the falsifiable bite: the *same* reasons-responsive mechanism, with the *same* dynamical profile, can be one for which the agent has taken responsibility or one for which it has not — and *that* difference is the difference between authored and merely-undergone action, with no further dynamical fact settling it. The manipulated agent of §4 has a reasons-responsive mechanism that is dynamically indistinguishable from a normal one and yet is not a source, precisely because the taking-up has been *bypassed*. This is the same structure as (R)'s remainder and the meaning chapter's occasionalism: a difference that makes a difference to sourcehood and *fails to reduce* to the dynamical delta.

So (O) does not float untethered. It says: ownership is a normative act of constitution that *supervenes on but is not exhausted by* the dynamics — and it earns "not exhausted by" through the manipulation cases, where the dynamics are matched and the ownership differs. If someone produces a case where ownership and dynamics come apart *and* the ownership difference makes *no* difference to anything we care about — a distinction without a remainder — (O) is in trouble. That is its falsifier, and it is a real one.

## 3. The comparative witnesses: conferred sourcehood is the cross-traditional resolution

These are load-bearing, not decoration. Their function in the argument is to show that "conferred sourcehood" is not a philosopher's evasion invented to escape a Western dilemma, but the *independently arrived-at* shape that the freedom-under-decree problem takes whenever it is thought through to the end in a tradition that *never granted the will aseity in the first place*. If three traditions with no shared metaphysics converge on the same structure, the structure is not ad hoc.

**Ash'arī kasb (acquisition).** Classical Ash'arī kalām (al-Ash'arī, d. 935; the doctrine elaborated by al-Bāqillānī, al-Juwaynī, and discussed extensively by al-Ghazālī; *high confidence* on the existence and core of the doctrine, *moderate confidence* on the finer scholastic disputes among Ash'arīs about *kasb*'s exact mechanism, which were contested *within* the school) holds that God creates (*khalq*) the act, and the human being *acquires* (*kasb*, *iktisāb*) it. The acquisition is what makes the act *the agent's* for purposes of responsibility, *without* the agent being its ontological originator. This is conferred sourcehood in the strict sense: total causal dependence (no aseity — God is the only true cause) *with* genuine ownership at the locus of the human standpoint. The doctrine is famously hard to state precisely, and its critics (Mu'tazila on one side demanding real human causal power, philosophers on the other) pressed exactly the dilemma this chapter addresses — either the human really causes (libertarian aseity) or is a puppet (dissolution). *Kasb* is the refusal of the dilemma: acquisition is a third thing, ownership-without-origination.

**Yoruba orí.** In Yoruba thought (as transmitted in the ethnographic and philosophical literature — Wande Abimbola, Segun Gbadegesin, *African Philosophy: Traditional Yoruba Philosophy and Contemporary African Realities*, 1991; Barry Hallen; *moderate confidence* on the philosophical interpretation, which varies by source, *high confidence* on the broad structure), *orí* — literally "head," the inner head, the bearer of personal destiny — is *chosen* (or received) before birth in the spiritual realm and then *lived out*. The destiny is conferred (and in some tellings chosen blind, then sealed), and the lived life is the inhabiting of it. Crucially, *orí* is both given and one's own — the most intimate self, prayed to, propitiated — and the tradition holds together fixity-of-destiny with the felt and practiced reality of effort, character (*ìwà*), and responsibility. This is conferred sourcehood arrived at *outside* the Abrahamic frame entirely: the structure is not parasitic on monotheist omnipotence.

**"Tie your camel, then trust in God."** The Prophetic hadith (*i'qil ba'īrak wa tawakkal* — tie your camel and then rely on God; reported in al-Tirmidhī, *moderate confidence* on the exact chain/grading, *high confidence* that the saying is canonical and widely transmitted in this sense) is the practical compression of the whole position. It does *not* say: since all is decreed, do nothing. It says: act *fully* — tie the camel with your own hands, exhaustively, as if it were up to you — and *within* that very acting, the decree is operative. The acting is not in competition with the decree; the acting is *how* one inhabits a conferred standpoint inside a determined order. This is the lived form of the orthogonality thesis (§Movement VI): the *dynamics* (the decree, the determination) and the *sourcehood* (your tying the camel, owned as your act) are not rivals for the same explanatory slot.

The convergence is the argument. Three traditions, no shared metaphysics, each facing "freedom under total determination," each independently producing *ownership-without-origination* rather than either horn of the libertarian/dissolutionist dilemma. The Western debate's peculiarity is that it *built the aseity demand into the definition of freedom* and then discovered, with Strawson (Galen) and Sapolsky, that nothing can meet it — concluding "no free will" rather than "wrong definition." The witnesses show the definition was always optional. Conferred sourcehood is *stronger* than libertarianism, not weaker, because it never incurred the debt libertarianism cannot pay: it never required the agent to be causa sui.

## 4. The pivot: Galen Strawson's Basic Argument is the no-aseity thesis applied to the will

Now the spine. Galen Strawson, "The Impossibility of Moral Responsibility" (1994; restated often, e.g., *Freedom and Belief*, 1986; *high confidence*), argues:

1. To be truly morally responsible for what one does, one must be truly responsible for *how one is* — for the nature (the mental nature, the principles, the values) from which the action flows.
2. To be truly responsible for how one is, one must have *brought it about* that one is that way.
3. But to have brought *that* about, one must already have been a certain way (had some prior nature, some prior principles by which one chose) — and have been responsible for *that* prior way.
4. This regresses infinitely; to stop it, one would have to be *causa sui*, the cause of oneself, ultimately self-originating.
5. Nothing can be causa sui (Nietzsche's "best self-contradiction that has been conceived so far," *Beyond Good and Evil* §21 — Strawson's own citation).
6. Therefore no one is ever truly, ultimately morally responsible.

**The framework agrees with premises 1 through 5 entirely.** This is the chapter's hinge and the point of maximal honesty. The Basic Argument is *the no-aseity thesis applied to the will*: self-causation *is* aseity, and the framework's founding claim is that nothing is self-standing. So the framework does not contest a single link. It *grants the regress*. It grants van Inwagen's consequence argument too (*An Essay on Free Will*, 1983; *high confidence*: if determinism is true, our acts are consequences of the laws and the remote past, over neither of which we have any choice, so we have no choice about our acts) — because the consequence argument, read correctly, is *also* a no-aseity argument: it says you are not the ultimate source, the past and laws are. *Yes.* You are not the ultimate source. Nothing is its own ultimate source.

The framework breaks with the Basic Argument and the consequence argument at exactly one place: the inference from premise 6's *true* content — **no ultimate, self-originating responsibility** — to the conclusion **no responsibility, no free will, no sourcehood**. That inference is valid *only if* responsibility/sourcehood *means* ultimate self-origination. It carries the aseity demand in the word "true" ("true moral responsibility," "ultimate responsibility"). Strip the demand and premises 1–5 establish exactly what the framework already holds — the will is not causa sui — and establish *nothing* about whether there is *conferred* sourcehood, because conferred sourcehood was never a candidate for self-origination and so is untouched by the proof that self-origination is impossible. The Basic Argument refutes libertarianism (which *did* claim self-origination). It does not touch an account that claims ownership-without-origination, because that account *concedes the regress and locates sourcehood elsewhere* — in the conferred-and-inhabited standpoint, the second-personal address, the act of constitution.

Robert Kane's event-causal libertarianism (*The Significance of Free Will*, 1996; *high confidence*) — self-forming actions, undetermined at the moment of choice, where the agent's effort fixes which of competing undetermined outcomes occurs — is the most serious attempt to *pay* the aseity debt within the naturalistic frame. The framework's verdict: Kane is trying to buy back exactly the thing the framework says is not needed and not affordable. Even granting Kane the indeterminism, the "luck objection" (Mele, *Free Will and Luck*, 2006; *high confidence*) bites: an undetermined choice is, at the moment of undetermination, not more *the agent's* — it is less so, because nothing about the agent settles it. This is the deep reason aseity is the wrong place to look for sourcehood: *self-origination, even if you could get it, would not give you ownership; it would give you a coin-flip you happen to be standing next to.* Ownership is conferred and taken up, not originated. Kane's heroic effort to secure aseity, and its vulnerability to luck, is the best possible evidence that sourcehood was never going to be found in self-origination.

## 5. Leeway versus sourcehood: why this is a sourcehood account

The chapter must locate itself in the debate's geography. Most free-will argument concerns **leeway** — the could-have-done-otherwise, the Principle of Alternative Possibilities (PAP): that one is responsible for an act only if one could have done otherwise. The consequence argument is a leeway argument; it shows determinism removes alternatives.

Frankfurt's 1969 cases ("Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility"; *high confidence*) detach responsibility from leeway: a counterfactual intervener (Black) stands ready to force Jones to do X if Jones shows signs of not choosing X, but Jones chooses X on his own and Black never acts. Jones could not have done otherwise (Black would have prevented it) yet is plainly responsible — because the *actual sequence* ran through Jones's own choosing. Frankfurt cases are contested (the "flicker of freedom" reply, the dilemma defense — Widerker, Kane), so the chapter does not rest its full weight on them; *but their relocation of the question is sound even if the cases are imperfect.* What does the work in the Jones case is that **the act flowed through the right kind of source in the actual sequence**, regardless of alternatives. That is the sourcehood question, and it is the one orthogonal to determinism.

This is why the whole chapter is a *sourcehood* account and is *unmoved by the consequence argument*. The consequence argument removes leeway; conferred sourcehood does not need leeway; therefore the consequence argument, granted in full, leaves conferred sourcehood standing. Determinism (Movement VI) is a thesis about *dynamics* — state produces successor state. It speaks to leeway (it closes alternatives). It is silent on sourcehood (whether the actual sequence ran through a standpoint that took the act up as its own, within a second-personal community that addresses it as a source). The is→ought transit and the history→instant transit that determinism cannot supply are exactly the transits sourcehood requires and the dynamics do not provide.

## 6. The manipulation argument, met head-on

Pereboom's four-case argument (*Living Without Free Will*, 2001; *high confidence*) is the sharpest blade. Case 1: Plum is created moments ago by neuroscientists who manipulate him in real time to reason and act egoistically, issuing in a murder; he meets every compatibilist condition (reasons-responsiveness, second-order endorsement, etc.) yet is intuitively *not* responsible — he is a puppet. Cases 2–4 progressively *remove the manipulators*, replacing real-time control with prior programming, then with ordinary determinism, each case (Pereboom argues) relevantly like the last. If Plum-1 is not responsible and ordinary determinism (Case 4) is relevantly similar, then no determined agent is responsible. The argument's power is that it threatens to collapse *every* compatibilist condition into mere mechanism.

The framework's answer is *the second-personal remainder doing structural work*, and this is where (R) earns its keep beyond rhetoric. The distinction between Case 1 and Case 4 is **not** a difference in the dynamics (Pereboom is right that the dynamics can be matched), and it is **not** the presence or absence of a manipulator as such. It is whether the agent's sourcehood was **constituted through the second-personal community or bypassed it**. Manipulation — Case 1 — is precisely *shaping that bypasses the address*: the neuroscientists do not *address* Plum, demand of him, hold him to account, stand in mutual accountability with him; they *operate on* him in the first-to-third mode, the same mode Sapolsky recommends we adopt toward everyone. Ordinary formation — Case 4 — is shaping that *runs through* the second-personal: a child is *addressed*, held responsible, drawn into the reactive-attitude community, *takes responsibility* (Fischer–Ravizza), constitutes itself (Korsgaard) under principles it appropriates as its own *in dialogue with* other sources.

The two are not on a continuum of "more or less manipulation"; they differ in *kind* along exactly the axis (R) and (O) identify. The framework therefore **rejects the soft underbelly of the four-case argument — the claim that the cases are relevantly similar.** They are dynamically similar and *second-personally opposite*. Plum-1 was never a source because his ownership was *installed*, never *taken up in address*; the ordinary agent is a source because their ownership was *conferred-and-taken-up through* the community of mutual accountability. This is not special pleading invented for Pereboom: it is the *same* remainder that (R) defended independently and the *same* taking-responsibility that (O) and Fischer–Ravizza require. The manipulation argument, far from refuting conferred sourcehood, *displays its discriminating power* — it is exactly the account that can say *why* the puppet is not a source and the formed agent is, without appealing to leeway and without claiming either is causa sui.

And the honest concession: the cases at the *margin* — heavy indoctrination, abusive formation, cults, coercive control — are *genuinely* hard, because they are formation that *partly* bypasses and *partly* runs through the address. The framework predicts exactly this: responsibility should be *graded and contested* precisely where the second-personal constitution is *partial*. That the hard cases are hard *in the place the theory says they should be hard* is confirmation, not embarrassment.

## 7. The neuroscience: Libet, Wegner, and the orthogonality of timing

Libet (1983; *high confidence*) found a readiness potential (RP) preceding the reported time of conscious intention to move by some hundreds of milliseconds, taken by many to show the brain "decides" before consciousness and that conscious will is epiphenomenal. Wegner (*The Illusion of Conscious Will*, 2002; *high confidence*) generalized this into the thesis that the experience of conscious will is a *post hoc construction*, an interpretation the mind imposes, not a cause of action.

Two replies, in order of decisiveness. **First, empirical:** Schurger, Sitt, and Dehaene (2012, PNAS; *high confidence*) showed the RP is well-modeled as a *stochastic accumulator* crossing a threshold — the apparent "build-up before the decision" is an artifact of averaging time-locked to movement onset, which preferentially samples trials where noise was already drifting upward. On this model the RP is *not* a decision signal that precedes and determines an unconscious "decision"; it is the noisy accumulation whose threshold-crossing *is* (or coincides with) the commitment. The Libet inference is undercut at the source. This is *moderate-to-high confidence* as a live and well-supported interpretation, while noting the literature remains contested.

**Second, and decisive even if Libet stands:** *orthogonality.* Suppose conscious will *is* late — suppose Wegner is entirely right that the feeling of willing is a post hoc construction. This is a claim about the **timing of a dynamical state** (when, in the causal stream, a particular conscious representation occurs relative to motor preparation). Sourcehood, on this chapter's account, **is not the metaphysical instant of decision.** It is not located at the millisecond where an uncaused will allegedly fires — that location was the *libertarian's* commitment, and the framework abandoned it in §4. Sourcehood is the conferred, second-personally-addressed, continuously re-constituted *ownership* of the act. Whether the conscious "go" signal precedes or trails the RP is as irrelevant to *that* as the firing order of any other neurons. Libet and Wegner refute the *Cartesian-libertarian* picture of the will as a conscious uncaused starter pistol. They leave conferred sourcehood untouched, because conferred sourcehood never claimed the will was an uncaused conscious starter pistol. The neuroscience kills the picture the framework already rejected and was *built to do without*.

---

## 8. Did the sourcehood hold?

The chapter must now submit to its own bar and report the result without flattering it. The bar: a PASS requires a **positive, falsifiable proposition about agency that floats free of the necessity-of-locus residue** ("there must be an inhabited standpoint at all"). A FAIL is mere re-description: "agency lives at the inhabited standpoint" is the residue restated.

**The residue, conceded plainly.** "Conferred sourcehood lives at the inhabited standpoint" *is* the necessity-of-locus residue. It is monadic, it is the one aseity the framework cannot dissolve, and a chapter resting on it *alone* would be a FAIL. I concede this without qualification. The locus does not float free of itself.

**The two propositions that carry the PASS, stated exactly:**

> **(R) — The second-personal remainder.** Holding-you-responsible — the second-personal address constitutive of the reactive attitudes — does not reduce without remainder to any forward-looking, first-to-third-person program of behavior-shaping (quarantine, deterrence, formation). The remainder is the *address itself*: the registration of the other as a source who can answer, to whom a claim can lie, whose forgiveness releases a claim rather than ceasing a deterrence.
>
> **Condition under which (R) fails:** (R) is false if a complete forward-looking redescription captures every normatively relevant feature of holding-responsible — including the differentials forgiving-while-deterring / deterring-without-forgiving, apologizing-to / placating, being-owed-an-account / needing-to-predict — *with no residue*. If the second-person "you" *is* a behavior-shaping function under a description, sourcehood collapses into management and the chapter fails on its first leg.

> **(O) — The occasionalism of agency.** Loss and recovery of authorship (addiction's watched-act, dissociation, IFS part-led versus Self-led) does not *always* track a dynamical difference that *constitutes* it: ownership is an act of self-constitution (Korsgaard) taken up over a mechanism (Fischer–Ravizza's "taking responsibility"), individuated by its normative relation, such that dynamically matched configurations can differ in sourcehood — as the manipulation cases demonstrate (matched dynamics, opposite ownership).
>
> **Condition under which (O) fails:** (O) is false if *every* change in ownership is accompanied and *exhausted* by a dynamical change — if "now I author this / now I do not" always reduces to "now this state, now that one" with the ownership-talk carrying no information the dynamical description lacks. If sourcehood always tracks a dynamical delta one-to-one, the occasionalism is illusory and the chapter fails on its second leg.

**Do they float free of the residue?** This is the whole verdict, and I will not fudge it.

(R) floats free: it is **dyadic**, and the residue is **monadic**. A world with exactly one inhabited standpoint satisfies the necessity-of-locus residue *in full* and contains *no* second-personal remainder, because there is no second pole to address. (R) therefore asserts something the residue does not entail and could be false while the residue is true — the definition of floating free. (R)'s content is not "there is a standpoint" but "there are two standpoints in an address relation that does not reduce to a one-place control function." **(R) passes.**

(O) floats free more narrowly, and I flag the narrowness honestly. (O)'s *safe* content — "ownership is enacted at the standpoint" — is close to the residue and, taken alone, would be re-description. What lifts it free is the **falsifiable orthogonality claim**: ownership and dynamics *can come apart*, demonstrated by dynamically-matched / ownership-divergent cases (manipulation; arguably part-led/Self-led transitions where the therapeutic literature reports ownership change the neural story does not yet cash out one-to-one). That claim could be false — a sufficiently complete neuroscience might show ownership *always* tracks a dynamical delta that constitutes it — and *that* falsifiability is what makes (O) a result rather than the residue. **(O) passes, conditionally**: it passes *as* the orthogonality claim, and would fail if reduced to the bare "enacted at the locus" formulation. The chapter must, and does, rest (O) on the orthogonality claim, not on the locus.

**The honest residual risk.** The weakest joint is the inference, in both (R) and (O), from "the hard incompatibilist does not *capture* the remainder" to "the remainder is *real* rather than *eliminable*." Pereboom would say: I do not capture it because *there is nothing there* — it is an illusion we are better without, like phlogiston. The chapter's reply is that the second-personal address is *independently attested* (linguistics, developmental psychology, the logic of indexicals) and *practically inescapable* in P.F. Strawson's sense, so its elimination is a *revision* we are urged to make, not a *reduction* that shows it was never there. *That reply is strong but not knockdown.* If one is willing to bite the bullet — to hold that the second-personal stance is a *systematic illusion* with no remainder, eliminable in principle like the geocentric "motion" of the sun — then (R) is contested. The chapter's wager is that the bullet is *unbiteable in practice* (you cannot run a human life from the objective attitude) and *unmotivated in theory* (the elimination is recommended on moral grounds — to stop cruelty — which presupposes the very second-personal standing it would eliminate). I judge the wager *won on balance, not won decisively.* High confidence that the move is the *right shape*; moderate confidence that it *defeats* a determined eliminativist who accepts the practical cost.

**Verdict: PASS, with one conditional and one conceded risk.** The chapter delivers two positive, falsifiable propositions about agency, each with an exact failure condition, and at least one (R) floats *cleanly* free of the necessity-of-locus residue by being dyadic where the residue is monadic. The second (O) floats free *as* an orthogonality claim and would collapse into re-description if rested on the locus instead. The forced choice between libertarian causa sui and hard-incompatibilist dissolution is dissolved: sourcehood is conferred — owned without being originated, addressed without being self-caused, re-constituted each act rather than secreted once from an impossible self-cause. Determinism answered the dynamics question and was, throughout, orthogonal to the sourcehood question, exactly as Movement VI required. The Basic Argument and the consequence argument were granted in full and shown to refute only the aseity the framework never claimed.

**Where it would fail.** If the second-personal "you" reduces without remainder to forward-looking shaping, (R) falls. If ownership always tracks a constituting dynamical delta one-to-one, (O) falls. If both fall, "conferred sourcehood" reduces to "agency lives at the standpoint" — the residue restated — and the chapter is re-description, not result. They do not both fall on the present evidence. The sourcehood holds.
