**WINNER: [reactive]**

[reactive] is the strongest chapter on all four axes. It is the only draft that does what the test actually demands without quietly cheating on it.

**(1) Floats-free test.** This is the decisive axis, and [reactive] is the only draft that adjudicates it with full honesty. It separates the two propositions cleanly: (R) the second-personal remainder passes *cleanly* because it is **dyadic where the residue is monadic** — the solipsistic one-standpoint world satisfies the locus-residue in full yet contains no remainder. That is a genuine, rigorous proof of floating-free, not an assertion of it. Crucially, [reactive] then refuses to claim the same clean pass for (O): it concedes that (O)'s "safe" content ("ownership enacted at the locus") *is* near-residue and would be a FAIL if rested there, and that (O) only floats free *as the falsifiable orthogonality claim*. That self-imposed downgrade ("PASS, conditionally") is exactly the intellectual honesty the brief's own test was built to extract. [compat] gives both propositions a symmetric clean PASS and never notices that (O) is the weaker float — it asserts the necessary/contingent distinction does equal work for both, which is too generous. [reactive] catches what [compat] misses.

**(2) Strength against the hard incompatibilist.** Both [compat] and [reactive] meet Pereboom's four-case argument head-on with the same core move (the manipulation bypasses the second-personal standpoint vs. ordinary formation runs through it). But [reactive] is stronger in two ways. First, it adds the **eliminativist's-unbitten-bullet** concession in §8 — naming the genuinely weak joint (the inference from "Pereboom doesn't *capture* the remainder" to "the remainder is *real* rather than *eliminable*") and grading its own confidence ("won on balance, not won decisively"). [compat] never isolates this inferential weak point; it presents the Pereboom answer as more secure than it is. Second, [reactive] correctly diagnoses the **abolition-vs-reduction** distinction: Sapolsky/Pereboom *recommend replacing* the second-personal stance, which concedes rather than reduces it. That is the single best argumentative move in any of the three drafts and it directly strengthens (R) against the hardest opponent. [reactive] also handles the **margin cases** (cults, coercive control) as *predicted* gradation rather than embarrassment — a sophistication absent from [compat].

**(3) Scholarly accuracy.** [reactive] is the most thorough and the most accurately sourced. It correctly adds Mele's *Free Will and Luck* (2006) luck objection to Kane — which [compat] omits — and deploys it precisely (indeterminism gives "a coin-flip you happen to be standing next to," not ownership). It cites Nietzsche *BGE* §21 as Galen Strawson's own source for "best self-contradiction" — accurate and a mark of genuine familiarity; [compat] attributes the same phrase loosely to Strawson without the Nietzsche provenance. On the comparative material, [reactive] is markedly more careful: it names the actual Ash'arī chain (al-Ash'arī d. 935, al-Bāqillānī, al-Juwaynī, al-Ghazālī) and flags that *kasb*'s mechanism was contested *within* the school; it cites real Yoruba-philosophy scholars (Gbadegesin, Abimbola, Hallen) and the *ìwà* (character) dimension. [compat] gives kasb and orì correctly but generically, with no scholarly apparatus. No fabricated citations in [reactive].

**(4) Prose and unity.** [reactive] is a single sustained argument with a deliberate architecture: it *leads* with the two float-free components (§§1–2) before the apparatus, which is the right move given that the test is about those two propositions, then converges everything in §8. The lettered propositions (R)/(O) recur as fixed reference points, giving genuine unity. [compat] is also well-written and well-organized, but its order is more conventional (apparatus first, components later) and its §9 over-claims symmetry. [reactive]'s prose is slightly denser but more controlled.

**BEST GRAFT FROM EACH LOSER:**
