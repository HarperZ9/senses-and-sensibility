# Part III — Conferred Sourcehood: Free Will After Aseity

## 1. The shape of the problem, and what this chapter owes

The meaning chapter (Part IV) passed its test by producing two propositions that floated free of the framework's one irreducible residue — the necessity of an inhabited standpoint. It did not merely say "meaning lives at the locus"; it said something the residue does not entail: that undergoing leaves a remainder no functional welfare description captures (the A/B remainder), and that meaning is withdrawable without subtraction of fact and re-bestowable at the locus (the occasionalism of meaning). Both are falsifiable. Both could have come out the other way.

This chapter owes the same coin for agency. The temptation — and the failure mode named in advance — is to write a chapter whose entire content is "sourcehood lives at the inhabited standpoint." That is the residue restated. The residue is real and will be conceded openly in §9; conceding it is not the result. The result, if there is one, must be carried by two propositions that say more than "there must be someone there": the **second-personal remainder** (§6) and the **occasionalism of agency** (§7). If those two do not float free — if each collapses into either the residue or into the determinist's own forward-looking description — the chapter fails, and §9 will say so.

I take that risk seriously enough to state the failure condition first. The chapter fails if (1) reduces without remainder to behavior-shaping and (2) always tracks a dynamical difference. Under those conditions there is no conferred sourcehood, only the bare fact that dynamics run through a standpoint, which determinism already grants and which establishes nothing about authorship.

## 2. Leeway and sourcehood: locating the real question

The free-will literature splits along a fault line that most popular framings miss. **Leeway** concerns alternative possibilities: the agent could have done otherwise (the Principle of Alternate Possibilities, PAP). **Sourcehood** concerns origination: the agent is the source or author of the act, whatever the modal facts about alternatives. The two come apart.

Harry Frankfurt's 1969 cases ("Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility," *Journal of Philosophy*) — confidence high on the citation and the argument's structure — are the wedge. Construct a case in which a counterfactual intervener (Black) stands ready to force Jones to do A if Jones shows any sign of choosing otherwise, but Jones chooses A on his own and Black never acts. Jones could *not* have done otherwise — the intervener seals every alternative — yet our judgment that Jones is responsible is untouched, because the explanation of what Jones actually did runs entirely through Jones. If the intuition holds (it is contested; Widerker and others press the "dilemma defense," and I grant the dispute is live), leeway is shown to be inessential to responsibility. The real question is not *could he have done otherwise* but *was he the source*.

This relocation is decisive for a no-aseity framework. The leeway question is where libertarianism makes its stand, and it is precisely the question determinism appears to settle by fiat: if state produces successor state, the alternatives are not "open" in the categorical sense the libertarian wants. But sourcehood is a different question, and — this is the chapter's spine — **determinism does not answer it**. Determinism is a thesis about dynamics: a complete state of the world together with the laws fixes the successor state (Movement VI). That thesis tells you the trajectory is fixed. It does not tell you who, if anyone, authored the act that the trajectory includes. Authorship is not a modal fact about branching; it is a fact about whose act it is. The framework's wager is that this second fact is conferred, inhabited, and re-spoken — and that it survives the loss of leeway intact.

## 3. Analytic compatibilism, and why the standard version is not yet enough

Compatibilism in its classical form (Hobbes, Hume, and in the twentieth century Schlick, Ayer, and most rigorously Frankfurt and Fischer) holds that freedom is the absence of *relevant* constraint, not the absence of causation. The unfree act is the coerced, compelled, or manipulated one; the free act is the one that flows from the agent's own will in the right way. Determinism is irrelevant because determination is not constraint — being caused to want what you want is not the same as being chained.

Frankfurt's 1971 hierarchical account ("Freedom of the Will and the Concept of a Person," *Journal of Philosophy* — confidence high) gives the canonical "right way": an agent acts freely when she acts from a first-order desire that her second-order volition endorses — when she *wants to want* what moves her, and is not, like the unwilling addict, dragged by a desire she repudiates. The willing addict and the unwilling addict have the same dynamics and the same drug; what differs is identification. Freedom is structural: a relation between orders of desire.

John Martin Fischer and Mark Ravizza, *Responsibility and Control* (1998 — confidence high), refine this into **guidance control**: an act is free when it issues from the agent's *own*, **moderately reasons-responsive** mechanism. The mechanism must be responsive to reasons (it would have produced a different act under some range of sufficient reasons to do otherwise) and it must be the agent's own — made own by **taking responsibility**: the agent comes to see herself as an apt target of the reactive attitudes, sees her choices as having effects in the world, and does so on the basis of the evidence. "Regulative control" (the dual power to do otherwise) is abandoned; guidance control (actually guiding the act via one's own reasons-responsive mechanism) is retained as sufficient.

Daniel Dennett, *Elbow Room* (1984) and *Freedom Evolves* (2003 — confidence high), supplies the deflationary register: the free will worth wanting is not the libertarian's contra-causal spark but the evolved, real, degreed capacity for self-control, deliberation, and responsiveness — "could have done otherwise" cashed out as a fact about agent-types and near possible worlds, not a metaphysical exemption from physics.

All of this is correct as far as it goes, and the chapter inherits it. But standard compatibilism, left here, is vulnerable to the charge that it has smuggled in exactly what the no-aseity framework forbids: **a self that does the identifying, the taking, the guiding** — a self treated as a standing origin, an unexplained explainer. Frankfurt's regress is the symptom. Why is the second-order volition *authoritative*? Because the agent identifies with it. And the third order? At some point identification must "resound through the whole tower of desire" (Frankfurt's later "decisive identification") and simply *settle* — and that settling looks suspiciously like a little aseity, a point where the buck stops in the self. Gary Watson's and J. David Velleman's critiques press exactly here: the hierarchy needs an authoritative self to halt the regress, and compatibilism tends to install one without paying for it.

This is where conferred sourcehood diverges from ordinary compatibilism. **It does not halt the regress in the self. It denies that the regress needs a self-standing terminus at all.** The buck does not stop *in* the agent as an uncaused identifier; it stops at a standpoint that is itself conferred — caused, constituted, re-spoken — and whose authority is not self-generated but bestowed-and-inhabited. Compatibilism says: the act is free when it flows from the self in the right way. Conferred sourcehood says: there is no self that stands outside the flow to receive it; the "self" is the continuous activity of constituting an author, and that activity is no more self-caused than anything else. It is compatibilism without the smuggled origin — no-aseity all the way down.

## 4. The pivot: Strawson's Basic Argument is the no-aseity thesis applied to the will

Galen Strawson, "The Impossibility of Moral Responsibility" (*Philosophical Studies*, 1994 — confidence high on citation and reconstruction), gives the cleanest statement of the hard case, and the framework's relation to it is the hinge of the whole chapter. The Basic Argument:

1. You do what you do, in any situation, because of the way you are.
2. To be truly responsible for what you do, you must be truly responsible for the way you are — at least in certain mental respects.
3. To be truly responsible for the way you are, you must have brought it about that you are the way you are.
4. But you cannot have brought yourself about from nothing; any bringing-about presupposes a prior nature you did not choose.
5. So you cannot be truly responsible for the way you are, and therefore cannot be truly responsible for what you do.

The hidden premise — the one the framework wants to surface and *keep* — is at step 3–4: true responsibility would require being **causa sui**, the cause of oneself. Strawson is explicit that the causa sui is "the best self-contradiction in the history of thought." Robert Sapolsky, *Determined* (2023 — confidence high), gives the same regress its empirical body: every link of "the way you are" is itself caused — by the second before, the hormones that morning, the childhood, the culture, the genes, the evolutionary deep time — turtles all the way down, no gap anywhere for an unmoved chooser to stand. Derk Pereboom, *Living Without Free Will* (2001 — confidence high), reaches the incompatibilist conclusion by a different route (below) but shares the core: desert-grounding responsibility requires a kind of origination determinism (and indeterminism) cannot supply.

**Here is the framework's move, and it is a concession, not a rebuttal.** The Basic Argument is *correct*. It is the no-aseity thesis applied to the will: nothing is self-standing, and the will is not the exception. Nothing is causa sui — the framework asserts this of *everything*, so of course it asserts it of the will. Grant Sapolsky every link in the regress. There is no point in the causal history where an uncaused self-author inserts itself. The libertarian's contra-causal will is exactly as impossible as the self-grounding being the framework denies everywhere else. Van Inwagen's Consequence Argument (*An Essay on Free Will*, 1983 — confidence high: if determinism is true, our acts are consequences of the laws and the remote past, and we have no power over those, so no power over our acts) is *granted* without resistance — it is the no-aseity point in modal dress. The framework does not flinch at any of this.

The disagreement is one inferential step, and only one. Strawson, Sapolsky, and Pereboom infer **"therefore no free will, no true responsibility."** That inference is valid *only if* free will and responsibility require aseitic self-origination — only if "true source" means "causa sui." **It does not.** That is the unargued identification the framework refuses. The argument establishes that the will has no aseity. It does not establish that sourcehood requires aseity. Conferral severs the two.

## 5. Conferred sourcehood stated

The thesis: **One can be the author of one's acts without being their uncaused cause.** Sourcehood is conferred — bestowed by relation, constituted in address, inhabited at a standpoint, and re-enacted in each act — not generated *ex nihilo* by a self that stands prior to its own causes.

The forced choice the literature presents — *either* libertarian causa sui (Kane's self-forming actions, event-causal libertarianism, *The Significance of Free Will*, 1996 — confidence high) *or* hard-incompatibilist dissolution (Pereboom, Strawson, Sapolsky) — is a false dilemma generated by the shared assumption that real sourcehood must be aseitic. Drop the assumption and a third option appears, and it is not a compromise between the two: it is the denial of their common premise. Conferred sourcehood does not give the libertarian a watered-down origination; it tells the libertarian he was demanding the wrong thing, the same thing he demands nowhere else in his metaphysics. And it does not give the hard incompatibilist a redefinition-by-fiat ("let's just call shaped behavior 'free'"); it identifies a real feature of agency — developed in §6 and §7 — that survives the loss of aseity and that the hard incompatibilist's description leaves out.

The cross-traditional witnesses establish that this is not an *ad hoc* dodge invented to save compatibilism, but the standing resolution of freedom-under-decree wherever the problem has been posed seriously:

- **Ash'ari *kasb* (acquisition)** — confidence moderate on the technical reconstruction, high that the doctrine exists and has this shape. In classical kalām, God creates (*khalq*) the act; the human *acquires* (*kasb*) it. The human is the locus and owner of the act — responsible, addressed, judged — without being its ontological originator. This is conferred sourcehood in a fully deterministic theology: the dynamics belong entirely to God; the sourcehood is acquired at the human standpoint. The doctrine was *designed* to hold responsibility and total divine determination together, and it did so without ever demanding that the human be causa sui.

- **Yoruba *orì*** — confidence moderate. Before birth the person chooses (or receives) their *orì*, the inner head that is destiny; then they live it out. The chosen-and-received destiny is both given and owned. Sourcehood is conferred at a pre-natal standpoint and inhabited in the life — and crucially this is a non-Abrahamic articulation, showing the structure is not an artifact of monotheist apologetics but a recurrent human solution.

- **The Prophetic "tie your camel, then trust in God"** (*iʿqil wa tawakkal*) — confidence moderate on attribution (a well-known hadith, Tirmidhi), high on its meaning. Full agency (tie the camel — really act, really be the source of the tying) *within* the decree (then trust). Not agency *against* providence, not agency *as a gap in* providence, but agency *as conferred inside* it.

These are load-bearing because they show the libertarian's demand — that to be truly free I must be the uncaused origin — is the historical *outlier*, not the default. The cross-cultural default treats sourcehood as conferred. Conferred sourcehood is **stronger** than libertarianism precisely because it never incurred the debt libertarianism cannot pay: it never demanded aseity, so the Basic Argument, fatal to libertarianism, leaves it untouched.

The neuroscience is handled by the same orthogonality. Libet (1983, the readiness potential preceding reported conscious decision — confidence high it exists) and Wegner (*The Illusion of Conscious Will*, 2002 — confidence high) are standardly taken to show conscious will is epiphenomenal or late. Two responses. First, empirically, Schurger et al. (2012, PNAS — confidence high) reinterpret the readiness potential as a stochastic accumulator crossing threshold — an artifact of how the data are time-locked to movement, not the neural shadow of a pre-conscious decision. The "decision was already made" reading is not forced. Second, and more important, *grant Libet entirely*. "Conscious will is late" is a claim about the **timing of a state** — pure dynamics. Sourcehood, on this account, is **not the metaphysical instant of decision**. It is not located at the millisecond where the readiness potential crosses threshold. It is the conferred, second-personally-addressed, continuously re-constituted ownership of the act. The timing of the proximate neural cause is orthogonal to where authorship lives, exactly as Movement VI says the dynamics question is orthogonal to the sourcehood question. Libet could be completely right and conferred sourcehood entirely intact.

## 6. The first float-free component: the second-personal remainder

P.F. Strawson, "Freedom and Resentment" (*Proceedings of the British Academy*, 1962 — confidence high), is the chapter's central positive resource, and it must be read for more than its famous naturalism. Strawson's observation: responsibility is not a fact we read off the agent's causal history and then *respond* to. It is **constituted in the reactive attitudes** — resentment, gratitude, indignation, hurt feelings, love, forgiveness — which express our involvement in relationships as members of a community of agents who address one another. To hold someone responsible just *is* to be susceptible to these attitudes toward them; it is to take the **participant stance**, the second-personal stance of one who addresses a *you*, as against the **objective stance** of one who manages, predicts, treats, or handles an *it*.

The claim I extract and defend as falsifiable:

> **"I hold YOU responsible" is an address between two standpoints that does not reduce without remainder to forward-looking behavior-shaping.**

The contrast case is sharp and is the agency-analog of the meaning chapter's A/B remainder. Sapolsky (and Pereboom's "free-will skepticism," and the public-health/quarantine model of Pereboom's later work) propose to **keep the consequences and drop the desert**: abandon blame and basic-desert responsibility, retain a purely forward-looking practice of protection, deterrence, rehabilitation, incapacitation. On this picture, when I "hold you responsible," what is *really* going on, fully and without remainder, is that I am applying a behavior-shaping intervention — a sanction calibrated to modify your future dispositions and signal to third parties. The reactive attitude is, at best, an evolved heuristic for delivering the intervention; strip it away and nothing of value is lost.

The float-free claim is that **this reduction leaves a remainder**, and that the remainder is principled. Resentment is not a covert prediction-and-correction device aimed at your future states. It is an **address**: it treats you as a *second person*, a someone-to-whom, a co-occupant of the space of reasons who can be *answered to* and can answer back. The proof that the remainder is real is the **asymmetry of substitution**. Forward-looking shaping is fully specifiable in the third person, over the agent as object: *this organism's* future behavior is the target. But "I resent *you*" cannot be cashed out in third-person terms without changing its subject. Forgiveness makes this vivid: to forgive is not to stop applying a sanction (one can cease sanctioning while still resenting, and can forgive while still, for prudential reasons, sanctioning). Forgiveness is the *release of a second person from a debt that was owed between standpoints*. There is no forward-looking behavioral fact that *is* forgiveness; a quarantine model has no place to put it. It can recommend ceasing treatment when treatment no longer pays. It cannot forgive, because forgiveness presupposes a *you* who was a source, not an *it* that was a hazard.

Stephen Darwall, *The Second-Person Standpoint* (2006 — confidence high), gives the structure its sharpest form: moral address presupposes a second-personal authority and accountability — I address a claim *to* you and presuppose your standing to *be* so addressed, to answer, and to address me in turn — that is irreducible to any third-personal, agent-neutral fact about states of affairs or about your dispositions. The reactive attitudes are the lived form of this address.

**Why this floats free of the residue.** The residue is "there must be an inhabited standpoint." The second-personal remainder asserts something the residue does not contain: that responsibility is constituted in the *relation between two* standpoints — addresser and addressed — and that this relation is not derivable from facts about either standpoint taken singly, nor from any third-personal description of behavior-shaping. The residue gives you *a* locus. The remainder requires *two*, in a stance of mutual address, and asserts that the stance is not reducible to management. That is a substantive, non-trivial proposition. The bare necessity of a locus does not entail that holding-responsible is irreducibly second-personal; one could have a locus and a fully objective, management-only practice (this is precisely what Sapolsky recommends and believes is all there really is). So the second-personal remainder says more than the residue, and it could be false.

**Why it is principled and not ad hoc.** The worry is that I have merely *stipulated* a remainder to avoid reduction. The defense: the remainder does explanatory work the reduction cannot. (i) It explains the **felt difference** between being held responsible and being managed — between an accusation and a diagnosis — which the reduction must dismiss as illusion but which structures every actual moral relationship. (ii) It explains why **apology and forgiveness** are not redundant once optimal behavior-shaping is in place: they transact in the currency of address, not consequence. (iii) It explains why **the objective stance feels like a withdrawal of regard** — why treating a competent adult purely objectively (as one treats the very young, the severely impaired, the merely dangerous) is experienced by them as a denial of standing, not merely a change of policy. A pure behavior-shaping theory predicts the objective stance should feel like *better, fairer treatment* (no more irrational blame!); the second-personal account predicts, correctly, that it feels like *exclusion from the community of addressers*. The remainder earns its keep.

**The failure condition (stated now, adjudicated in §9).** If "I hold you responsible" reduces without remainder to behavior-shaping — if the asymmetry of substitution dissolves under analysis, if forgiveness and apology turn out to be nothing but optimization heuristics with no second-personal content, if the objective stance carries no loss — then the second-personal remainder is false, and with it falls this half of the chapter's positive claim. The claim is hostage to that possibility. That is what makes it a claim.

## 7. The second float-free component: the occasionalism of agency

The meaning chapter's second component was the occasionalism of meaning: meaning is *withdrawable without subtraction of fact* and *re-bestowable at the locus*. The agency analog:

> **Authorship drains and returns without a corresponding subtraction of dynamical fact. Agency-loss does not always track a dynamical difference.**

The phenomena are familiar and undeniable. In addiction and compulsion, the agent **watches themselves act against their will** — the unwilling addict of Frankfurt's analysis, but now considered diachronically: the drained authorship of the act one disowns even while performing it. In dissociation, the act proceeds and the author is absent. In Richard Schwartz's Internal Family Systems model (confidence moderate — IFS is a clinical framework, empirically uneven, but phenomenologically apt), the same person is sometimes **part-led** (a protector or exile has the helm; the act issues from the system but is not Self-authored) and sometimes **Self-led** (acting from the calm, centered Self; authorship present). The behavior may be similar; the *authorship* differs. And authorship **returns** — in recovery, in integration, in the IFS "unblending," in the moment the compulsion's grip loosens and the act becomes one's own again.

The falsifiable core is the word **always**. The claim is *not* that authorship never correlates with dynamics — of course it often does; addiction has neurobiology, dissociation has correlates, and where they leave dynamical traces, fine. The claim is the **occasionalist** one: agency-loss does **not always** track a dynamical difference. There are cases where the dynamical description is the same — same reasons-responsive profile, same proximate causes, same behavioral output, no detectable difference in the state-to-successor-state transition — and yet authorship is present in one instance and drained in the other, because authorship is *conferred and re-enacted*, not read off the dynamics. **If agency-loss always tracked a dynamical difference, the claim would be false** — agency would just *be* a dynamical property under another name, and there would be no occasionalism, only neuroscience awaiting completion.

This connects to the chapter's account of what sourcehood *is*. Christine Korsgaard, *Self-Constitution* (2009 — confidence high), holds that agency is not a standing property of a self but the **continuous activity of constituting oneself as the author of one's actions** — to act is to *unify* oneself around a principle, and the self that authors is *produced in and by* the acting, not presupposed before it. There is no self-caused origin sitting behind the act; there is the act of self-constitution, re-performed each time. This is exactly the no-aseity shape: the author is not a prior, self-standing thing that the act expresses; the author is constituted in the authoring, conferred each occasion. Fischer and Ravizza's **taking responsibility** is the same insight from the responsibility side — ownership of a mechanism is something one *does*, an achievement and an ongoing stance, not a metaphysical given. Sourcehood is **re-enacted each act, never a standing self-caused origin.** This is why "occasionalism" is the right word: as in the meaning chapter, the relevant property is not deposited once in the substance but re-bestowed at each occasion, at the locus.

**Why this floats free of the residue.** The residue says "there must be an inhabited standpoint." The occasionalism of agency says something the residue does not: that *the same inhabited standpoint, with the same dynamics, can have authorship present or absent* — that authorship is a **further, withdrawable fact** not fixed by the standpoint's existence or by its dynamical state. The residue gives you a standpoint that is always there. The occasionalism claim says authorship is *not* always there even when the standpoint is, and that its presence does not reduce to a dynamical difference. That is more than the residue, and — critically — it could be false in a way the residue could not. The residue is necessary (you cannot dissolve the standpoint). The occasionalism claim is contingent: it stakes itself on there being cases where authorship varies while dynamics do not.

**Why it is not the residue in disguise.** One might object: "agency lives at the standpoint, and sometimes the standpoint is in a state where it doesn't author — that's just dynamics after all." No: the whole point is the *decoupling*. If every authorship difference were a state difference, the objection would win and the claim would collapse into "agency is a state of the standpoint" — which is residue-plus-neuroscience, not a float-free result. The claim survives only if authorship and dynamics can come apart. The occasionalism of meaning survived the same test in Part IV (meaning withdrawn without any fact changing); this is its agency twin.

**The failure condition (adjudicated in §9).** If agency-loss *always* tracks a dynamical difference — if, in every case of drained authorship, there is a discoverable difference in the state or its transition that constitutes the loss — then authorship just is a dynamical property, the occasionalism is false, and this half of the chapter falls. The claim is hostage to the completion of a reductive neuroscience of authorship. It bets that completion will not come, not as faith but as a structural prediction: authorship is conferred address-and-ownership, and conferral is not a state variable.

## 8. Meeting the opponents head-on

**Van Inwagen, the Consequence Argument.** Granted in full (§4). Our acts are consequences of the past and the laws; we have no power over those; in the leeway sense we cannot do otherwise. The framework does not contest a step. It denies only that this touches sourcehood, which is not a leeway property. The Consequence Argument is the no-aseity thesis in modal form, and the framework is a no-aseity framework: it would be incoherent to resist it.

**Kane, event-causal libertarianism.** Kane's self-forming actions locate freedom in undetermined "torn" choices where indeterminacy amplifies to the macro level and the agent's effort settles the will. The framework's reply is not that Kane is empirically wrong (though the amplification story is fragile — confidence moderate) but that he is *solving the wrong problem*. Kane accepts the libertarian's demand for ultimate origination and tries to buy it with indeterminism. But indeterminism purchases *leeway*, not *sourcehood* — a randomly-settled will is no more *authored* than a determined one; if anything less, since chance is not authorship. Kane pays for aseity in the one currency (indeterminism) that cannot buy it. Conferred sourcehood declines the purchase: it never needed the aseity, so it never needed the indeterminism.

**Pereboom, the four-case manipulation argument.** This is the hardest opponent and the test of whether conferred sourcehood is more than a relabeling. Pereboom (*Living Without Free Will*, 2001; *Free Will, Agency, and Meaning in Life*, 2014 — confidence high) constructs four cases forming a continuum. Case 1: Professor Plum is created moments ago by neuroscientists who manipulate him in real time to commit a murder, having designed him to be exactly as reasons-responsive as an ordinary person — he meets every compatibilist condition (Frankfurt identification, Fischer–Ravizza guidance control, taking responsibility) and yet is intuitively *not* responsible, because manipulated. Case 2: the same, but programmed at the start of life rather than controlled in real time. Case 3: rigorous deterministic upbringing engineered by his community. Case 4: ordinary deterministic causation by nature. Pereboom argues there is **no principled difference** along the continuum: if Plum is not responsible in Case 1, and each step is relevantly similar to the next, then ordinary determined agents in Case 4 are not responsible either. The compatibilist conditions are all met throughout, so they cannot be what is doing the exculpating; manipulation and ordinary determination are on a par.

The framework's answer must show a **principled break** in the continuum that is *not* an appeal to leeway (granted gone) and *not* an appeal to a further compatibilist structural condition (Pereboom built those into all four cases). The break is supplied by the two float-free components, and this is where they earn their place as more than decoration.

Conferred sourcehood distinguishes **shaping-that-leaves-the-second-personal-remainder** from **shaping-that-bypasses-it**. The relevant question across the continuum is not "was he caused?" (yes, throughout — granted) and not "does he meet the structural conditions?" (yes, throughout — granted) but: **is he a standpoint that stands in, and can be drawn into, relations of mutual second-personal address — can he be genuinely held responsible, answer, answer back, apologize, forgive, and be forgiven, as a co-member of the community of addressers — or is he an object being routed through?**

In Case 1, the real-time manipulators do not *address* Plum; they *operate* him. He is, in that moment, on the receiving end of the objective stance made total: an artifact being driven. The exculpation in Case 1 is not that Plum was caused (everyone is caused) but that the manipulation **bypasses the second-personal standpoint** — it makes Plum an *it* in the very act, a conduit for the manipulators' agency, not a *you* who is the source. He is not even a candidate for the reactive attitudes *toward himself as author*; the appropriate target of resentment is the manipulators, because *they* are the authoring standpoints and Plum is their instrument. The occasionalism component makes the same point from inside: Plum's authorship is *drained* — he is part-led with a vengeance, the part being an external will — even though his dynamics are, by stipulation, ordinary-reasons-responsive. This is precisely a case where authorship and dynamics come apart, which §7 said must be possible.

Now the continuum. The reason Case 1's verdict does **not** propagate to Case 4 is that the second-personal remainder and the inhabited authorship are *present* in ordinary determined agents and *absent* in the manipulated one — and their presence or absence does not track the causal facts Pereboom's continuum holds fixed. The ordinary determined agent is not being operated by an authoring standpoint that bypasses his own; his act issues from a standpoint that *is itself* an author — conferred, caused, yes, but inhabited and addressable. He can be drawn into mutual address; he can answer for the act as his; the reactive attitudes find their proper target *in him*, not behind him. There is no further authoring standpoint that has reduced him to a conduit. The manipulation cases exculpate not because of *causation* but because of *displacement of the source* — another agent's authorship has supplanted his, leaving him an instrument. Ordinary determinism displaces no source; it *is* the medium in which the conferred source is constituted.

So the principled break is: **does the etiology route through, and thereby constitute, the agent's own second-personally-addressable authoring standpoint (ordinary causation, including ordinary deterministic upbringing — Cases 3 and 4), or does it bypass and supplant that standpoint by substituting another agent's authorship for it (manipulation — Cases 1 and 2)?** Pereboom will press that this is itself a difference along the continuum that he can blur — that engineered upbringing (Case 3) shades into manipulation. The reply: where the upbringing *constitutes* a standpoint that then authors (the child grows into a co-member of the community of addressers, who can answer for herself), sourcehood is conferred and present; where it *installs a remote control* that bypasses the standpoint (Plum as puppet), sourcehood is absent. The difference is not in the *amount* of causation but in whether the process **yields an addressable author or a routed object** — and that, the second-personal and occasionalist components insist, is a real difference, not reducible to a point on a causal gradient. Pereboom's argument works by holding the compatibilist *structural* conditions fixed and varying only the etiology; it does not, and cannot on its own terms, hold fixed the *second-personal standing* and the *presence of inhabited authorship*, because those are not structural-dispositional facts. That is exactly why they can break a continuum that the structural conditions cannot.

This is the payoff of having insisted the two components float free. If they were just the residue ("there's a standpoint"), they could not break Pereboom's continuum, because a standpoint is present in all four cases. They break it only because they assert something the residue does not: that *addressable authorship* can be present or absent independently of the causal and structural facts the continuum varies. The manipulation argument is met not by a new compatibilist gear but by the very propositions on which the chapter stakes its test.

## 9. Did the sourcehood hold?

The test: PASS requires a positive, falsifiable proposition about agency that floats free of the necessity-of-locus residue ("there must be an inhabited standpoint at all"). FAIL is mere re-description — "agency lives at the inhabited standpoint" restated as if it were a result.

I adjudicate honestly, component by component.

**The residue, conceded.** "Conferred sourcehood lives at the inhabited standpoint" *is* the residue. A chapter resting on that sentence alone has produced nothing; it has renamed the one aseity the framework cannot dissolve. I concede this without qualification. If the chapter's content reduced to that sentence, it would FAIL.

**It does not so reduce, because of two propositions that say more than the residue and can each be false:**

**Proposition 1 (the second-personal remainder), stated exactly:**
> Holding-responsible — "I hold *you* responsible" — is an irreducibly second-personal address between two standpoints (addresser and addressed, each with standing to address and answer) that does not reduce without remainder to any third-personal, forward-looking behavior-shaping description of the agent.
>
> **Falsifiable. It is false if** "I hold you responsible" reduces without remainder to behavior-shaping: if the asymmetry of substitution dissolves under analysis, if forgiveness and apology have no second-personal content beyond optimization, and if the objective stance carries no loss of standing. Under those conditions Sapolsky's quarantine-and-deterrence model captures everything real, and the remainder is illusion.

**Proposition 2 (the occasionalism of agency), stated exactly:**
> Authorship is conferred and re-enacted at each occasion; agency-loss does **not always** track a dynamical difference. There exist cases in which authorship is present in one instance and drained in another with no difference in the dynamical (state-to-successor-state) description.
>
> **Falsifiable. It is false if** agency-loss *always* tracks a dynamical difference — if, for every case of drained or restored authorship, there is a discoverable difference in the state or its transition that constitutes the change. Under that condition authorship is a dynamical property under another name, and a completed neuroscience absorbs it without remainder.

**Do they float free of the residue?** Yes, and the proof is the same for both: the residue is *necessary* (the standpoint cannot be dissolved — that is what makes it residue), but both propositions are *contingent* — each describes a way things could fail to be even given the standpoint. A locus could exist *and* hold-responsible be pure management (Proposition 1 false). A locus could exist *and* every authorship difference be a dynamical difference (Proposition 2 false). Since both propositions can be false while the residue holds, neither is entailed by the residue; both say strictly more. That is the float.

**Do they do work the residue cannot?** Yes — decisively in §8. They break Pereboom's four-case continuum, which the residue alone cannot break (a standpoint is present in all four cases; only *addressable, inhabited authorship* is differentially present, and that is exactly what the two propositions add over the residue). A component that only restated the residue would leave the manipulation argument unanswered. These answer it.

**Verdict: PASS — conditionally and honestly.** The chapter delivers two positive, falsifiable propositions about agency that float free of the necessity-of-locus residue, that do explanatory work the residue cannot, and that meet the strongest opponent (Pereboom) head-on rather than by relabeling. Conferred sourcehood is established as a *sourcehood* account (leeway granted away via Frankfurt), as *no-aseity all the way down* (the Basic Argument and Consequence Argument granted in full; the will conceded to have no aseity), and as *distinct from ordinary compatibilism* (it installs no self-standing self to halt Frankfurt's regress; the author is conferred and re-constituted, never a smuggled origin).

**The exact condition of failure, named as required.** The PASS is not unconditional, and intellectual honesty requires stating what would retract it. **Conferred sourcehood FAILS** — collapses back into mere re-description of the residue — **if and only if both** of the following obtain: (a) the second-personal "you" reduces without remainder to behavior-shaping (Proposition 1 false), **and** (b) agency-loss always tracks a dynamical difference (Proposition 2 false). If both hold, then "I hold you responsible" is nothing but management, authorship is nothing but a state variable, and all that remains is "dynamics run through a standpoint" — which determinism already grants and which is the residue wearing a result's clothes. The chapter would then have shown only that there is a someone where the dynamics pass through: true, necessary, and empty.

The chapter does not claim to have *proven* (a) and (b) false — that would overclaim, and the propositions are falsifiable precisely because their negations are coherent and empirically live. It claims to have located the two propositions whose joint truth carries the PASS and whose joint falsity is the exact failure condition, and to have shown, against Pereboom, that their truth is not idle. That is the most the test permits and the least it demands. The sourcehood held — on the strength of the remainder and the occasionalism, not on the strength of the locus, which was never in question and was never enough.

---

Notes on the verdict's honesty: the single inferential disagreement with Strawson/Sapolsky/Pereboom (§4) is genuine and narrow — the framework concedes every premise and resists only the identification of sourcehood with aseity. The two float-free propositions are stated as hostages to fortune (Sapolsky-style reduction; a reductive neuroscience of authorship), not as proven theorems. If a future neuroscience shows authorship to be a state variable in all cases, Proposition 2 falls; if the second-personal remainder dissolves under analysis, Proposition 1 falls; if both, the chapter fails as marked. That is the falsifiability the test demanded, given without hedging.
