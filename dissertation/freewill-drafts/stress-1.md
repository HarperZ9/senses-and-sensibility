## VERDICT: The strongest draft PASSES — but only on one of its two legs. The chapter's actual standing is a SPLIT, and the honest drafts ([reactive], [pivot]) see this while [compat] overstates.

### Single best candidate proposition (verbatim)

From DRAFT [reactive], §8, Proposition (R):

> **"(R) — The second-personal remainder.** Holding-you-responsible — the second-personal address constitutive of the reactive attitudes — does not reduce without remainder to any forward-looking, first-to-third-person program of behavior-shaping (quarantine, deterrence, formation)."

This is the single best candidate across all three drafts, and the reason is the argument [reactive] attaches to it and [compat] does not: **the monadic/dyadic structural proof.**

### What is genuinely free of the residue

The residue is *monadic*: "there must be an inhabited standpoint." (R) is *dyadic*: it asserts a relation between two standpoints that does not reduce to a one-place control function. The decisive move — present in [reactive] §1.3 and §8, weaker in [compat] §6 — is the **solipsism test**:

> "A solipsistic universe with exactly one inhabited standpoint has the necessity-of-locus residue in full and has *no* second-personal remainder, because there is no one to address."

This is a genuine float-free. It is not the residue re-described, because the residue is *satisfiable in full* while (R) is *false* (the one-standpoint world). A proposition that can be false while the residue is true is, by construction, not entailed by the residue. The arity gap (monadic residue, dyadic claim) is a structural difference, not a verbal one. **This leg passes, and passes cleanly.** [reactive] earns it; [compat] asserts the same conclusion (§6) but never runs the solipsism test that proves detachability — [compat]'s "the residue gives you a locus, the remainder requires two" gestures at arity without the disproving witness.

### What is residue re-described (the second-personal remainder's weak joint)

The *falsifiability* of (R) is softer than either passing draft admits, and [reactive] alone flags it (§8, "The honest residual risk"). The inference is:

> "the hard incompatibilist does not *capture* the remainder" → "the remainder is *real* rather than *eliminable*."

Pereboom/Sapolsky do not claim to reduce the second person; they claim to **abolish** it. [reactive] correctly notes "an abolition is not a reduction" (§1.2) — but this cuts both ways. If the opponent's program is *revisionary* (replace the stance) rather than *analytic* (show the stance was always shaping), then (R)'s falsifier — "a complete forward-looking redescription captures every feature with no residue" — is one the opponent **never attempts**, which makes (R) nearly unfalsifiable *against that opponent*. The remainder is then defended by the *practical inescapability* of the participant stance (P.F. Strawson) — and inescapability-of-a-standpoint is uncomfortably close to the residue. So: the *dyadic structure* floats free; the *irreducibility-vs-eliminability* claim leans back toward "there must be an inhabited stance you cannot exit," which is the residue in second-personal dress. [reactive] concedes exactly this ("won on balance, not won decisively; moderate confidence it defeats a determined eliminativist"). That concession is correct and is the most honest sentence in any of the three drafts.

### The occasionalism of agency (O): residue re-described, dressed as orthogonality

This is where [compat] fails strictly and [reactive]/[pivot] are right.

(O) — "agency-loss does not always track a dynamical difference" — has two readings:
- **Safe reading:** "ownership is enacted at the standpoint." This is monadic. It *is* the residue. [reactive] says so outright (§8: "would be re-description").
- **Load-bearing reading:** the *orthogonality* claim — authorship and dynamics come apart, demonstrably.

The orthogonality claim is what must carry the float, and **under physicalism it is in serious trouble**, exactly as [pivot] argues in its question 2:

> "under any physicalism, the addict watching themselves relapse *is* in a different dynamical state (different prefrontal/striatal activity), so the dissociation you want between authorship-loss and dynamical-fact looks like it collapses."

[pivot] identified the correct fault line without writing the chapter. [reactive] then tries to rescue (O) by relocating the orthogonality demonstration from the *phenomenology* (addiction, dissociation — where there IS a dynamical delta) to the *manipulation cases* (matched dynamics, divergent ownership). This is the right repair, but notice what it costs: (O) no longer floats free **on its own evidence**. Its sole surviving witness is Pereboom's Case 1 — and that is borrowed from (R)'s machinery (the bypass-of-address). So (O) does not independently float; it rides (R). [reactive] half-admits this ("the *same* taking-responsibility that... (O)... require," §6) but still scores (O) as "PASS, conditionally."

**[compat] is the strict failure here.** [compat] §7 rests (O) on the phenomenological cases (addiction watched diachronically, IFS) and asserts "no detectable difference in the state-to-successor-state transition" — a claim physicalism gives no reason to grant and [compat] never defends against the obvious neural-correlate objection. [compat]'s (O) is "residue + a promissory anti-reductive bet," presented as a falsifiable result. That is residue re-described with a hostage-to-future-neuroscience flourish.

The chapter PASSES on **(R)** and only on (R), via the monadic/dyadic arity gap (the solipsism witness). That single proposition genuinely floats free.
