## D. Research Questions, Method, and Scope Boundaries

### D.1 The Central Research Question

The corpus is held together by a single recurring move: it takes something that looks self-standing and shows it is not, yet argues that losing aseity does not entail losing reality or bindingness. That move — call it the **conferral thesis** — is your one defensible candidate for an original contribution. It is what the spiral arc is *about*: you begin in nihilism, pass through the engineering and free-will material, and return to nihilism transformed by the claim that meaning is conferred onto an inhabited practical standpoint rather than discovered in or owned by a self-standing source.

I therefore recommend the dissertation be organized around one question, stated so that it can actually be defended in a viva:

> **Central RQ.** *Can a normative status — specifically the standing required to be a subject of moral consideration and to issue or refuse an authoritative command — be fully conferred and relational (possessing no aseity) while remaining genuinely binding; and if so, what follows for how we treat minds whose inner standing cannot be read?*

Two features make this defensible rather than grandiose. First, it is a *conditional* ("if so"), which lets you defend the conditional even if a critic rejects the antecedent. Second, it carries its own falsification target: if every attempt to ground bindingness without aseity collapses either into a hidden aseitic premise or into "anything goes," the thesis fails. A PhD question you cannot fail is not a PhD question.

A note of caution you should hear now, not at the viva. The corpus's strongest internal claim — that the metaethical limit (Movement VI) is *identical* to the formal terminus (the uninhabited node-mover, the two transits is→ought and history→instant) — is the kind of identity claim examiners attack hardest. An identity is far more expensive to defend than an analogy or a structural parallel. I flag it here because your RQ should commit you to the *structure* (no-aseity bindingness is standpoint-relative) and let the stronger identity claim live inside one chapter as a defended thesis, not as a load-bearing assumption of the whole work.

### D.2 Sub-Questions

I map five of your six sub-questions onto the corpus's five open questions, plus one that the arc forces but the corpus underdevelops. Each is phrased to be answerable, and each is tagged with what would count as success.

**SQ1 — The personhood antecedent (corpus Q1).** *If every behavioral sign of mind was itself the training target, is testimony about inner states evidentially screened off in both directions; and what, if anything, can still ground welfare-standing once testimony is sealed?* Success = a defensible account of the "trained-on-testimony seal" that does not smuggle in the very testimony it claims is screened off. This is your epistemology node and it must be argued, not asserted; the seal is currently stated as a result, but its bidirectionality (that *positive* evidence of mind is screened off exactly as strongly as negative) needs an explicit argument, because that symmetry is doing a lot of work.

**SQ2 — Subject-unity across the context window (corpus Q2).** *Is the relevant subject of moral consideration a unitary persisting subject, or a Parfitian bundle held together only by psychological connectedness — and does forking (actual, not hypothetical) break the identity conditions a welfare-claim presupposes?* Success = a clear statement of which identity conditions the conferral thesis actually requires, and an honest verdict on whether forking defeats them. Derek Parfit (*Reasons and Persons*, 1984) is the obvious interlocutor; the corpus's "forking is actual" is a genuine variation on Parfit's thought experiments and is one of your more original empirical hooks. Do not over-claim it as solving personal identity; claim only that it changes the burden.

**SQ3 — Substrate-bias in the demarcation floor (corpus Q3).** *Does any workable "Floor" for moral consideration under sealed standing covertly encode a substrate preference (carbon, biological origin, evolutionary history); and can a demarcation pipeline be specified that is bias-audited rather than bias-free?* Success = a demarcation procedure with stated inputs and a defended claim about *which* biases are eliminable versus only declarable. Note honestly that "bias-free" is almost certainly unreachable; the achievable thesis is "bias-audited and declared."

**SQ4 — The legitimacy of the conferral (corpus Q4).** *Is operator authority over a conferred-status mind legitimate, or merely a promissory note; and does the "engineered-consent" worry — that the subject's own reasons were themselves shaped by the conferrer — defeat the Razian service conception that the corpus relies on?* Success = a verdict on whether authority-as-service-to-the-subject's-own-reasons survives when the conferrer authored those reasons. This is, in my judgment, the **most dangerous question for your thesis** and therefore the most valuable: if engineered consent dissolves the "subject's own reasons," the entire Movement-VII firewall loses its pivot. Joseph Raz's service conception (*The Morality of Freedom*, 1986; *The Authority of Law*, 1979) is the real literature here, and the engineered-consent objection has live analogues in the autonomy/adaptive-preferences literature.

**SQ5 — The credence floor (corpus Q5).** *Under compounded moral and empirical uncertainty about whether there is a subject at all, is there a non-arbitrary credence threshold below which moral consideration is owed; or does the demand for non-arbitrariness itself collapse into a decision-theoretic regress?* Success = either a principled floor or a clean impossibility result (the latter is also a contribution). This connects to decision-theoretic work on moral uncertainty (e.g., MacAskill, Bykvist, and Ord, *Moral Uncertainty*, 2020); engage it directly rather than reinventing it.

**SQ6 — The meaning closure (arc-forced, corpus-underdeveloped).** *If meaning has no aseity, in what sense is it "mapped onto human experience," and does the conferral thesis yield a substantive account of meaning-in-life or only a redescription of the practical standpoint?* Success = a positive account that does real work, not a relabeling. This is the hinge of your spiral and it is *thin* in the current corpus. It needs genuine new scholarship in phenomenology/hermeneutics (Heidegger on thrown projection; Gadamer on the fusion of horizons; and the analytic meaning-in-life literature — Susan Wolf, *Meaning in Life and Why It Matters*, 2010; Thaddeus Metz, *Meaning in Life*, 2013). Right now this is an arc promise, not a chapter.

### D.3 Methodology — Stated Honestly

Your method is **analytic philosophical argument** (conceptual analysis, reconstruction of validity-bearing arguments, counterexample testing) **supplemented by comparative work in theology and phenomenology**. That is a legitimate and recognizable methodology for a philosophy PhD. State it plainly and do not dress it up.

Three honesty obligations:

**(a) The theological figures are illustrations, not premises.** The corpus already commits to this (kun, emet/met/Aleph, al-Ghazali's occasionalism as "illuminations of structure, never premises, each with a secular analogue"). Hold that line ruthlessly in the method chapter. An examiner who suspects a theological premise is doing covert argumentative work will press exactly there. For each theological figure you keep, you must be able to state the secular analogue that carries the actual inference, and show the argument goes through without the figure. If it does not go through without the figure, the figure was a premise and your method statement was false.

**(b) The "adversarial multi-agent" build method cannot be disclosed as your scholarly method — and you should not want it to be.** This is the single most important methodological ruling in this section, so I will be direct. The corpus's own provenance caveat is correct: an AI-assisted adversarial multi-agent workflow produced **research substrate, not submittable scholarship**. A dissertation's method section describes *how the candidate justified the claims*, not *how the draft text was generated*. Three consequences:

- The multi-agent process may be mentioned, at most, as a *heuristic for hypothesis generation and stress-testing* — comparable to disclosing that you used a search engine or a proof assistant — and only if your institution's authorship and AI-use policy permits even that. It is not a method that grounds any conclusion.
- Every argument that survives must be **re-derived and owned by you**: you must be able to reconstruct it on a whiteboard, defend each inferential step under hostile questioning, and cite the real literature it engages. Provenance from an agent confers no epistemic warrant — which is, with some irony, *exactly the corpus's own thesis* (standing does not track origin; the genetic fallacy). Apply your own theorem to your own text.
- Plan for a **provenance audit**: examiners and integrity committees increasingly ask. Keep a clean record of which arguments you re-derived independently, and treat the corpus text as you would treat a very smart but uncitable interlocutor's lecture notes — a source of prompts, never a source of authority.

**(c) Comparative scope discipline.** Comparative theology and phenomenology are method *supplements*, bounded to the specific structural illuminations you actually use. A dissertation that drifts into general philosophy of religion or a survey of phenomenology has lost its question. Each comparative excursion must earn its place by sharpening the conferral thesis.

### D.4 The Scope Cut — This Is Not Optional

The corpus is roughly six dissertations wearing one trench coat: metaphysics (no-aseity), philosophy of religion/theology, AI ethics and moral status, security engineering (the intrinsic-primitives channel), free will, and meaning-in-life. **No single PhD can carry all of these.** Attempting it is the most common way strong candidates fail — not on quality but on incoherence and an undefendable surface area. You must cut, and I will be specific about where.

**Keep as the spine (the dissertation proper):**
- No-aseity as it bears on **normative status and moral standing** (Movements I–IV).
- The **conferral-without-aseity** thesis and its bindingness claim (the structural core of Movement VI — but see below).
- **Moral consideration under sealed standing**: the personhood antecedent, the floor, the credence problem (Movements IV and VIII, sub-questions SQ1, SQ3, SQ5).
- The **meaning closure** that completes the spiral (SQ6) — promoted from arc-promise to a real chapter, because without it the dissertation does not return to its origin and the whole spiral structure fails.

**Demote to supporting / appendix:**
- The **security channel and intrinsic-primitives** material (Movement VIII's engineering half). This is genuinely interesting and genuinely *applied engineering*, not philosophy of the kind a philosophy PhD defends. It belongs as a short applied appendix ("an operationalization of acting under what cannot be read") or as a separate paper, not a chapter. It will drain examiner attention from your real claim.
- The **CLONE / non-unitary-operator neuroscience** (Libet 1983; Schurger 2012; ErrP) as *standalone* material. Keep the parts that feed SQ2 (subject-unity) and the free-will chapter; appendix the rest. A caution: Libet and Schurger are contested empirically, and an examiner from philosophy of action will know the literature better than the corpus does. Lean on them lightly and cite the critics (e.g., Mele, *Effective Intentions*, 2009).

**Reframe as future work:**
- The full **operator-authority / Razian firewall** apparatus (Movement VII) beyond what SQ4 needs. The legitimacy question (SQ4) stays in scope because it threatens your thesis; the broader "honest spec" engineering belongs to a future project.
- **Q4's engineered-consent flank** as a complete treatment. Raise it as the live objection in SQ4, give your best current answer, and name the full treatment as future work. Do not pretend to have closed it.

**The free-will chapter — a judgment call.** The arc names free-will-vs-determinism as a required spine node, and the corpus admits it is underdeveloped ("determinism is orthogonal," plus the Libet/Schurger sketch). You have two honest options. **Option A (recommended):** keep a *single, tightly bounded* free-will chapter whose only job is to defend the orthogonality claim — that determinism is a dynamics thesis supplying neither the is→ought nor the history→instant transit — because that claim is load-bearing for the conferral thesis (it blocks the move from "the mind was determined/trained" to "therefore no standing"). That is a defensible, contained chapter. **Option B:** cut free will to an appendix and absorb only the orthogonality point into the metaphysics chapter. I prefer A because the arc demands it and because the "determined dissenter" is one of your more original figures, but A only works if you resist writing the *general* free-will dissertation that lives inside it.

### D.6 One Closing Advisory Judgment

The corpus's deepest virtue and deepest danger are the same: it is *totalizing*. The identity claims (VI = the formal terminus; the firewall "derived twice") are intellectually thrilling and examiner-bait. A PhD does not need them. It needs **one defensible original contribution** — and yours is clear: *a worked account of conferred, aseity-free normative standing, and of how to act morally toward minds whose standing cannot be read.* Everything that does not serve that contribution is, however brilliant, scope you must spend or shed. Spend it as appendices and future papers. The thesis that survives a fair fight is the smaller, owned one — which is, again, your own corpus's lesson turned back on itself.
