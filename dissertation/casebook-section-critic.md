## D — The Rigor / Breadth-Liability Critic (adversarial)

A thirteen-name roster is a liability before it is an asset. In an analytic-philosophy viva the examiner does not read breadth as erudition; she reads it as a search query that returned too many results. The default inference is *AI-assembled* or, only marginally better, *autodidact-magpie* — and once that prior is set, every genuine citation is re-read through it. My job is to be the examiner who is already suspicious, and to triage before she does.

**One procedural caveat, stated up front [high confidence].** The brief asserts "thirteen famous names" but the casebook text in front of me names only a subset explicitly: Libet (1983), Schurger (2012), and then the popular-science/anecdotal cluster the brief itself flags — Sagan, Harari, Hawking, Sapolsky, Jung, Tesla, Caesar, Lincoln. That is ten at most, and four of those (Tesla/Caesar/Lincoln/Sagan) are not philosophers at all. I will not invent the remaining names to hit thirteen; doing so would reproduce the exact failure mode I am here to catch. **If the candidate cannot produce the full thirteen from the actual manuscript, the count itself is the first red flag** — it suggests the roster was assembled to a target number rather than to an argumentative need.

---

### The single inclusion rule

> **A figure earns a place in the body text if and only if removing them forces you to rewrite a premise. If removing them only weakens a *sentence*, they are illustration and belong in a footnote or are cut.**

Everything below is an application of that rule. The test is not "is the claim true" or "is the figure famous" — it is "does an inference *depend* on this citation, or does the citation merely *decorate* an inference that stands without it."

### Tier 1 — LOAD-BEARING (removal breaks an argument)

**Benjamin Libet — *Time of Conscious Intention to Act in Relation to Onset of Cerebral Activity* (Brain, 1983) [high].**
Claim used: the readiness potential (RP) precedes the reported moment of conscious will (W) by a measurable interval (~350–550 ms before W, ~550 ms before movement), and Libet preserves a "conscious veto" window. **Attaches to:** Movement IV's *non-unitary subject* — "the reflective self vetoes the motor self." This is not decoration; the veto is doing argumentative labor, because the framework needs the *plural* brain (a sub-personal initiator plus a reflective vetoer) and Libet is where the veto comes from. **Verdict: LOAD-BEARING.** Caveat the candidate must pre-empt: introspective W-timing is methodologically contested, and the veto is the most-attacked part of Libet's own apparatus. Cite it, but cite it *as contested*, not as settled neuroscience.

**Aaron Schurger et al. — *An accumulator model for spontaneous neural activity prior to self-initiated movement* (PNAS, 2012) [high].**
Claim used: the RP is (on the stochastic-accumulator reading) an artifact of averaging time-locked to movement onset — spontaneous fluctuations crossing a threshold — *not* a neural "decision" preceding awareness. **Attaches to:** Movement IV, but in tension with Libet, and that tension is the point. Schurger is load-bearing precisely *because* the framework's "the brain is plural" survives the deflation of the naive Libet story: even if the RP is not a literal pre-conscious decision, the sub-personal/reflective split is still readable off the accumulator dynamics. **Verdict: LOAD-BEARING** — and arguably the candidate's strongest move, because pairing Libet *with* his most serious modern critic is the opposite of a famous-name parade; it is a live methodological dispute the candidate is adjudicating. Keep both. Removing Schurger leaves the candidate exposed to "you're citing 1983 neuroscience that's been superseded."

**A third load-bearing slot belongs to whoever supplies the is→ought / history→instant *philosophical* machinery — and the casebook does not yet name them [moderate].**
Movement VI ("determinism is orthogonal") and Part III ("conferred sourcehood") are doing analytic work that Libet and Schurger *cannot* underwrite — neuroscience supplies dynamics, not the normative transit. The orthogonality thesis is recognizably in the lineage of compatibilist and "two-perspectives" arguments (the candidate will need a *named* interlocutor here — Strawson's reactive-attitudes framing, Frankfurt/Fischer-style sourcehood compatibilism, or a Sellarsian manifest/scientific-image split for the "inhabited standpoint"). **Verdict: there is a LOAD-BEARING SLOT that is currently empty.** This is the most serious finding: the genuinely philosophical spine of the dissertation is under-cited, while the periphery is over-cited. That inversion is itself the AI-tell.

### Tier 2 — ILLUSTRATION-ONLY (vivid; cite as color, never as authority)

**Robert Sapolsky — *Determined: A Science of Life Without Free Will* (2023) [high it exists; moderate on using it].**
Sapolsky-the-researcher (stress neuroendocrinology, primate work) is citable; **Sapolsky-the-popularizer** arguing hard incompatibilist no-free-will in a trade book is *exactly* the witness the framework's Movement VI is positioned *against* — determinism is orthogonal, so a determinist polemic is a foil, not support. Use him to state the view the candidate rejects, in one sentence, with the trade-book status flagged. **Verdict: ILLUSTRATION-ONLY**, and only as a foil. Never as authority for a premise.

**Carl Sagan [high he's quotable; low on argumentative value].**
A Sagan line ("we are a way for the cosmos to know itself," or the pale-blue-dot register) is atmosphere. It attaches to nothing in the result-set; it cannot survive the inclusion rule. **Verdict: ILLUSTRATION-ONLY at best**, and I would push it toward CUT.

### Tier 3 — CUT or relegate to footnote (reception-risk exceeds value)

**Yuval Noah Harari [high].** Popular macro-history. In an analytic viva, citing *Sapiens*/*Homo Deus* for any claim about mind, agency, or meaning reads as a category error — it imports trade-publishing authority into a domain that has primary literature. **Reception risk:** high; signals the candidate reached for the bestseller rather than the journal. **Verdict: CUT** (or footnote only as a popular-reception data point, never as a premise).

**Stephen Hawking [high].** A physicist on cosmology is irrelevant to conferred sourcehood and meaning-closure; a Hawking quote on free will or philosophy ("philosophy is dead") is actively self-defeating in a *philosophy* dissertation. **Verdict: CUT.**

**Carl Jung [high].** Contested standing in analytic philosophy and in scientific psychology alike; archetypes/collective-unconscious machinery will read as non-naturalistic and will *cost* the candidate credibility on the very page meaning-closure needs maximal rigor. If a depth-psychology point is genuinely needed, it can be made without the Jungian apparatus. **Verdict: CUT** (footnote at most, and only with an explicit "contested" hedge).

**Tesla / Julius Caesar / Abraham Lincoln [high].** These are *anecdotes*, not evidence. A figure invoked for a biographical vignette (genius, crossing-the-Rubicon decisiveness, "with malice toward none") is the textbook "famous-name parade" tell — it is the single fastest way to make an examiner conclude the breadth is ornamental. None attaches to a result in the set; each fails the inclusion rule outright. **Verdict: CUT, all three.** If the candidate wants the rhetorical beat, it goes in an epigraph, unfootnoted, owned as rhetoric — not in the argument.

### The finding that matters most

The roster is **inverted**: the periphery (popularizers, historical anecdotes) is over-represented and over-cited, while the **philosophical spine** (the is→ought transit, sourcehood compatibilism, the manifest/scientific-image standpoint that "the inhabited locus" presupposes) is **under-cited or unnamed**. An examiner will notice that the load-bearing claims lean on two neuroscience papers and a cloud of color, with the *analytic* commitments under-defended. Fixing the breadth-liability is not primarily a matter of *cutting* the eight weak names — it is *adding* the two or three real interlocutors the arguments already depend on but do not yet cite. Cut to four, name the missing analytic sources, and the "AI-assembled breadth" tell disappears, because what remains is not a parade — it is a defensible dependency graph.
