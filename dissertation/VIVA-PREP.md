## Q1 — THE SPINE

**Question.** Your contribution statement says the explanatory gap is "relocated to the conferral-seam and forced there." But if P1 — your single load-bearing premise — is merely a declared starting point rather than an earned result, the whole structure rests on a stipulation. Why should the committee accept a premise you yourself concede you cannot prove?

**Best Honest Answer.**
P1 is not a uniform premise; the P1-defense adjudication establishes a precise internal distinction, and the answer turns on it entirely. In its *thin* reading — that any conferral act, as an occurring token event, occupies a spatiotemporal index — P1 is *not* merely declared. It is derived non-circularly from premises both the deflationary physicalist and the Prāsaṅgika Mādhyamika already grant: the Peircean type/token distinction (a token "does not exist"; it is "significant only as occurring just when and where it does"), spatiotemporal individuation of tropes, and Kim's property-exemplification account of events (a time-constituent is constitutive of the event's structure, not a posterior label). The performative regress closes the loop: the opponent who denies that her denial is a located token must enact another located token to make that denial. The circularity audit clears: the index is derived from the general metaphysics of token individuation, not from any conferral-specific assumption. This is the P1-FORCED-CONVENTIONALLY verdict.

In its *thick* reading — that conferral occupies a *perspectival* standpoint, a "from-here-as-this" structure — P1 is a declared premise, and the dissertation says so explicitly. The conventional-dialectical pressure on the thick joint (the burden-shift: anyone arguing is committedly treating her act as this-partition-and-not-that-one) is genuine but does not derive the thick index; it sustains it as a conventional operative commitment, not a proof. The distinction is held throughout.

The structure therefore rests on a genuine, bounded, non-circular result (the thin forcing) plus a declared extension (the thick premise) whose dialectical support is real but acknowledged as support, not proof. The committee should accept the thin result because it is earned; it should accept the thick premise dialectically if the burden-shift is persuasive at the conventional level. Neither acceptance requires swallowing a bare stipulation.

**The Trap.**
The examiner wants to know whether the candidate has collapsed the thin/thick distinction — either overclaiming that the full perspectival premise is proved, or under-selling by treating the whole premise as unearned. Both failures are fatal. The overclaim is philosophically dishonest; the under-sell abandons the genuine conventional result.

**Fallback if Pressed.**
Concede openly: the thick index is undischarged. The thin forcing stands. The contribution on the spine is therefore: a non-trivial disjunctive-transcendental argument with genuine structural innovations (premise on the opponent's enacted act; identity-of-occurrence conclusion; disjunctive not conditional form) that forces a conventional, thin-indexed seam — bounded by *saṃvṛti*, adequate for the thesis, not extending to *paramārtha*. That is a real, publishable philosophical result. It is not the grand relocation of the explanatory gap; it is its precondition.

## Q2 — THE THICK INDEX

**Question.** The thick index is, by your own concession, undischarged at every level — including the conventional. How can a forcing argument rest its modal weight on a premise you cannot derive? And if you drop the thick index, what exactly is left of the claim to have "forced the explanatory gap"?

**Best Honest Answer.**
The direct answer to the second question first, because it is more important: if the thick index is dropped, what survives is the thin forcing plus the membrane framing plus the demarcation filter. The claim that *survives* is: wherever conferral occurs, a non-zero occupied seam occurs as the form of that conferral; the only route to zero is cessation; the thin index is forced conventionally; the explanatory gap as Nagel and Levine identified it (between physical description and what-it-is-like) is *not* what is forced — that identification is the type-B-dependent claim (see Q4), and it is declared conditional in the contribution statement.

On the first question: the thick index is not the basis on which the thin forcing is made. The argument's valid core — P2 (dissolution is itself a conferral), P3 (the disjunction C/Z, exhaustive under P1-thin), P4 (zero is cessation not ground) — forces the thin seam. The thick index is the stronger claim that what is forced is a *perspectival* standpoint, not merely a located token-event. That stronger claim is what would be needed to identify the forced seam with the Nagel/Levine explanatory gap. The dissertation does not pretend to deliver this identification unconditionally; it declares the conditionality.

The honest dialectical move is: the thick index is defended by the burden-shift (anyone who remains in argument is conventionally committed to treating her act as *this*-partition-from-here), but "committed to treating-as" is not "proved to occupy." Stroud's gap between must-treat and obtains is uncrossed. The candidate defends the thick premise as a conventional-dialectical commitment of any arguer, concedes it is empty at *paramārtha*, and holds the thin result as the unconditional floor.

**The Trap.**

**Fallback.**
If pressed to the absolute limit: the thick index fails as a derived result. The conventional forcing establishes that a located token-event occurs. The identification of that with a phenomenally perspectival standpoint requires either a phenomenological argument not constructed here or an acceptance of the thick premise. The dissertation declares the latter. A dissertation that honestly marks its undischarged premises with the precision this corpus displays is not weakened by the concession; it is strengthened. The examiner cannot accuse a candidate of overclaiming what the candidate has already printed as undischarged.

## Q3 — BRUTE IDENTITY AND TYPE-B PHYSICALISM

**Question.** Loar's recognitional account — the view that phenomenal concepts are direct recognitional capacities that refer to their physical referents by their intrinsic nature — is precisely designed to explain the explanatory gap without conceding dualism. On that account, the gap is a feature of phenomenal concepts, not of phenomenal properties, and your relocated gap inherits the same explanation. Why should we accept that your forced seam is anything other than a further instance of Loar's account?

**Best Honest Answer.**
The direct answer to Loar is the one the TYPE-B-VERDICT establishes. The Loar objection is that any apparent explanatory gap at the conferral seam can be attributed to a recognitional concept of "sorting-from-a-standpoint" that is cognitively isolated and refers to its physical referent by intrinsic character — exactly as phenomenal concepts refer to phenomenal properties. If that attribution holds, the gap is conceptual and the physical story is complete. This is the brute-identity escape: accept an explanatorily primitive identity between the physical state of conferring and the perspectival character of conferring, and no irreducibility remains except as an artifact of the concept.

The dissertation's response operates at two levels:

First, for the *membrane framing and thin-index results specifically*: the Loar account is a theory of *concepts of conscious states* (Chalmers 2006, primary source, fetched verbatim: "phenomenal concepts — our concepts of conscious states — have a certain special nature"). Whether the recognitional account extends to intentional act-concepts — to "sorting-from-a-standpoint" as a non-qualia concept — is the contested question, and Chalmers' paper explicitly leaves it open. A vocabulary-type gap in standard deontic logic (Pigden's conservativeness theorem, which governs the membrane framing) is untouched by PCS: a zombie tax lawyer faces the is/ought gap identically. PCS has no machinery over deontic vocabulary-conservation. These results are TYPE-B-INDEPENDENT.

Second, for the *relocation claim specifically* — the identification of the forced seam with the hard-problem explanatory gap: the dissertation concedes that this identification is type-B-dependent. Loar's recognitional account is not rebutted in the body with the force it requires. This is the honest position: the grand relocation is *declared and defended dialectically*, not proven. Chalmers' 2006 paper establishes that the phenomenal concept strategy gives type-B no support — it does not establish type-B is false. Rejecting type-B is declarable, not provable. Confidence: low that the rejection is a knock-down.

**The Trap.**
The examiner is testing whether the candidate will either overclaim (pretending the brute-identity escape is answered when it is not, in the body) or underclaim (abandoning the membrane results by attributing them unnecessarily to the type-B debate). The precise answer is: the membrane framing and thin seam are independent of this debate; the relocation is not, and the dissertation says so.

**Fallback.**
Concede that Loar's account, applied to the thick index, is a genuine and undefeated threat to the grand relocation. The committee does not fail the candidate for this concession; it fails the candidate who avoids it. The contribution that survives is the membrane framing (partially anticipated, moderate novelty), the thin-index seam (novel but modest), and the demarcation filter in its post-revision form. The distinctive originality is type-B-dependent and declared as such. A committee that knows the TYPE-B-VERDICT.md was produced by the candidate's own adversarial process will credit the honesty.

In Alter & Walter (eds), *Phenomenal Concepts and Phenomenal Knowledge*, OUP 2006. — **Primary source, fetched verbatim**: scope disclaimer at lines 892–895: "I am not arguing that type-B materialism is false... the phenomenal concepts strategy provides no support for type-B materialism." ## Q6 — WITNESS INDEPENDENCE

**Question.** You claim three independent adjudications — the P1 defense, the Remainder Law test, and the type-B analysis — converge on the membrane as the unconditional core. But if all three used the same substrate, the same load-bearing premise P1, the same framing, and the same author, in what sense are they independent witnesses rather than one mind asking itself the same question three times?

**Best Honest Answer.**
The question names a genuine defect that the mock viva surfaced as defect 6 of 10, and the contribution statement was revised to acknowledge it. The three adjudications are *methodologically independent* — they asked different questions of the substrate at different angles (can P1 be defended? can the Remainder Law certify the seam? what does the contribution owe to rejecting type-B?) — but they are *etiologically correlated*: one author, one corpus, a shared P1 as load-bearing premise across all three, and a Remainder Law that was itself *forward-constructed* for its own test (REMAINDER-LAW-TEST.md line 103: "forward-constructed demarcation criterion built on the corpus's ontology for this experiment, not a verbatim corpus artifact"). A criterion built to test the thesis cannot be an independent witness to the thesis. Deuteronomy 19:15 applies: one witness interrogated three times under three labels is still one witness.

The methodological independence establishes *internal consistency*: the corpus is not self-contradicting, the membrane-as-core verdict is stable under three attack angles. This is genuine and worth stating. It is not the mind-independent confirmation that "independent witnesses" would claim. The thesis's own Movement VII makes the asymmetry binding: "the agreement a single mind reaches with itself confirms nothing." That principle applies to the corpus's own workflow, and the contribution statement applies it.

The genuinely independent evidence is the *cross-domain convergence*: metaethics (Pigden's conservativeness theorem) and security architecture (ABLP's formal non-entailment, XACML PDP/PIP) arriving at the same no-aseity structure through apparatus that shares no model and no vocabulary with the philosophy-of-mind sections. A metaethicist and a security architect "share no model and no vocabulary" (THESIS.md 1078–79); the convergence on one structure is genuine abductive evidence, not etiological correlation. That convergence is what the contribution stands on for its cross-domain claim.

**The Trap.**
The examiner is testing the most sophisticated methodological problem in the corpus. A candidate who defends the workflow convergence as genuinely independent fails immediately. A candidate who concedes it but then abandons the cross-domain evidence also fails. The precise answer is the one printed in the revised 00-CONTRIBUTION.md: methodologically independent, etiologically correlated, establishes internal consistency; the independent evidence is cross-domain.

**Fallback.**
Concede the workflow-convergence problem fully and without hedging. Then hold the cross-domain convergence as the genuine evidence. The unconditional core does not need the workflow agreement; it already has cross-domain support and its own arguments. Playing the weak chip when the strong chips win the hand is what the mock viva called out.

## Q8 — MEMBRANE DIVERGENCE

**Question.** You claim the membrane result follows "from the naming and only from the naming" — that identifying ABLP's bridge premise as deontic, and not merely establishing its absence, is what yields the unmeasurability result and the failure taxonomy. But ABLP already establishes the formal non-entailment. Couldn't any engineer with ABLP in hand have derived both consequences without ever using the word "deontic"? And if so, what has the naming actually added?

**Best Honest Answer.**
The question is the strongest objection to the membrane framing's novelty claim, and the answer requires a distinction between two different kinds of "derivation." ABLP establishes formal non-entailment: the access decision is not derivable from the "says"/"speaks-for" facts. That is a syntactic result about the calculus. What ABLP cannot derive, within its own machinery, is *why* the gap is constitutive and not merely architectural — why no expansion of the descriptive base, however detailed, can close it. ABLP lacks the vocabulary to distinguish a measurement deficit (an epistemic gap, closeable by better authentication) from a type-level gap (the deontic operator is absent from the descriptive base as a matter of inferential form). ABLP establishes *that* the gap exists; it does not classify *why* — and the "why" is what licenses the unmeasurability result as a logical claim rather than an engineering recommendation.

The practical consequence is precise. An engineer with ABLP in hand knows that an ACL/policy premise is needed; she might believe that sufficiently strong authentication — hardware-bound attestation, biometrics, continuous behavioral signals — would eventually substitute for explicit policy. Nothing in ABLP's formalism rules this out; ABLP just says "the ACL premise is needed, so add it." The deontic classification rules it out at the level of inferential structure: no quantity or quality of IS-facts introduces a deontic operator. Once the bridge premise is identified as a *permission* — a member of the deontic vocabulary — the gap is typed as type-level, and the engineer's substitution belief is refuted structurally, not empirically. That is what the naming adds.

The same applies to the failure taxonomy. Hardy (1988) describes confused-deputy, ambient authority, and token-as-fact-vs-grant as engineering failures with independently discovered engineering remedies. The deontic framing shows they share a single formal structure — IS-predicate serving as premise for a permission conclusion — and derives a single class-level invariant from that structure. The capability-security literature achieves a design-level unification; the deontic framing achieves an inference-type unification, which is complementary and different.

**The Trap.**
The examiner is testing whether the candidate will either overclaim (pretending the naming is a novel theorem rather than a framing) or underclaim (conceding that ABLP-plus-careful-reading implies everything). The answer is to hold the precise intermediate: ABLP establishes the gap; the deontic naming classifies its type; the classification licenses two consequences the gap's bare existence does not.

**Fallback.**
Concede that the novelty is a framing contribution at moderate confidence, not a mathematical theorem. The search of the literature (arXiv, Google Scholar, PhilPapers — MEMBRANE-NOVELTY.md) found no prior explicit statement of the deontic classification with these two consequences, but the search was incomplete and the claim is labelled accordingly. The honest-sized contribution is: a framing that licenses consequences the prior literature left undrawn. If a prior statement is found in revision, the contribution shrinks to a worked explication, which still has value.

## Q11 — THE ARITY GAP (FREE WILL)

**Question.** The mock committee named your arity-gap argument — that a one-place target-fixing vocabulary cannot fix the target of a two-place reactive-attitude relation — as the corpus's best independent philosophy. Walk us through the phlogiston objection and why it fails.

**Best Honest Answer.**
The arity-gap argument is the free-will chapter's cleanest result (second-personal remainder, proposition R), and it is the one place the committee's credit is unequivocal and deserved. This is where the candidate should be most confident.

The phlogiston objection — the strongest form of the hard-incompatibilist / Sapolsky-Pereboom reduction — runs as follows: "I am not urging you to abandon a thing identical to deterrence. I urge you to abandon the desert-laden reactive attitude — the *wrapper* — while keeping the behavior-shaping function it wraps. This is a reduction-plus-elimination, exactly as phlogiston-talk was urged-abandoned while oxidation was kept. Combustion is what phlogiston-talk was tracking; behavior-shaping is what reactive-attitude-talk was tracking. Your 'remainder' is just the name you give to your refusal to finish the reduction."

The dissertation concedes, explicitly and without hedging, that the earlier reply ("you can only be urged to abandon what is not identical to what you adopt") is a non sequitur. The phlogiston analogy is apt in its *form*: you can urge abandonment of what is-all-along the thing you adopt minus a gloss. That reply is withdrawn.

What carries R is not the form of the program but the *arity of the target*. The phlogiston elimination works because there is a third-personal, one-place target-fixing description of combustion — oxidation — available in the third person, against which the phlogiston gloss can be measured and found empty. The forward-looking program owes the same for holding-responsible: a third-personal, one-place target-fixing description of *what reactive-attitude-talk was tracking*, against which the second-personal "gloss" can be measured and found to add nothing.

The program does not have one, and the reason is *structural*: oxidation is a one-place property of the combusting stuff. Holding-responsible is a two-place relation whose second place is occupied by an *addressee qua addressee* — a standpoint that has standing to answer and to address in turn. The forward-looking vocabulary (quarantine, deterrence, formation, signaling) is, in its logical form, first-person-to-third-person: an agent acts upon a system modeled as a bundle of modifiable dispositions. You cannot fix the target of a two-place relation with a one-place predicate. The "gloss" the opponent proposes to strip is not a predicate added to an already-one-place property; it is the second argument-place of the relation. Strip it and you are not removing the phlogiston gloss from combustion; you are removing one of the two relata and describing something else. The phlogiston analogy fails at the arity, not at the form.

The solipsism test seals it: a universe with exactly one inhabited standpoint satisfies the necessity-of-locus residue in full and contains no second-personal remainder, because there is no one to address. R is therefore false in a world where the residue is true. A proposition that can be false while the residue holds is not entailed by the residue. The arity gap is structural, not verbal.

**The Trap.**
The examiner will push on the phlogiston analogy precisely because it is the strongest opponent move in the literature, and the earlier draft had a bad answer to it. The well-prepared candidate has the better answer ready: concede the bad reply, then deploy the arity argument. An examiner who hears "the earlier reply was a non sequitur and I withdrew it; here is what actually carries R" is listening to a candidate who thinks rather than defends.

**Fallback.**
The arity argument is the strongest and most independent result in the corpus. It floats entirely free of P1, the thick index, the type-B dispute, and the provenance issue. If the examiner presses the arity-gap claim beyond what the argument delivers, the fallback is: R establishes that the phlogiston reduction schema fails *on the two-place case*, and that the failure is structural. It does not establish that no reduction of the reactive attitudes is possible — only that this family of reductions, which achieves its work by first-to-third conversion, cannot be one. The existence of a possible reduction along different lines is left open. R is bounded at that finding, and the boundary is defensible.
