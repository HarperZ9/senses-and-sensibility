### How to use this ledger, and a disclosure-statement template

**Purpose**

The mock viva (MOCK-VIVA.md, Defect 1) identified the absence of an operationalized provenance firewall as the single existential condition standing between this corpus and a misconduct proceeding. The provenance firewall was declared in CONSOLIDATION.md §4 and in the Contribution preamble. It was not built as an artifact. This ledger is that artifact.

It is a working tool, not a summary. Its value is its honesty. Every row names a claim the corpus makes or has made, identifies where that claim originated, states what a human candidate must do to own it, and leaves an Evidence-of-Act column for the candidate to fill in before submission or viva. A viva examiner should be able to pick any row, ask the candidate to perform the act in that row's third column, and receive a demonstration — not a citation of this ledger.

**Three rules for using this ledger**

1. The ledger governs the candidate's relationship to the substrate, not the substrate's relationship to the world. The philosophical arguments in the corpus may be sound; the ledger does not adjudicate that. It adjudicates whether the candidate can own them independent of the substrate that articulated them.

2. Filling in the Evidence-of-Act column is the human work. AI generation cannot perform that work; performing it is the definition of what authorship means on the thesis's own terms. A candidate who submits a completed Evidence-of-Act column filled by further AI generation has enacted the aseity error the thesis was written to expose.

3. Confidence labels in the Origin column are inherited from the corpus's own adjudicated verdicts. They are not this ledger's assessments. Where the corpus returned a verdict, that verdict's confidence grade travels with the row.

**How to read each column**

| Column | Content |
|--------|---------|
| Claim | The specific philosophical claim, as stated or implied in the corpus. Quoted from the adjudicated text where possible. |
| Adjudicated Status | The verdict returned by the relevant workflow (forcing-argument, P1-DEFENSE, membrane, novelty, type-B, Remainder-Law-Test, mock viva). |
| Origin | PRIOR-LITERATURE [cite] / AI-SUBSTRATE [this corpus] / CANDIDATE-TO-ORIGINATE. A claim can have multiple origins for different parts. |
| What the Candidate Must Do | The specific intellectual act required to own the claim — not "understand it" but derive it, construct it, or explicitly disclose its origin. |
| Evidence of Act | BLANK — for the candidate to complete before submission. |
