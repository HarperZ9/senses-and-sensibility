## A — The Determinism Cluster → Part III (Conferred Sourcehood)

Seven thinkers are convened here under a single charge: each, in his own register, denies the self-authoring will. The chapter does not contest them. It **grants** them — fully, without reservation — and then shows that the grant costs the thesis nothing, because every one of them is making a claim about *dynamics* (how states succeed states), whereas Conferred Sourcehood is a claim about an *inhabited standpoint* (the locus at which significance-for is re-bestowed). This is the **orthogonality result of Movement VI** doing its single, repeatable work. Determinism supplies neither the is→ought transit nor the history→instant transit; it neither locates nor dissolves the standpoint at which sourcehood and meaning are conferred. So the cluster is real, the citations are load-bearing where marked, and the route around them is one move, applied seven times.

### 1. Robert Sapolsky — THE CENTRAL FOIL — *LOAD-BEARING SOURCE*

- **Works / claims.** *Determined: A Science of Life Without Free Will* (2023) and *Behave: The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst* (2017). Sapolsky defends **hard incompatibilism**: behavior is the cumulative product of genes, prenatal environment, hormones, childhood, culture, and the immediately preceding neuronal state, with **no uncaused causer** anywhere in the regress — his signature image is that it is **"turtles all the way down."** [high] His verified central conclusions, per the book: "our choices are determined by our genetics, experience, and environment," the common use of "free will" is "erroneous," and the book turns to "the ethical consequences of justice and punishment" in a model that **dispenses with desert-based blame**, replacing backward-looking blame with forward-looking quarantine/management. [high]
- **The corpus result it attaches to.** Movement VI (orthogonality) for the agency half; the **Meaning Closure** (Part IV, passed) for the ethics-and-meaning half — Sapolsky's blame-elimination is where his determinism reaches *toward* meaning and where the orthogonality cut must be shown to hold, not merely asserted.
- **Chapter served.** Part III, Conferred Sourcehood — Sapolsky is the **foil the whole part is built against**, because he is the strongest contemporary statement of the position the chapter must grant without being absorbed by.
- **Why granting him costs nothing.** Grant every link in the regress. The thesis never asserted libertarian self-origination — Part III explicitly defines conferred sourcehood as *neither* libertarian self-origination *nor* determinism's dissolution. Sapolsky's regress refutes only the first disjunct. The inhabited standpoint at which significance-for is re-bestowed is not a *gap in the causal chain* that he could close; it is the locus the causal story is *about*, and a complete account of how a state was produced does not tell you what it is *like to undergo* the bestowing — the **A/B remainder no functional redescription closes**. His ethics-without-blame is the sharpest test: even there, eliminating *desert* leaves untouched the withdrawable-without-subtraction / re-inhabitable-without-addition structure of meaning. He has removed the metaphysical ground of *retribution*; he has not located, and so cannot subtract, the **occasionalism of meaning**.
- **Honest caveat.** Critics (Riskin, NYRB; Fischer, NDPR; Piovarchy) charge *scientism* — that "is there free will" is philosophical, not settled by neuroscience. [high] The chapter should **not** lean on these to dismiss him; it should grant his science entirely and win on the orthogonality cut, which is the stronger and more honest position.

### 2. Albert Einstein — *LOAD-BEARING SOURCE* (for the Schopenhauer transmission)

- **Work / claim.** "My Credo" / *Mein Glaubensbekenntnis* (1932), and "What I Believe" (1930). Verified verbatim from "My Credo": **"I do not believe in freedom of the will. Schopenhauer's words: 'Man can do what he wants, but he cannot will what he wills' accompany me in all situations throughout my life and reconcile me with the actions of others…"** [high] In a separate statement: **"I am a determinist… I believe with Schopenhauer: We can do what we wish, but we can only wish what we must… Practically, I am nevertheless compelled to act as if freedom of the will existed."** [high]
- **Attribution, verified carefully (per your instruction).** The proposition is **Schopenhauer's**, not Einstein's — original in *On the Freedom of the Will* (1839): *"Der Mensch kann tun was er will; er kann aber nicht wollen was er will."* Einstein **quotes and endorses** it; he is the *transmitter and twentieth-century witness*, not the author. Citing the line as "Einstein's" would be the fabrication-by-misattribution the casebook forbids. Cite it as *Schopenhauer (1839), as adopted by Einstein (1930/1932)*. [high]
- **The corpus result it attaches to.** Movement VI orthogonality — and note that Einstein himself performs the orthogonality move in miniature: he denies will-of-the-will *and in the same breath* says he is "compelled to act as if freedom existed" and "must act as if man is a responsible being." That "as if," lived rather than resolved, is precisely the inhabited standpoint surviving the determinist concession.
- **Chapter served.** Part III, as the canonical statement of the **second-order** denial (you can will, but cannot will your willing) — the most precise formulation of what conferred sourcehood must do without contradicting.
- **Why granting it costs nothing.** Schopenhauer/Einstein deny *self-determination of the want*. Conferred sourcehood concedes this and relocates agency from origination to **inhabited re-speaking**: the want is not self-caused, yet the standpoint that *undergoes* and *re-bestows* it is not thereby vacated. Einstein's own forced "as if" is the datum the chapter formalizes.
