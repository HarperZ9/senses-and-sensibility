# The Seam That Holds While It Is Crossed: A Disjunctive-Transcendental Argument for a Conventional Conferring Seam

**Abstract**

A long tradition relocates the explanatory gap — the irreducibility of normative or phenomenal facts to purely descriptive ones — from a property of a state to a property of inference, treating it as a limit-condition on conceptual transitions. This paper proposes a different relocation: the gap is a property of the *conferral-seam*, the event-form that every act of sorting, designating, or meaning-something instantiates. The argument for this relocation is a disjunctive-transcendental argument with three structural features that distinguish it from the class of arguments that Barry Stroud's 1968 objection defeats. First, its load-bearing premise is relocated onto the sceptic's own *enacted act* of dissolution rather than onto a fact the argument must independently establish. Second, the conclusion is an *identity of occurrence* — the seam *is* the conferral-event under a second description — rather than a grounding relation that posits a second entity behind the first. Third, the argument's form is disjunctive (conferral is occurring, or it has wholly ceased) rather than the conditional form Stroud's critique targets. Together these features close Stroud's gap not by crossing it but by removing the step it was supposed to prevent. The argument survives its two hardest adversaries — Nāgārjuna's *śūnyatā-śūnyatā* and Stroud's transcendental deflation — on the following honest terms: the *thin* index (spatiotemporal location, established by the token-reflexive route non-circularly) is forced at the conventional (*saṃvṛti*) level; the *thick* index (the perspectival *from-here-as-this* structure the argument's strongest form would require) is declared and defended dialectically but never derived; and the *ultimate* zero (*paramārtha*) is conceded to Madhyamaka without qualification. The verdict is **FORCES-CONVENTIONALLY-ONLY**, printed as a header rather than buried as a qualification, because the conventional result is a genuine bounded contribution and its honest size is its actual size. The paper also names the Candrakirti-absorption risk, the undischarged thick index as the live viva front, and concedes — without manufactured symmetry — that the ultimate silence wins cleanly and costs nothing by way of conventional argument.

**Keywords:** transcendental argument, Stroud, Nāgārjuna, Madhyamaka, conferral, is/ought, conventional truth, token-reflexive, performative self-reference, explanatory gap

## 1. The Setting: A Framework That Cannot Afford a Floor

There is a class of philosophical frameworks that begin by denying aseity — self-originating, self-standing existence — across the board. Existence is conferred, not secreted. Status, authority, normative standing, meaning: all are put into rather than emanated from. The appeal of such frameworks is real. They dissolve a recurring illusion — the thought that some item in the inventory of the world, pressed hard enough, will turn out to be the ground that needs no grounding — and they cohere with what the sciences have found: layers of dependence all the way down, no stratum that stands without the stratum below it.

The structural pressure such frameworks face is reflexive. If *nothing* is self-standing, the framework's own claim cannot be self-standing. More acutely: if *existence* is conferred, then the conferral itself — the *act* of sorting this from not-this, of designating A rather than its negation, of applying a convention — must either be self-standing (refuting the framework) or be itself conferred (raising the question of what confers the conferring), or dissolve entirely to zero, leaving no positive result. The framework either posits a covert aseity, starts an infinite regress, or ends with nothing to say.

This is not a marginal objection. It is the objection Nāgārjuna anticipated and addressed in the *Vigrahavyāvartanī* (the "Dispeller of Disputes"), by denying that the emptiness-claim is itself a self-standing assertion — the medicine that purges itself. But notice the dialectical situation this creates: the no-aseity framework, taking the Madhyamaka move, concedes that *at the ultimate level* the dissolution reaches zero. Nothing persists. And then the question becomes whether the framework has anything to say *short* of the ultimate — whether any result holds at the conventional level, while conferral is occurring, before the silence that dissolves it.

The answer defended here is that something is forced, at the conventional level, as an event-form of every occurring conferral: a non-zero occupied seam. This seam has *seity* (ownerless dependently-arisen structure) and no *aseity* (no self-standing). The argument for its existence is disjunctive-transcendental in form, and it has a genuine structural innovation over the class of arguments Stroud defeated in 1968.

## 2. The Argument: P1 Through C

### 2.1 P1 — Conferral Is Occupation

*Status: stipulated-and-contested — the single load-bearing premise; forced in its thin reading by non-circular argument; declared in its thick reading.*

Any act of conferral — sorting A from not-A, meaning, designating, applying a convention — is performed as *this* discrimination and not its negation, indexed at a standpoint. An occurrence indexed at nothing is no occurrence.

The phrase "indexed at a standpoint" carries two readings that must be kept distinct throughout:

**The thin index** is spatiotemporal location: the conferral-event is a concrete particular at a determinate position. This reading has a non-circular defense given in §4. It earns more than *pratītyasamutpāda* restated. A conferral with zero thin-index residue is conventionally incoherent: a type is occurring while no token of that type is occurring, which is a formal contradiction at the *saṃvṛti* level.

**The thick index** is a standpoint *from which* sorting is performed *as this discrimination and not its negation* — the perspectival from-here structure, the intentional directedness that marks the act as *this* act and not its complement. This reading is what P1's strongest modal content would require to force the disjunction in full. It is declared, not derived. The step from "located at L" to "sorts from L as this sorting" reads perspectival structure off a physical description, and that step is Barry Stroud's gap reopened inside the predicate "conferral" itself [high confidence on this diagnosis; Stroud 1968, "Transcendental Arguments," *Journal of Philosophy* 65: 241–256].

The argument below proceeds honestly: the thin index is the load-bearing item, non-circularly established; the thick index is dialectically pressured but conceded as undischarged; the contribution is bounded accordingly.

### 2.2 P2 — The Dissolution-to-Zero Is Itself a Conferral

*Status: granted; survives every adversary.*

To assert, show, or even *intend* "the residue is zero" is to sort *empty* from *non-empty*, *ultimate* from *conventional*, *dissolved* from *undissolved*. The *Vigrahavyāvartanī*'s "I have no thesis" reply defuses the charge of asserting a self-standing *proposition*; it does not defuse the charge of *performing a partition*. Even the self-purging medicine is administered, and administering is sorting.

This is the pivot on which the argument's structural innovation turns. The Madhyamaka sceptic, running a *prasaṅga* reductio against the view that a conferring seam persists, must *select* certain consequences as unwelcome — as reductios the interlocutor ought to reject. To display a consequence as unwelcome, she must evaluate: this-consequence-is-damaging and not that one, by the interlocutor's lights. That evaluative sorting is performed. It is an act of conferral — at minimum a thin-indexed one — even if it is not an act of assertion about the mind-independent world.

P2's status is high: both hardest adversaries (the Madhyamika and the Stroudian deflationist) concede that an act of dissolution is performed. The dispute is over what the act *carries* — whether it carries an index — not whether it occurs. That concession is all P2 requires, and P2 requires only that [high].

### 2.3 P3 — Exhaustive Disjunction

*Status: valid conditional on P1.*

For any region: either (C) conferral is occurring, or (Z) it has wholly ceased. There is no third cell. "Conferral with a zero residue" — a sorting performed from no index while sorting — is *p* ∧ ¬*p* under P1: it is conferral occurring, which by P1 means an occurrence is tokened, which means a thin index is occupied; and it is zero residue, which means nothing is occupied. The conjunction is a formal contradiction, not merely implausible, at the conventional level.

The Madhyamaka probes a cell C′: dependently-arisen sorting with no occupied index. C′ is contradictory *only if* P1's thin-index reading holds. The entire conditional weight lives here. The argument inherits P1's contestation exactly: P3 is as secure as P1 is.

### 2.4 P4 — Zero Is Cessation, Not Ground

*Status: granted; load-bearing in the decisive direction; no adversary dislodges it.*

The cell (Z) — total cessation of conferral — is reached only by conferral *stopping*, never by going "underneath" it. There is no vantage both at-zero and conferring. The ultimate emptiness (*paramārtha*) is not a deeper stratum on which the conventional world rests. It is what remains when the conventional world — including all conferral — ceases to operate.

This premise does enormous work, and it does it without being contested. Both the Madhyamika and the Stroudian deflationist concede it. The Madhyamika wins at *paramārtha* not by finding a deeper ground but precisely by *dissolving* everything, including the conferring activity. The deflationist who denies that physical events carry intentional structure does not claim there is a ground under the conferring; he simply denies the conferring is real as conferring. Neither objector claims that zero is a stratum beneath the conventional; they claim the conventional does not force what the argument claims it forces.

P4's function is to convert the concession of the ultimate into a *license* for the conventional result: precisely because zero is cessation and not a ground, the conventional cannot borrow zero-ness. Wherever conferral is occurring, it is occurring from a non-zero location, and the fact that the ultimate level offers no such location is not a refutation — it is the cessation of the subject matter itself.

### 2.5 C — Conclusion

*Forced from P1–P4; i.e., forced conditional on P1.*

Wherever conferral occurs, a non-zero occupied seam occurs — as the *form* of that conferral, identical to the conferral-event under a second description, not a ground beneath it — and the only route to zero is the cessation of conferral, which is the absence of the conferred world, not its foundation. The dissolution of aseity therefore cannot reach zero while any conferring is going on, including the conferring that performs the dissolution.

The seam so forced has *seity* — ownerless dependently-arisen structure — and no *aseity*. It is not self-standing. It exists *while it is enacted* and only then. Between conferrals, in cessation, there is zero. No floor persists under the world.

## 3. The Structural Innovation: Why This Is Not Stroud-Vulnerable in the Standard Way

### 3.1 Stroud's Objection, Stated Fairly

Stroud's 1968 paper ["Transcendental Arguments," *Journal of Philosophy* 65: 241–256; high confidence] identified a structural gap in Kant's transcendental method as deployed by neo-Kantians. The argument form he targeted runs: "X is a necessary condition of experience/thought/discourse; therefore X exists." Stroud's objection is that such an argument establishes at best that we must *think* there is an X, not that X *obtains*. There is a gap between conceptual necessity and objective existence. At best transcendental arguments deliver a "must think" conclusion; they deliver "exists" only by importing verificationism or some equivalent anti-sceptical assumption. Without that assumption the argument is invalid. With it the argument is question-begging against the sceptic.

This is a genuine and powerful objection. It has been refined, contested, and partially answered in the forty years since [Robert Stern, "Transcendental Arguments," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, rev. 2023, high confidence], but its core point — that conceptual necessity does not by itself entail existence — remains a genuine constraint.

### 3.2 Feature One: The Premise Is Relocated onto the Opponent's Act

Stroud's critique targets arguments of the form "X is necessary for Y; therefore X exists." The vulnerability is in the step from *necessary-for* to *exists*: the existence claim needs more than the conceptual-necessity claim.

The present argument removes this step by relocating the load-bearing premise onto the *enacted act* of the opponent who is performing the dissolution. The relevant existence — the act of partitioning — is not inferred from a concept. It is *already on the table*, granted by the opponent's own performance of the argument. The Madhyamika who asserts "the residue is zero" grants that she is asserting and sorting; the Stroudian who dissolves the conferring-event to a physical substrate grants that he is performing an evaluative operation. P2 is not an inference about what must exist; it is a description of what the opponent is doing *right now*, which she has already conceded.

Stroud's wedge has nothing to enter at this point, because no thought-to-being inference is made. The being is conceded at the start.

### 3.3 Feature Two: The Conclusion Is an Identity of Occurrence

Standard transcendental arguments of the Kantian form conclude that *X exists* or *X is the case* — a claim about a second entity (the condition) over and above the datum (the experience or thought in question). Stroud's wedge enters in the space between the datum and the further entity posited as its condition.

The present argument's conclusion (C) is not of this form. The seam is not posited as a further entity that the conferral-event requires; it *is* that event under a second description. "A conferring-event is occurring" and "a non-zero occupied seam is occurring" are descriptions of the same occurrence, related by identity, not by grounding or necessitation. There is no second thing to be proved to exist. The identity is not a deep metaphysical claim about essence; it is the same move a physicist makes when she says that a lightning-event is an electrical-discharge-event — a second description of the same occurrence, not an additional ontological commitment.

Stroud's wedge has nothing to enter because no second entity is claimed. The conclusion is an identity, and identities do not posit new existents.

### 3.4 Feature Three: The Disjunctive Form

Stroud's critique targets arguments of the conditional form: "X is necessary for Y" or "without X, Y is impossible." The disjunctive form of P3 does not have this shape. The argument does not say "a seam is necessary for conferral to occur." It says: conferral is occurring (cell C), or it has wholly ceased (cell Z). Cell C *is* a seam-occurring, by identity (feature two). Cell Z is cessation. There is no third cell.

The conditional form invites the question: why must the necessary condition exist, given that the conditioned thing exists? The disjunctive form does not: the disjunction is *exhaustive* by construction (cell C′ — conferral with zero residue — is excluded as formally contradictory under P1), and the question of whether C's seam "must exist" does not arise in the same way. Anyone who is conferring is *already in cell C*. The question is not whether the seam must exist given that conferring is occurring; the question is whether the act of conferring, which is already conceded, is a seam-event or a non-seam-event. The identity claim (feature two) says it is the same occurrence described twice. The disjunction says there is no middle ground.

### 3.5 What the Three Features Buy and Do Not Buy

These three features distinguish the present argument from the generic Stroud-vulnerable class. They are not disputed by either hardest adversary: both the Madhyamika and the Stroudian deflationist concede, under examination, that the *structural* innovation is real. What they contest is P1 — specifically whether P1 holds in its thick reading — and P1's contestation is exactly what the argument acknowledges as the live condition.

The three features buy the following: the argument is not defeated by Stroud's 1968 objection in its standard form, because the gap Stroud identified — thought-to-being — does not appear in the argument's derivation chain. They do not buy: unconditional force for C. C is forced conditional on P1, and P1's thick reading is undischarged. The argument's claim to have escaped Stroud is not a claim to have crossed Stroud's gap; it is a claim to have removed the step that gap was supposed to prevent.

## 4. The Token-Reflexive Defense: Earning the Thin Index Non-Circularly

### 4.1 What Non-Circularity Requires Here

The circularity charge against transcendental arguments runs: the argument assumes the conclusion in the premises. For P1's thin reading, the charge would be: the argument assumes that conferral-events occupy spatiotemporal positions in order to conclude that conferral-events occupy spatiotemporal positions. The defense must derive the thin index from premises the opponent already grants on *independent* grounds — grounds that make no mention of conferral, standpoints, or discrimination.

The derivation proceeds as follows.

### 4.2 Steps One Through Four

**Step one.** C.S. Peirce's type/token distinction (introduced 1906, formulated in the Collected Papers; reported at high confidence via Liebesman, "Types and Tokens," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, first published 2026) establishes the baseline: a type "does not exist; it only determines things that do exist." A token is "a single event which happens once and whose identity is limited to that one happening... significant only as occurring just when and where it does." A type without any token is an uninstantiated abstract pattern; it does not occur. This is not a special claim about conferral; it is the general metaphysics of occurrence.

**Step two.** What individuates two exactly similar token events as numerically two rather than one? The standard accounts surveyed in the SEP entry on Tropes (Maurin, "Tropes," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, rev. 2023; high confidence) divide between *spatiotemporal individuation* (SI: for any tropes exactly resembling each other, they are numerically distinct iff at non-zero spatiotemporal distance; Campbell 1990; Lowe 1998; Schaffer 2001) and *primitivist individuation* (PI: numerical distinctness is primitive; Ehring 2011; Campbell 1990). PI does not deny that tokens occupy positions; it denies only that their positions *explain* their distinctness. Under any account, a token that occupied no spatiotemporal position at all would be indistinguishable from an uninstantiated type. And on Kim's property-exemplification view of events (Kim 1966, 1969; cited throughout Varzi, "Events," *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, rev. 2025; high confidence on attribution), the time-constituent is a *constitutive component* of the event's structure, not a label attached afterward.

**Step three.** Applied to conferral: if a conferral occurs — if the sorting act *takes place* — it takes place as a token event. Peirce's formulation: this conferral is significant as *this* conferral because it occurs just when and where it does. A conferral with zero positional residue would have no identity conditions distinguishing it as a token from any qualitatively identical conferral, or from the uninstantiated type "conferral in general." Uninstantiated types do not occur. "Conferral occurring, at no position" is therefore equivalent to "a type occurring while no token of that type is occurring" — a formal contradiction at the conventional level.

**Step four.** The performative regress. The Prāsaṅgika who asserts "conferral occurs ownerlessly, with zero index residue" is herself performing a conferral act — discriminating the Madhyamaka position from svabhāva-positing alternatives, at a moment, in a conversation, as *this* speaker. She grants she is discriminating; the antecedent is conceded by her own act. By steps one through three, that assertion is a token event. Its being *this* assertion — numerically this one rather than a qualitatively identical assertion made yesterday — requires, at minimum, that it occupy a position. The denier cannot perform the denial without instantiating a token, and instantiating a token requires being located. If she then denies *that* denial is a located token, she performs a further token act of denial. The regress closes the loop: every act of denying the thin index is itself a thin-indexed act, at the conventional level.

### 4.3 Why This Is Non-Circular — for the Thin Index Only

The derivation chain: *occurs (as a conferral-token, granted by anyone arguing)* → *is individuated as this token* → *occupies a spatiotemporal position* → *occupies a thin index.* Each link is drawn from the general metaphysics of particulars applied to all occurrent events without prejudice to conferral specifically. Neither the Peircean type/token distinction nor spatiotemporal individuation mentions conferral, standpoints, or discrimination. The thin index falls out of what it is to be an occurring token, not out of a prior assumption about what conferral is. The non-circularity badge attaches to the thin index only. It does not transfer to any thick-index material.

The important scope qualifier: the performative regress is airtight against anyone who is *arguing* — every interlocutor conducting the C/Z dialectic concedes they confer. What the forcing does not deliver is a general proof that physical events *qua* physical carry a conferral-index; that would require identifying a physical event as a conferral-token rather than as substrate dynamics at the same coordinate — a step the deflationist refuses. The seam binds discourse-participants: which is exactly and only what the C/Z disjunction needs.

## 5. The Thick Index: Dialectical Pressure, Not Forcing

### 5.1 What the Thick Index Would Require and Why It Is Not Derived

The conventional thin-index forcing establishes that conferral-as-token occupies a spatiotemporal position. What the C/Z disjunction's *strongest* form would require is the thick index: the standpoint *from which* sorting is performed *as this discrimination and not its negation* — the perspectival from-here structure, the intentional directedness that marks the act as enacted-as-this-partition.

The move from "located at L" to "sorts from L as this sorting" reads intentional or perspectival structure off a physical description. This is Stroud's gap reopened *inside the predicate "conferral"* itself. It is not a gap between conferral and some further entity; it is a gap within what it would mean for the conferral to be fully indexically determinate. The thick index is not derived here — at any level, including the conventional. Any argument that claims to derive it either imports entelecheia, Husserlian act-structure, or perspectival determinacy as a "real relational property" — each of which *śūnyatā-śūnyatā* dissolves at the ultimate level — or it re-opens Stroud's gap in a new location. Neither move is made here.

The candidate who defends this argument at a viva must hold this boundary firmly. The three structural innovations of §3 escape Stroud's critique for the conclusion-as-stated; they do not license a stronger conclusion than P1 warrants, and P1 in its thick reading is not warranted.

### 5.2 The Burden-Shift and Its Honest Ceiling

The strongest dialectical move available is not to claim the thick index is proved but to raise the cost of dissolving it to zero *while arguing*. The dissolutionist who presses the dissolution claim in discourse is performing an act of evaluation: selecting this-consequence-as-the-correct-characterization rather than its alternative. That evaluative discrimination is itself a sorting act. It is not a first-order assertion about the world, but it is a partition of the space of philosophical options. The Mādhyamika who runs a *prasaṅga* selects consequences as *unwelcome* — as reductios the interlocutor ought to reject. To display a consequence as unwelcome, she must evaluate: this-consequence-is-damaging and not that one, by the interlocutor's lights. That evaluative tracking is performed from a conventional interpretive standpoint *while she argues.*

This establishes that the argumentation-context *presupposes* the from-here structure, in the sense that any participant conducting the argument must *treat* their own acts as directed, as this-partition-and-not-that. But — and this is the boundary that must not be moved — treating-as is not occupying. The presupposition is present as conventional performance for as long as and only as long as the argument is conducted; it lapses the instant discourse lapses. The opponent can eliminate the thick index without pragmatic self-contradiction in two ways: by conceding thin location and denying perspectival structure (the deflationist's clean horn), or by exiting to non-conceptual silence (the Prāsaṅgika's silence-exit). The self-contradiction bites only on denial of the thin index. "Forced" is reserved for the thin result and is not used for the thick index.

### 5.3 The Candrakirti-Absorption Risk Named

There is a specific and non-trivial absorption risk the present argument must name rather than paper over. Candrakīrti's Prāsaṅgika Madhyamaka, in the *Prasannapadā* (his commentary on the *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*; moderate confidence on attribution given no independent primary-text verification here), pressed the *vikalpa* point more sharply than Nāgārjuna's own *Vigrahavyāvartanī* text: conceptual construction (*vikalpa*) is itself the mechanism of error, and the conventional-truth framework is a provisional ladder, not a stable platform. Applied here: the conventional forcing of the thin index is itself a conceptual construction, and on Candrakīrti's account the conventional-truth framework has no stable floor; it is a web of mutually-implicating designations, none of which is fixed by the kind of token-individuation argument Steps one through four provide. The token-individuation argument imports a *substantial* notion of token-particularity that a thoroughgoing Prāsaṅgika may deny.

This is a live objection, not a merely formal one. The response available is to hold at *saṃvṛti*: the argument does not require token-particularity in any ultimate sense; it requires only that the Prāsaṅgika argumentation context conventionally individuates its own moves as discrete performances. That is a very thin, conventionally available claim. But the Candrakīrti objection presses whether even this is covertly smuggling a svabhāva-committed notion of particularity into the conventional framework — and the present paper does not have a worked response to the *Prasannapadā* on this point. The absorption risk is named and left as the live viva front alongside the thick index.

### 5.4 The Ultimate Concession: Zero without Manufactured Symmetry

The Prāsaṅgika wins at the ultimate level. *Śūnyatā-śūnyatā* — emptiness is itself empty — dissolves occurrence, token, position, and perspectival character at the *paramārtha* level. Even "occurrence = tokening" and "tokening = spatiotemporal individuation" are *saṃvṛti* truths with no ultimate-level residue. The thin index is empty at *paramārtha*. The thick index is not merely unearned there; it is dissolved, along with everything the conventional forcing uses to establish the thin index.

There is a temptation to manufacture a symmetry at the ultimate level — to claim that the occupation move "cannot be refuted there any more than it is proved there." This temptation must be declined. The honest asymmetry: the Prāsaṅgika does not assert the dissolution as a *paramārtha* thesis from within discourse, and she does not need to. The zero at *paramārtha* is not a competing claim that could win or lose an argument; it is what remains when conceptual designation ceases. She wins the ultimate not by a successful counter-thesis but by the cessation of proliferation. At *paramārtha* the thick index is not merely unproved with protected standing; it was never earned there, and at *paramārtha* everything — thin index included — is empty.

The cost to the dissertation: real. The cost to the *contribution*: bounded and non-fatal. A conventional, discourse-scoped result — that any act of conferral occurring in discourse instantiates a non-zero thin-indexed seam, by identity, not by grounding — is a genuine bounded result. The Mādhyamika gets *paramārtha*; the argument gets *saṃvṛti*. And the question of whether *saṃvṛti* is enough for the contribution is answered: it is, if the contribution is stated honestly at *saṃvṛti*, which it now is.

## 6. The Cogito-at-the-Act and Performative Self-Reference

### 6.1 The Lineage

P1 and the argument's P2 move have a recognizable lineage, and the paper is clearer for naming it rather than discovering it covertly. The performative self-reference structure — the observation that the act of denying X presupposes X or enacts X — has two canonical philosophical ancestors.

**Descartes's cogito, read as act rather than proposition.** The traditional reading of "cogito ergo sum" treats it as an inference. A more defensible reading (Hintikka, "Cogito ergo sum: Inference or Performance?" 1962, *Philosophical Review* 71: 3–32; high confidence on existence and thesis) treats it as a performative: the act of doubting instantiates a doubter; not because "there must be a doubter for there to be doubting" is a valid inference to existence, but because the *act of doubting*, as an enactment, is itself the existence it was supposed to deny. The present argument's P2 has the same structure, applied to the act of dissolving-to-zero: the act of performing a dissolution is itself an instance of the conferral it attempts to dissolve.

**Performative self-reference in Apel and Habermas.** Karl-Otto Apel's transcendental pragmatics (*Transformation of Philosophy*, 1973; *Towards a Transformation of Philosophy*, English 1980; moderate confidence on primary-text specifics) and Jürgen Habermas's theory of communicative action (*The Theory of Communicative Action*, vol. 1, 1984; high confidence on thesis) each developed the observation that certain philosophical positions are self-refuting at the level of the act of arguing, not the level of the proposition argued. Apel's "Letztbegründung" (ultimate grounding) argument claims that the pragmatic presuppositions of discourse — that the participants recognize one another as capable of giving reasons, that truth is a regulative ideal — are enacted by anyone who argues, including the sceptic who argues against them [high confidence on the claim, moderate on specific Apel citations without primary-text verification]. This is structurally close to the present argument's P2 move.

The difference is crucial: the present argument does not claim to derive *oughts*, Habermasian validity claims, or anything about the semantic content of discourse from the performative structure. The only content derived is the *thin index* — spatiotemporal location — and that derivation goes through the general metaphysics of token individuation rather than through any pragmatic presupposition about the norms of discourse. The Apelian lineage is acknowledged; the Apelian ambition is explicitly disclaimed.

### 6.2 Why the Cogito Comparison Does Not Overreach

The Descartes comparison is informative but potentially misleading if it imports the first-person singular. The cogito, traditionally read, secures the existence of a thinking subject. The present argument secures the existence of a thin-indexed occurrence; it does not secure a subject, a perspective, or anything that could be called a *who*. The thin index is spatiotemporal location, available in principle to any event-metaphysics, not a first-personal standpoint. The seam is *ownerless* — structural seity, not aseity — and the argument says nothing about whether there is a subject behind it. This is the point at which the framework's no-aseity commitment bears directly: the seam is forced without positing an ego, a soul, a transcendental subject, or anything the Buddhist opponent has good reason to reject. The cogito is cited for its performative form, not its first-personal content.

## 7. The Arity Gap: A Companion Result That Floats Free

The mock viva committee returned one verdict in the free-will chapter as the corpus's best independent philosophy: the *arity-gap* argument against eliminativist reductions of the reactive attitudes (as documented in `MOCK-VIVA.md`, §1 credit panel; `dissertation/part-III-free-will.md`, §1.3–1.5). It is reported here as a companion, not as part of the forcing argument, because it is structurally independent and instructive about the form the forcing argument must take.

The arity-gap argument: the eliminativist reduction of blame, resentment, and the reactive attitudes in general to a forward-looking behavior-shaping program invokes the phlogiston analogy. Phlogiston-talk is urged-abandoned because combustion (oxidation) provides a target-fixing description of what phlogiston was tracking, available in the third person. The eliminativist owes the same for the reactive attitudes: a forward-looking, third-personal target-fixing description of what holding-responsible was tracking. He does not have one, and the reason is structural: oxidation is a one-place property of the combusting stuff; holding-responsible is a two-place relation whose second place is occupied by an addressee *qua* addressee. A one-place target-fixing vocabulary cannot fix the target of a two-place relation. Strip the second relatum and you are not removing phlogiston-gloss from combustion; you are eliminating one of the two relata and describing a different relation.

This argument is included here because it instantiates the same logical form as the forcing argument's structural innovation: the relevant structure cannot be reduced to something at a lower arity because the lower-arity description leaves out one of the relata that constitutes the phenomenon. The forcing argument's claim that the seam is *identical to* the conferral-event under a second description (not a grounding relation behind it) is, structurally, the refusal to reduce to a one-place description (the event without the standpoint-description). The arity observation binds both cases: one-place reductions cannot force the target of a two-place relation.

The arity-gap argument for the reactive attitudes is type-B-independent — it does not require anything about the hard problem of consciousness — and it passes the floats-free test: a solipsistic universe with one inhabited standpoint satisfies the necessity-of-locus residue in full and contains no second-personal remainder, because there is no one to address. The two-place structure (*from* a standpoint, *addressed to* a standpoint) is distinct from the one-place monadic residue ("a standpoint is occupied"). The arity gap is a genuine structural difference, not a verbal one.

The companion result is offered because it is the forcing argument's own form generalized: the escape from both Stroud and the Madhyamaka is not to add a new entity but to refuse the reduction to a lower arity. The seam is not behind the conferral; it *is* the conferral at a second description. The second person is not behind the blaming; it *is* the blaming as a two-place enactment. The structure appears in both places because it is the correct structure for resisting certain reductive moves.

## 10. Contextual Positioning and Honest Novelty Assessment

The paper's position in the literature is best characterized as follows:

**Against Apel:** the present argument does not attempt to establish communicative-rationality norms, Habermasian validity claims, or anything about the content of discourse from the performative structure.
